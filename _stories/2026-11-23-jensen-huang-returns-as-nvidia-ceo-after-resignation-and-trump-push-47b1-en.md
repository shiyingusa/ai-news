Nvidia said on November 23 that its board voted unanimously to reinstate Jensen Huang as president, CEO and board director, effective immediately. The company said the board took input from senior levels of the U.S. government and is confident his return poses no national security risk.

Jensen Huang resigned as Nvidia's CEO on November 12 after admitting he had been "unfaithful" to his wife and had "created problems for the board" amid scrutiny of alleged affairs, then returned to the role after President Trump pushed Nvidia to reinstate him, according to Axios. Axios, citing unnamed sources, reported that Trump's allies argued Huang was "too big to fail" for the US economy and a victim of "deep state" maneuvering.

The Verge's account ties the resignation to earlier New York Times reporting on Huang's "close relationship" with a woman passing intelligence to China; those espionage-related claims have not been independently confirmed.

<div class="fact-check" markdown="1">

## Verified and unconfirmed

- **Verified:** The Verge reported that Nvidia's board accepted Jensen Huang's resignation as CEO on November 12, and Reuters and the New York Post reported that the board reinstated him on November 23, 11 days later; Huang himself acknowledged he had been "unfaithful" to his wife and had "created problems for the board." The New York Post reported that President Trump praised the board's decision as "correct and proper."
- **Not yet confirmed:** Axios's account that Trump personally pushed Nvidia to reinstate Huang, which rests on unnamed sources, along with the underlying allegations that Huang had an affair with a woman reportedly passing intelligence to China and that a Chinese intelligence agency was involved in the matter — accusations that come from NYT reporting cited by The Verge, not from any official finding. The Verge's article is truncated and no source details the board's deliberations or the terms of Huang's return.

</div>

## Background

Jensen Huang cofounded Nvidia in 1993 and had led it ever since, becoming the public face of the company whose GPUs underpin most large-scale AI training and whose market value made him one of the most closely watched CEOs in tech. Scrutiny of his tenure was not only personal: AI Daily Radar's November 15 digest reported a Wall Street Journal investigation finding that Nvidia chips were reaching Chinese companies through large Southeast Asian data centers that exploited gaps in U.S. export controls, a business the Journal tied to Huang's time as CEO. The personal allegations that followed — including a New York Times report of a close relationship with a woman said to be passing intelligence to China — turned that pressure into a board crisis.

## Why it matters

For Nvidia's investors and customers, the episode raises a board-independence question: the board accepted Huang's resignation and then reinstated him after reported pressure from President Trump, according to Axios.

## What to watch

Nvidia's next quarterly earnings call is the first scheduled checkpoint at which Huang would face investors as reinstated CEO — watch whether he or the board addresses the China intelligence allegations and the terms of his return, since Nvidia's reinstatement announcement gave no reason for the reversal. Also watch for any board statement or regulatory filing covering the circumstances of his November 12 resignation and the board's decision to reinstate him effective immediately.
