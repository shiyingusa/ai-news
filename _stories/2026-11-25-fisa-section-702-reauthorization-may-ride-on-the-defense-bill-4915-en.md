Sen. Mike Rounds (R-SD), a member of the Senate Select Committee on Intelligence, told Politico that Section 702 of the Foreign Intelligence Surveillance Act could be attached to the National Defense Authorization Act (NDAA), the military spending bill Congress treats as must-pass, as the legislative session runs down. "We don't have two weeks to work just on FISA," Rounds said, according to The Verge, adding that amending it onto the NDAA would open a path.

Section 702 is the authority that lets US intelligence agencies collect the communications of foreigners abroad without individual warrants, which also sweeps in some Americans' messages; The Verge reports it has not technically lapsed even though an earlier reauthorization vote failed. No bill text has been filed, and Rounds described only a possible procedural route, not a settled plan.

## Background

Section 702 of the Foreign Intelligence Surveillance Act authorizes U.S. intelligence agencies to target non-U.S. persons reasonably believed to be abroad, using programmatic selectors, to acquire foreign intelligence information; the Brennan Center notes that the resulting data has also been used to gain warrantless access to Americans' communications. The current reauthorization fight has already drawn opposition to the NDAA route: on Nov. 12, 2026, civil-society and surveillance-reform groups urged lawmakers to keep Section 702 reauthorization out of the NDAA or any other must-pass legislation in the waning days of the 119th Congress.

## Why it matters

Attaching 702 to the NDAA would force lawmakers who oppose warrantless collection to either accept the reauthorization as part of the defense bill or vote the bill down, rather than fighting it as standalone legislation with room for amendments. For the intelligence agencies whose collection rests on 702 orders, the difference is continuity instead of a lapse.

## What to watch

Watch the final NDAA text for a 702 reauthorization provision, and whether it emerges before the session ends rather than being pushed into the next Congress.
