<div class="story-lead" markdown="1">

Tesla's robotaxi account said on Friday that it had invited "select customers" to buy their own Cybercab, a two-seat gold vehicle with no steering wheel and no brake pedals, for $29,900 with delivery promised in December. Tesla called it a "limited release" but did not say how many cars or customers are involved. Buyers would also owe $299 a month to keep the car on the road, according to the report, though Tesla has not publicly detailed what that fee covers. The offer differs from Waymo and Amazon's Zoox, which sell rides on their own networks rather than vehicles to individuals.

</div>

<div class="fact-check" markdown="1">

## Verified and unconfirmed

- **Verified:** Tesla's own robotaxi social accounts posted that select customers were invited to purchase a Cybercab for $29,900 with December delivery, describing it as a limited release; the vehicle has no steering wheel or pedals.
- **Not yet confirmed:** how many cars or buyers are included, what the $299 monthly charge pays for, whether regulators in buyers' states will permit private ownership of a vehicle without steering-wheel or brake controls, and whether December deliveries will actually happen.

</div>

## Background

Tesla already runs a robotaxi ride-hailing service of its own; AI Daily Radar's November 16 digest reported that its Miami coverage area had nearly doubled to 59 square miles with 24-hour rides — but those are rides in Tesla-operated cars, not vehicles sold to individuals. The same digest reported Tesla's plan to spend €3 billion expanding Gigafactory Grünheide near Berlin, including a building designed to make up to one million Cybercabs a year once fully scaled, with Cybercab production there slated from 2029 — far larger than the limited December delivery run now on offer.

## Why it matters

For a buyer, the $29,900 sticker is only part of the cost: at $299 a month, a Cybercab owner pays roughly $3,600 a year on top of the purchase price, so the economics only work if the car drives enough — and Tesla has not published the terms, cancellation rights or what happens if the self-driving software is unavailable.

## What to watch

Watch December to see whether Tesla actually delivers cars to the invited customers and how many, since that would separate a limited release from a real public sale.
