LanceDB Inc., which sells what it calls a "multimodal lakehouse" for AI training, announced a $57.5M Series B led by Bessemer and Innovation Endeavors. The platform is built on Lance, an open data format, and is aimed at storing and querying the mixed media — images, video, audio, embeddings — that multimodal models train on, rather than the text-heavy tables a conventional data warehouse handles. The announcement, reported by SiliconANGLE, gives no valuation, total amount raised to date, customer counts, or a timeline for how the new capital will be spent.

## Background

LanceDB is trying to address a specific data problem in model development: training corpora now mix images, video, audio and text, while conventional analytics stacks were designed mainly for tables. A “lakehouse” combines inexpensive file storage with database-style management, and LanceDB says its lakehouse is built on Lance, an open format for that mixed-media data. The company announced the round on its own blog, and Bessemer published a companion post describing LanceDB as “the data layer for multimodal AI.”

## Why it matters

For teams assembling training data for multimodal models, the relevant detail is the open format: because the lakehouse sits on Lance rather than a proprietary store, data written to it is not tied to LanceDB's own software, which is the lock-in question worth testing before a purchase. The funding also puts another venture-backed vendor alongside established table-format and vector-database options that buyers will compare it against.
