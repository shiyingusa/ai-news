<div class="story-lead" markdown="1">

Reuters reported, citing unnamed sources, that US President Donald Trump and Vice President JD Vance personally intervened in Nvidia's decision to reinstate Jensen Huang as CEO over the weekend, convinced that Huang alone was protecting American-made open-weight AI models — models whose trained weights are published so anyone can download and modify them. The report, as summarized by Techmeme, does not explain why Huang needed to be reinstated, what form the intervention took, or how Nvidia and the White House responded.

Neither Nvidia nor the administration has confirmed the account on the record.

</div>

<div class="fact-check" markdown="1">

## Verified and unconfirmed

- **Verified:** Reuters reported, citing sources it did not name, that President Donald Trump and Vice President JD Vance personally intervened in Nvidia's decision to reinstate Jensen Huang as CEO over the weekend, convinced he alone protected American-made open-weight AI models (Reuters, byline Steve Holland and Courtney Rozen, Nov 23). Trump himself acknowledged speaking to Nvidia's board directly about Huang, and Semafor reported that he dialed into Nvidia's Sunday board meeting hours before the reinstatement was announced. Separately, the Senate banking committee announced that Huang, now reinstated, will testify on 3 December in a hearing titled "Protecting America's AI Advantage: Oversight of the US Export Controls."
- **Not yet confirmed:** Why Huang had been out of the CEO role, what specifically Trump and Vance said to the board, and whether the open-weight AI argument was the board's actual stated reason rather than the officials' belief as described by Reuters' unnamed sources. The report carries no on-record comment from Nvidia, the board, the White House or Huang, and no company statement, commitments on open-weight policy, or details of any quid pro quo appear in the available accounts.

</div>

## Background

Open-weight models are AI models whose trained weights are published so anyone can download, inspect, modify and run them on their own hardware; Nvidia's own policy paper, "Open Weights and American AI Leadership," argues they widen access to the AI economy rather than concentrating it. Nvidia has lobbied publicly on the question: Reuters reported that Huang wrote to US lawmakers earlier this year urging them to support open-weight AI development rather than restrict it.
