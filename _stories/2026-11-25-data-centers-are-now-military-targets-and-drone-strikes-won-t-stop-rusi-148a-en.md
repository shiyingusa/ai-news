<div class="story-lead" markdown="1">

Ars Technica hosted a live discussion in which Joseph Jarnecki of the Royal United Services Institute (RUSI, a UK defense think tank) argued that public cloud data centers have become military targets because armed forces now run battlefield management systems, HR and resource management on commercial compute — and that under international targeting principles, military use can turn civilian infrastructure into a military objective. He added that cheap kamikaze drones can impose disproportionate economic cost and widespread cloud disruption on such facilities.

The conversation follows what Ars describes as the first-ever military attack on Amazon data centers in the Middle East this year, and an unprecedented exchange of drone strikes on data centers between Russia and Ukraine. Jarnecki's conclusion that the attacks will continue is an analyst's assessment offered in the discussion, not a measured forecast, and the recap gives no independent count of strikes or damage assessments.

<div class="fact-check" markdown="1">

## Verified and unconfirmed

- **Verified:** Ars Technica's own recap confirms it hosted a live conversation between reporter Jeremy Hsu and RUSI's Joseph Jarnecki, and quotes Jarnecki saying that military use of cloud compute can make civilian data centers legitimate military objects and create an incentive to target them.
- **Not yet confirmed:** the prediction that attacks on data centers will continue is Jarnecki's judgment, not a documented trend line; the recap offers no tally of incidents, no attribution for specific strikes beyond the countries named, and no independent damage assessment. The description of the Middle East strikes as the first-ever military attack on Amazon data centers is Ars's characterization, and Ars's own earlier September reporting — that Iranian strikes caused permanent loss of customer data — is referenced but not detailed here.

</div>

## Background

The warning rests on a shift that has already happened in this conflict: Ars Technica reported in September 2026 that Iranian strikes on Amazon data centers caused permanent loss of customer data, and the live discussion treated that as the opening case rather than a hypothetical. The concept behind it is "dual use" — when a military runs battlefield-management systems, HR or resource planning on commercial cloud compute, targeting principles can reclassify a civilian facility as a military object, which is why Joseph Jarnecki of the Royal United Services Institute argues cheap kamikaze drones now carry disproportionate economic and service-disruption leverage against public cloud infrastructure.

## Why it matters

Organizations running workloads in public cloud regions near conflict zones cannot treat geographic redundancy inside one provider as protection: Ars's earlier reporting said Iranian strikes on Amazon data centers caused permanent loss of customer data, which points to verifying off-provider or off-region backups and checking whether cloud contracts' war and force-majeure clauses cover kinetic damage.
