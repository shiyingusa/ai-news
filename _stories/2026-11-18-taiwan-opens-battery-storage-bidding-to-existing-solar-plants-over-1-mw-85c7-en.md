<div class="story-lead" markdown="1">

Taiwan's Ministry of Economic Affairs (MOEA) has published a regulatory framework letting existing grid-connected solar plants larger than 1 MW bid for permission to add battery energy storage, with a competitive auction setting the capacity tariff paid to winners. Eligible projects must install at least 2.61 MWh of storage for every MW of rated storage power, and successful applicants must generally finish installation and grid connection within 15 months. In exchange, they must free up grid connection capacity equal to 1.5 times their rated storage power, and they receive a daily guaranteed billing quantity tied to 2.61 times that rated capacity, subject to conditions. Discharge is scheduled by the state utility Taipower, part of an effort to move solar output out of the midday peak. The article states the guidelines were issued on November 6, 2024, an internal date that is inconsistent with the November 2026 publication date.

</div>

## Verified and unconfirmed

- **Verified:** pv magazine, citing the MOEA's published regulatory framework in Taiwan's official gazette, reports the >1 MW eligibility threshold, the 2.61 MWh-per-MW minimum sizing rule, the 1.5x grid-capacity release requirement, the 15-month completion window and Taipower-coordinated dispatch.
- **Not yet confirmed:** the issuance date is contradictory — the text says November 6, 2024, while the article is dated November 2026 — and the sources give no tariff levels, auction timetable, volume caps or budget, nor confirmation that a first bidding round has actually opened.

</div>

## Background

Taiwan's January 2025 plan targeted an additional 8.2 GW of combined solar and offshore wind capacity by the end of 2026, but deployment has run slower than anticipated, and the government has kept 2026 solar feed-in tariff levels unchanged from the second half of 2025 to sustain investment. Because most of the island's utility-scale PV was connected without on-site batteries, its output is concentrated in midday hours, and moving that electricity to more valuable periods depends on storage sitting at or near existing plants. The framework's discharge rules, which follow Taipower's scheduling and dispatch requirements, are aimed at that shift rather than at building new generation.

## Why it matters

Owners of existing Taiwanese solar plants above 1 MW now face a concrete trade-off: the framework pays a bid-determined capacity tariff and guarantees a daily billable quantity, but requires surrendering grid connection capacity at 1.5 times the rated storage power and completing the build within 15 months. Developers should check whether their interconnection point can release that much capacity before bidding, since the requirement is tied to storage power rather than to the energy capacity they are obliged to install.

## What to watch

Watch for the MOEA or Taipower to publish the first bidding round's schedule, offered capacity and awarded tariffs, which would also settle the confusion over when the framework took effect.
