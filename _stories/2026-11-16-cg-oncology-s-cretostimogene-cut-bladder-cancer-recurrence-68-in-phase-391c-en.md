<div class="story-lead" markdown="1">

CG Oncology said Monday that cretostimogene, an immunotherapy infused directly into the bladder, reduced the risk of tumor recurrence by 68% compared with current medical practice in a randomized Phase 3 trial of 367 patients with intermediate-risk bladder cancer. The treatment is built from a common cold virus engineered to target cells carrying a specific mutation found in bladder tumors; delivered by catheter, it enters those cells, replicates rapidly and ruptures them. The figures are topline results announced by the company, not peer-reviewed data, and the available report does not give absolute recurrence rates, a statistical significance measure, or safety results. Intermediate-risk bladder cancer is a localized form that is usually removed surgically and then monitored every three months, with repeat procedures if the cancer returns.

## Verified and unconfirmed

- **Verified:** CG Oncology announced topline results from a randomized Phase 3 trial in 367 patients with intermediate-risk bladder cancer showing a 68% reduction in recurrence risk versus current medical practice, according to the company as reported by STAT+.
- **Not yet confirmed:** the absolute recurrence rates behind the relative 68% figure, statistical significance, durability and safety data, and whether the results have been submitted for regulatory review — all rest on the company's announcement, with no peer-reviewed publication or independent verification in the supplied material.

</div>

## Background

Cretostimogene belongs to a class called oncolytic viruses: a common cold virus engineered to enter cells carrying a specific mutation found in bladder tumors, replicate inside them and burst them open. Because the bladder is a hollow organ, the therapy can be infused directly through a catheter rather than delivered systemically. The patients in this trial have intermediate-risk bladder cancer, a localized form of the disease that is usually removed with a minimally invasive operation and then checked every three months; if tumors return, surgery can be repeated, but repeated procedures eventually cost patients bladder function while the cancer can keep growing.

## Why it matters

For these patients the current path is repeated surgery and monitoring rather than a drug, so a therapy that delays recurrence could postpone the loss of bladder function that comes with successive procedures — but because the 68% is a relative risk reduction, clinicians cannot judge the actual benefit without the absolute event rates and safety data that the announcement leaves out.
