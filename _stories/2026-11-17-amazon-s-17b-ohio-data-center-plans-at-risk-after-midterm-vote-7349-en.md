Voters in Ohio passed midterm ballot measures restricting data-center development, putting three proposed Amazon projects at risk within a planned investment of more than $17 billion, according to a Financial Times analysis re-reported by Techmeme. The report frames the vote as part of a wider pattern of local resistance to Big Tech's AI infrastructure buildout across the US.

The account is a news analysis rather than an Amazon statement, regulatory filing or project notice, so it does not specify the terms of the measures, which three projects are affected, or how much of the $17 billion-plus figure is now in doubt. Amazon has not publicly confirmed any cancellation, delay or change to its Ohio plans in the supplied material.

## Background

In the US, data-center projects depend on local land-use approvals and tax or utility arrangements, which in some jurisdictions can be overturned or blocked through municipal or county ballot measures — giving voters a direct route to stop specific projects rather than national AI policy. That route was used widely this cycle: Fox Business reported that data centers were themselves on the ballot in communities across the country, with voters backing restrictions on development. The electricity demand behind that backlash has also been quantified recently — AI Daily Radar's November 16 digest noted Harvard research, reported by Wired, finding that the 403 US data centers operating between May 2024 and April 2025 used as much power over the year as the entire state of Michigan, a figure that excludes facilities running their own off-grid gas plants.

## Why it matters

For AI compute planning, this makes county- and municipal-level ballot outcomes a gating factor alongside power, land and permits: an approved site can be blocked after the money is budgeted, so developers bear a new category of siting risk that is hard to hedge through conventional utility or zoning negotiations. Companies tracking Amazon's data-center pipeline should treat its Ohio capacity as unconfirmed until the company restates the project scope.

## What to watch

Whether Amazon publicly restates the scope, timing or dollar figure of its Ohio plans, and whether the passed measures face legal challenges that could suspend them before the three projects proceed.
