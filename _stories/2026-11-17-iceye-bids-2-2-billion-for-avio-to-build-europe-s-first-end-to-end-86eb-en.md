Finnish satellite developer ICEYE announced Tuesday an all-cash voluntary tender offer, made through an Italian subsidiary, to buy Italian rocket-maker Avio for more than $2.2 billion, with the stated goal of creating Europe's first vertically integrated end-to-end space company. Avio said its board "has taken note of the notice" and is considering the offer; the company's shareholders are led by the Italian defense conglomerate Leonardo.

Avio builds the solid-fueled Vega C launcher, designed to carry up to 3.3 metric tons to low-Earth orbit, which has flown successfully seven times in eight attempts since 2022, following an earlier Vega that went 20-for-22 since its 2012 debut. The transaction is proposed, not agreed: no price per share, timetable, or shareholder acceptance was disclosed.

## Verified and unconfirmed

- **Verified:** ICEYE announced the all-cash tender offer for Avio, valued at more than $2.2 billion and made through an Italian subsidiary, and Avio's board confirmed it has received notice and is considering it, according to the companies as reported by Ars Technica; Avio's Vega C has seven successes in eight flights since 2022, and the earlier Vega flew 20-for-22 since 2012.
- **Not yet confirmed:** whether Avio's shareholders, including lead holder Leonardo, will tender their shares or whether the board will recommend the offer; the per-share price, financing, regulatory approvals, and closing timeline; and any claim that the combined company will actually operate as a fully integrated launch-plus-satellite business, since that rests on ICEYE's own framing of the deal.

</div>

## Background

ICEYE, founded in 2014, operates the world's largest constellation of synthetic aperture radar (SAR) microsatellites — radar imagers that can capture imagery through cloud cover and darkness, unlike optical satellites — and has so far bought launches rather than owning a rocket. Avio's Vega C is itself a follow-on to the original Vega launcher, which debuted in 2012.

## Why it matters

If shareholders accept, a satellite operator would own its own launcher for the first time in Europe, letting ICEYE schedule its constellation's launches directly on Vega C — a rocket with a seven-of-eight success record since 2022 — rather than competing for rides. The immediate action point sits with Avio's investors: Leonardo and other holders must decide whether to sell, and until they do, Avio's existing Vega C launch customers face uncertainty over who controls the vehicle.

## What to watch

Avio's formal board response to the offer and whether Leonardo, its leading shareholder, commits to tendering its stake — the first checkable signals of whether the deal proceeds.
