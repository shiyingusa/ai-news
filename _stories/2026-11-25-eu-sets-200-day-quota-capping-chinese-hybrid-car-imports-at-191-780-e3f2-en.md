The European Union will limit imports of hybrid cars from China to 191,780 units over a 200-day period starting in December, electrive reported, and the same regulation restricts hybrid imports from other countries as well. A quota is a volume cap rather than a duty: it fixes how many cars may enter, not what they cost, and the report does not say whether shipments above the ceiling are refused or charged differently.

The report gives no breakdown of how the 191,780 units are shared among manufacturers, no figure for the restrictions applied to non-Chinese suppliers, and no statement of what happens when the 200 days expire. It also does not name the legal instrument behind the measure or describe the approval process that made it official.

</div>

<div class="fact-check" markdown="1">

## Verified and unconfirmed

- **Verified:** The EU has adopted a provisional cap allowing up to 191,780 China-made hybrid and plug-in hybrid passenger cars into the bloc from 1 December 2026 to 18 June 2027 — the 200-day period — as reported by carnewschina.com, Bloomberg and auto.mail.ru. Bloomberg reports the measure is temporary while the EU carries out a probe that is expected to lead to longer-term restrictions.
- **Not yet confirmed:** The reports give no detail on the parallel restrictions on hybrid imports from countries other than China — which countries are covered, at what volumes, or how those limits are calculated. auto.mail.ru describes plug-in hybrids as the main target, while carnewschina.com says the cap covers both hybrids and plug-in hybrids; no source details how the quota is allocated among manufacturers or how it will be enforced.

</div>

## Background

The measure targets hybrids — vehicles that pair a combustion engine with electric drive — rather than battery-only cars, and it takes the form of a volume cap (a quota counted in units) rather than a per-car duty. The cap runs for an initial 200 days from December, and the regulation also restricts hybrid imports from countries other than China, though the report does not give those volumes, the exact start date, or which countries are covered.

## Why it matters

The quota turns a fast-growing sales channel into a fixed allocation: Chinese hybrid exporters must fit within 191,780 units over 200 days — roughly 959 vehicles a day — after brands such as BYD, Chery and Leapmotor posted triple-digit EU growth. Importers and dealers should plan for tighter availability during the 200-day window rather than unlimited volume growth.

## What to watch

Watch for the quota window to open in December and for the EU's detailed rules on how the 191,780 units are allocated, which is when the effect on individual brands becomes measurable. Whether Brussels extends the measure once the 200-day period ends is the next decision after that.
