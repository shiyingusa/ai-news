Canada has announced just over $634 million to expand electric-vehicle charging infrastructure and grid capacity, with the charging funding intended to roughly double the more than 50,000 chargers supported through existing federal programs. The government is pairing that with a national electricity strategy that targets more than $50 billion to double Canada's grid capacity by 2050.

The item describes announced investment and targets, not completed construction or measured charger counts; it does not give a timeline for the charging expansion or specify how the money will be split.

</div>

## Background

Public charging in Canada has so far been built largely through government support programmes; the source notes that existing programmes have backed more than 50,000 chargers, a number the new funding is meant to roughly double. "Grid capacity" refers to how much electricity a network can deliver at times of peak demand, so adding chargers and electrifying transport raises the upstream requirement on transmission and distribution infrastructure as well as on charging sites themselves.

## Impact

For Canadian EV drivers and charging operators, the intended consequence is that the federally supported public charger base would grow from more than 50,000 to roughly 100,000, though the source does not say when the new chargers will be operational or where they will be located.
