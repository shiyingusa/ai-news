<div class="story-lead" markdown="1">

BYD unveiled the Whale, a new flagship electric minivan (MPV), claiming it can travel over 559 miles (900 km) on a single charge and recharge in as little as five minutes. The company says the vehicle carries its latest technology, but the report gives no battery capacity, charger power required for the five-minute figure, test cycle behind the 900 km number, price, or launch market.

The range and charging figures are therefore BYD's own announced claims, not independently measured results, and no independent test has been reported.

</div>

<div class="fact-check" markdown="1">

## Verified and unconfirmed

- **Verified:** BYD officially unveiled the Whale at the 2026 Guangzhou Auto Show, describing it as the first MPV in its Ocean series and a 5.3-meter vehicle offered as both a battery-electric and a plug-in hybrid, according to CarNewsChina and Autoevolution; BYD's stated range figure is 900 km measured on China's CLTC test cycle, which Electrek converts to roughly 559 miles.
- **Not yet confirmed:** the five-minute charging claim, which rests on BYD's own announcement with no published battery size, charger type, or starting charge level; whether the 900 km CLTC figure translates to comparable range under US EPA or European WLTP testing; and price, on-sale date, and availability outside China, none of which the reports provide.

</div>

## Context

BYD has been rolling out flagship models in China in quick succession: AI Daily Radar's November 25 digest reported the third-generation Tang SUV launching there with a sub-$30,000 starting price, and that report likewise gave no battery, charging or range figures. The test cycle matters for reading the Whale's 900 km figure, since Chinese range numbers are commonly quoted on CLTC, the country's light-vehicle test standard, which typically yields higher results than Europe's WLTP or the US EPA cycle — the basis on which Volkswagen's China joint venture quoted 580 km for its ID. Aura L5 sedan, as AI Daily Radar's November 17 digest noted. The Whale report does not say which cycle, trim or conditions the 900 km and five-minute charging claims refer to.

## Why it matters

Because the report includes no price, launch market, or charging conditions, buyers and fleet operators cannot yet compare the Whale against existing electric minivans; the five-minute charge claim in particular depends on charger power that BYD has not specified, so it says little about what owners will experience on today's public charging hardware.
