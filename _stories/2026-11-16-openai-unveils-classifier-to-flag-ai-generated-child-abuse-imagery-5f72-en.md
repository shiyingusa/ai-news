<div class="story-lead" markdown="1">

At a Monday press briefing in Washington, DC, OpenAI announced a classifier it says can better detect abusive imagery of children, including flagging whether an image was AI-generated. The company said it will give vetted organizations free access to the tool, publish additional guidance for developers on detecting this type of content, and help train law enforcement on using AI in investigations. OpenAI did not disclose detection rates, a general availability date, or the criteria organizations must meet to be approved. The announcement describes a program being stood up rather than a tool the public or most developers can use today.

## Verified and unconfirmed

- **Verified:** OpenAI announced the classifier and the accompanying free access, developer guidance and law enforcement training at a Monday briefing in Washington, DC, as reported by The Verge.
- **Not yet confirmed:** OpenAI's claim that the classifier detects abusive imagery better than existing tools, including AI-generated material, which rests on the company's word with no published benchmark or independent test; the eligibility rules and vetting process for free access; and when, or whether, the tool becomes broadly available.

</div>

## Background

Automated CSAM detection has largely depended on matching files against databases of known material, which cannot catch images that have never been catalogued before — including AI-generated ones. OpenAI says its new classifier instead looks for visual signs of abuse so investigators can flag novel images, building on the company's existing child-safety protections. The announcement comes as policymakers push for tighter safety restrictions on AI tools, pressing OpenAI and other labs over the misuse of their systems.

## Why it matters

Child-safety investigators typically receive far more flagged material than they can review, so a classifier that reliably separates new AI-generated imagery from the flood is the practical bottleneck — but organizations can only plan around it once OpenAI publishes who qualifies as vetted and how quickly access is granted, neither of which the announcement specifies.

## What to watch

Watch OpenAI's linked policy page for the published eligibility criteria and any independent measurement of the classifier's detection and false-positive rates, which would be the first check on the company's accuracy claim.
