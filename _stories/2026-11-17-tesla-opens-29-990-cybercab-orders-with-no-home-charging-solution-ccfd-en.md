Electrek reports that Tesla has opened orders for the $29,990 Cybercab, with first deliveries expected in Austin next month. The report says the vehicle has no onboard charger, so it can only use DC fast charging, and the wireless home charging pad Tesla has been teasing since 2023 is still not available to buy. That leaves buyers without a home-charging option at launch unless Tesla releases the pad or offers another accessory. The account is a secondary report and does not specify an exact delivery date or a price for the pad.

## Verified and unconfirmed

- **Verified:** Electrek reports that Tesla is taking orders for the Cybercab at $29,990 with first deliveries expected in Austin next month, and that the vehicle has no onboard charger, so it cannot take AC power from a wall connector, public Level 2 station or household outlet and depends on DC fast charging; the wireless home charging pad Tesla has teased since 2023 is still not for sale. Follow-up coverage repeats these same points and attributes them to Electrek.
- **Not yet confirmed:** The supplied text does not include a Tesla statement or order page confirming the $29,990 price, the December delivery timing, or the lack of an onboard charger, and the Electrek article is truncated before its full argument; no availability date or price for the wireless home pad is given, and no independent outlet has tested or documented the charging limitation firsthand.

</div>

## Background

Most EVs carry an onboard charger, the component that converts household AC power into the DC voltage the battery needs, which is what lets them refill overnight from a wall outlet or a Level 2 home charger. The Cybercab lacks one, so per Electrek it can only take DC fast charging — the kind found at public stations — unless Tesla supplies a separate way to charge at home. Tesla has been showing a wireless home charging pad since 2023, but that product is still not something customers can buy, which is why the missing onboard charger becomes a practical problem as orders open.

## Why it matters

Buyers in the first Austin deliveries will have no way to charge the Cybercab at home unless Tesla starts selling the wireless pad or another home-charging accessory; because the car lacks an onboard charger, a standard AC home charger will not work. Prospective buyers should confirm a home-charging plan before placing an order.

## What to watch

Watch Tesla's expected first Cybercab deliveries in Austin in December 2026, and whether the wireless home charging pad becomes orderable by then.
