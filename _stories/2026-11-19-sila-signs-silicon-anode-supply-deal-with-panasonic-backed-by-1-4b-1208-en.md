Sila has signed a long-term offtake agreement to supply Panasonic Energy with silicon-carbon anode material for lithium-ion batteries, a deal Sila says could be worth more than $1 billion over time. Neither the total volume nor the price Panasonic will pay was disclosed; CEO Gene Berdichevsky said Panasonic committed to buying tens of thousands of tons, which he said would let Sila scale to at least 10 times its current production. Sila's Moses Lake, Washington factory opened in September 2025 and is already operating, and part of the financing behind its output is a $1.4 billion conditional loan commitment from the Defense Department, framed by the company as a response to a "significant technology sovereignty challenge." Sila says its anode weighs a fifth as much as the graphite used in conventional anodes and takes half the volume, which would let batteries store more energy per kilogram and recharge faster — figures that remain the company's own.

</div>

## Verified and unconfirmed

- **Verified:** Sila and Panasonic Energy announced a long-term offtake agreement for silicon-carbon anode material; Sila's own announcement states the deal and the company says it could exceed $1 billion over time. Sila opened its Moses Lake factory in September 2025 and it is operating, following a $100 million Department of Energy grant announced in 2022. The Defense Department committed a $1.4 billion loan, which Sila's press release describes as conditional.
- **Not yet confirmed:** the dollar value, total tonnage and price are not disclosed, so the $1 billion figure is Sila's estimate rather than a contracted number. The weight, volume, power and charging claims for the anode come from Sila, not independent testing, and no timeline was given for when Panasonic cells using the material reach commercial products.

</div>

## Background

Conventional lithium-ion battery anodes are made mostly of graphite, and China controls more than 90% of graphite production today — the dependence the US has been trying to reduce through domestic battery-material funding. Sila, founded in 2011, first put its silicon-based anode into Whoop fitness wearables in 2021, then announced a Moses Lake, Washington factory in 2022 with $100 million from the Department of Energy; that plant opened in September 2025 and is already operating, giving the Panasonic agreement existing production to scale from.

## Why it matters

This is Sila's first mass-market agreement, and the committed volume is what would move a novel anode chemistry from a niche wearable customer to automotive-scale cell manufacturing — the step where most US battery startups have failed, including 24M and ESS Tech, which the report says declared bankruptcy and shut down in the past year. For cell makers, the practical question is qualification: Panasonic must validate a new anode material in its own cells before any of this volume ships.

## What to watch

Whether the conditional $1.4 billion Defense Department loan closes, and when Panasonic announces cells or vehicles using Sila's anode material.
