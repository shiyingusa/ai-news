<div class="story-lead" markdown="1">

Amazon-owned Zoox's robotaxis can now be hailed through the Uber app in Las Vegas, the company's first partnership with a ride-hailing platform. TechCrunch reports the tie-up as a first for Zoox, which until now has not distributed its vehicles through a third-party app. Neither company disclosed the size of the fleet exposed to Uber, the area covered, pricing, or the operational terms of the arrangement, so the scale of the service is unclear. The report also gives no ridership or performance data showing how the vehicles are doing under Uber demand.

</div>

<div class="fact-check" markdown="1">

## Verified and unconfirmed

- **Verified:** According to TechCrunch, Zoox robotaxis are now bookable on the Uber app in Las Vegas, and this is the first time the Amazon-owned company has partnered with a ride-hailing platform.
- **Not yet confirmed:** Fleet size, geographic coverage within Las Vegas, fares, service hours, whether vehicles run without human safety operators, and whether Zoox's own app continues to offer rides — none of these details appear in the available reporting, and neither company's claims were independently tested.

</div>

## Background

Until now, riders could only hail a Zoox through the company's own app, so the Uber integration is the first time the Amazon-owned company's vehicles can be booked from a third-party ride-hailing platform. The companies describe the same sequence for Los Angeles: Zoox plans to begin its own operations there by the end of 2026, with the Uber partnership following in 2027.

## Why it matters

For riders in Las Vegas, Zoox vehicles become reachable through an app many already have installed, rather than only through Zoox's own channel; for Zoox, it is a first test of whether demand routed from a major platform sustains utilization better than its standalone service. Because no coverage area or vehicle count was disclosed, riders should not assume a Zoox car will be available for any given trip.
