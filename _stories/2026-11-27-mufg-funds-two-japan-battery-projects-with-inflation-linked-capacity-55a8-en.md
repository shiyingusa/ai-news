MUFG Bank has made initial loan disbursements under project-finance agreements for two grid-scale battery storage projects in Japan, HEXA Energy Services said: one in Bibai, Hokkaido, and one in Kumamoto, with combined grid-connection capacity of 90 MW. Both projects won capacity contracts in Japan's fiscal 2023 long-term decarbonization power auction, and the loans are the first to price in the auction's newly introduced contract price adjustment mechanism, which indexes payments to changes in inflation and interest rates.

HEXA said the initial disbursements were completed in about two months, but it did not disclose the loan amount, tenor, or the projects' storage capacity in megawatt-hours.

## Verified and unconfirmed

- **Verified:** HEXA Energy Services and MUFG Bank both said initial loan disbursements have been made for the two projects, and the companies put their combined grid-connection capacity at 90 MW; the projects won contracts in the fiscal 2023 long-term decarbonization auction, and MUFG said the financing addressed the auction's new contract price adjustment mechanism.
- **Not yet confirmed:** the loan amount, tenor, per-project megawatt-hour capacity, and the exact formula or caps of the inflation- and interest-linked price adjustment, none of which HEXA disclosed; no independent party has confirmed the financing terms.

</div>

## Background

Japan's Long-Term Decarbonization Power Source Auction, launched in fiscal 2023, awards successful bidders 20-year fixed capacity payments to offset the large upfront cost of assets such as grid-scale batteries; the first auction was held in January 2024. Because those payments were fixed in nominal terms, inflation eroded their value, and the government raised the auction's price cap ahead of its third round to counter inflation pressures. The contract price adjustment mechanism indexing payments to inflation and interest rates that MUFG had to model is the newer layer of that same design.

## Why it matters

This is HEXA's third round of project debt for auction-backed batteries, giving other fiscal 2023 auction winners a worked example of how banks will treat inflation- and interest-indexed capacity payments — a structure lenders had not previously financed in Japan. Developers weighing bids in later auction rounds should watch how much of the indexation risk lenders absorb versus pass back to the project.

## What to watch

HEXA plans to arrange project finance in its 2026 fiscal year for a battery project in Oyabe, Toyama prefecture; whether that deal reuses the same indexed-contract terms will show if the structure is becoming standard. Separately, Japan's connection queue — about 172 GW at the study stage versus roughly 0.64 GW actually connected at the end of 2025 — remains the binding constraint on turning financed projects into operating ones.
