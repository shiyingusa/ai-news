<div class="story-lead" markdown="1">

Robotics startup Embo emerged from stealth with a $110 million seed round led by Andreessen Horowitz (a16z) to build what it describes as a universal robot brain based on world models — AI systems that learn how physical reality behaves rather than only how text is sequenced. According to the company's announcement, its models are trained on large amounts of internet video and practice motor skills in simulation.

The team includes researchers from Google DeepMind, Meta and MIT. Embo has not released a product, published benchmark results, named customers, or given a timeline for availability.

## Verified and unconfirmed

- **Verified:** Embo announced its public launch and a $110 million seed round led by a16z, according to the company's own announcement.
- **Not yet confirmed:** The technical claims — that video-trained world models can generalize across robot bodies and acquire fine-grained motor skills in simulation — rest only on Embo's description; no model release, benchmark, demo video, customer deployment, valuation, full investor list or launch date has been disclosed.

## Background

A world model is an AI system that learns to predict how a physical scene will change over time from observation, rather than mapping sensor inputs directly to actions; Embo says it trains on large amounts of internet video and then practices motor skills in simulation. The company emerged from stealth alongside the announcement, with Andreessen Horowitz leading the $110 million seed round and Khosla Ventures, DST Global and Nvidia's venture arm NVentures participating. Aiparabellum reported that the team is led by former DeepMind researcher Danijar Hafner.
