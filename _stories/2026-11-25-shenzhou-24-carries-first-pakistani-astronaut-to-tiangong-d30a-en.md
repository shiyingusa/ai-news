China launched Shenzhou-24 on Nov. 25 at 00:18 UTC (08:18 Beijing time) from the Jiuquan Satellite Launch Center atop a Chang Zheng 2F/G, carrying commander Tang Hongbo, flight engineer Wu Yi and Pakistani payload specialist Muhammad Zeeshan Ali — the first foreign national to fly to the Tiangong station. The China Manned Space Agency named the crew at a press conference the day before launch.

Ali, who has trained in Beijing since April under a China–Pakistan agreement signed in February 2025, will stay about a week, run joint microgravity experiments and install experiment units before returning to Earth aboard the Shenzhou-23 craft; his countryman Khurram Daud was assigned as backup. Tang becomes the first astronaut to visit Tiangong three times, Wu is on his first flight, and Shenzhou-23 commander Zhu Yangzhu, who stays on, is set to become China's first astronaut to spend a year in orbit in a single visit.

It is China's 18th crewed spaceflight and the eighth of the station's application-and-development phase.

## Background

The flight follows a cooperation agreement China and Pakistan signed in February 2025, under which the two Pakistani astronauts selected for the mission began training in April at the China Astronaut Research and Training Center in Beijing. Until now, only Chinese crews had stayed aboard Tiangong, so Shenzhou-24 turns that bilateral training arrangement into the station's first foreign crew presence.

## Why it matters

Tiangong now has a working precedent for hosting foreign astronauts, but the access offered is narrow: a roughly one-week payload-specialist slot that depends on riding home on another crew's vehicle, rather than a full six-month expedition. Organizations negotiating similar arrangements should expect short-duration, experiment-focused visits rather than long-term crew positions.

## What to watch

Ali's return to Earth aboard Shenzhou-23, expected about a week after the Nov. 25 launch, will be the first test of the short-visit template. Zhu Yangzhu's year-in-orbit mark, roughly six months from now, is the next milestone the mission is tracking.
