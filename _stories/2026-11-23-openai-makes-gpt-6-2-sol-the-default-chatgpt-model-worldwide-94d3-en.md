<div class="story-lead" markdown="1">

OpenAI is switching ChatGPT's chat tab to a new default model, GPT-6.2 Sol, and rolling it out worldwide. The company says the model handles complex, multi-part everyday instructions correctly 78.1 percent of the time and scores 71.7 percent on its internal image-understanding test, up from 47.4 percent for the model it replaces.

OpenAI also claims the model adheres to its safety policies more consistently. All of these figures come from OpenAI's own testing; the report gives no methodology, no date for full rollout, and no word on API access or whether users can still select the previous default.

</div>

<div class="fact-check" markdown="1">

## Verified and unconfirmed

- **Verified:** The Decoder reports that OpenAI is making GPT-6.2 Sol the default model in ChatGPT's chat tab with a worldwide rollout, and that OpenAI cites internal-test improvements in image understanding (47.4 to 71.7 percent), difficult-instruction handling (78.1 percent correct), and safety-policy adherence.
- **Not yet confirmed:** The benchmark numbers are OpenAI's internal figures with no published test sets, scoring method, or comparison protocol, and no independent evaluation has reproduced them. The report also leaves out the exact rollout timeline, whether the model is available through the API, and whether users can keep using the previous default.

</div>

## Background

AI Daily Radar's November 19 digest reported that OpenAI released GPT-6.2 Astra and the cheaper GPT-6.2 Sol on November 18, with Sol priced at $2 per million input tokens and $10 per million output tokens; both models accept text and images but return text only. That launch framed Sol as an API model, and the change now places it in front of ChatGPT's general user base as the default in the chat tab.

## Why it matters

Because this is the default in the chat tab rather than an opt-in model, every ChatGPT user gets the change without choosing it, and prompts, guardrails, or evaluation suites tuned to the previous default's behavior may shift. Teams that depend on stable ChatGPT output should re-run their own tests against GPT-6.2 Sol rather than relying on the vendor's internal percentages.

## What to watch

Watch for OpenAI to publish the methodology behind the 71.7 percent image and 78.1 percent instruction-following figures, and for third-party benchmarks or leaderboard entries that can be compared against the model it replaced.
