<div class="story-lead" markdown="1">

CrowdStrike has announced plans to acquire Zscaler for $44 billion, a deal that would fold a major standalone zero-trust security vendor into the endpoint-security company. CrowdStrike said the purchase would better equip its customers to use AI agents, according to TechCrunch's report. Zscaler sells zero-trust products to large enterprises — access and network controls built on the assumption that no user or device is trusted by default. The report gives no deal structure, per-share price, financing plan, expected closing date or regulatory timeline, and carries no confirmation from Zscaler itself.

</div>

<div class="fact-check" markdown="1">

## Verified and unconfirmed

- **Verified:** CrowdStrike has agreed to acquire Zscaler in an all-stock transaction that values the zero-trust security provider at about $43.9 billion, according to statements reported by CNBC, Bloomberg and The Information; CNBC reported the deal is expected to close in the second half of CrowdStrike's fiscal 2028, and CrowdStrike said the combination would better equip customers to use AI agents.
- **Not yet confirmed:** The $44 billion figure in the headline is a rounding of the ~$43.9 billion stated deal value, and the sources give no exchange ratio, regulatory-review status, breakup terms, or financing detail. Neither company's own press release is in the available reporting, and no independent assessment of the price or of the AI-agent rationale has been published, so the strategic justification rests on CrowdStrike's word.

</div>

## Background

Zero trust is a security model that assumes no user, device or connection should be trusted by default and instead verifies each request before granting access; Zscaler sells that model to large enterprises as cloud-delivered gateways and network access controls. CrowdStrike built its business on endpoint and cloud-workload protection, so the reported deal would pair agent and endpoint telemetry with network-level enforcement. CrowdStrike framed the rationale around helping customers deploy AI agents, software that acts on a user's behalf across systems and therefore needs identity and access checks at every step.

## Why it matters

Enterprises that run both CrowdStrike and Zscaler will eventually face one combined product roadmap with overlapping controls, so security teams with contracts up for renewal should review change-of-control and termination terms before signing multi-year commitments. Until the transaction closes, the two companies continue to sell and support their products separately.

## What to watch

Watch for a definitive merger agreement and any confirmation or comment from Zscaler itself, neither of which appears in the report. Those would establish the terms and whether the deal is actually binding.
