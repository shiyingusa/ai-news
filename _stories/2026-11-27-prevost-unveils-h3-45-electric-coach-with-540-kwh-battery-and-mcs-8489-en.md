Prevost has unveiled the H3-45 Electric, its first battery-electric motorcoach, with a 540 kWh Volvo battery, a 450 kW (600 hp) dual-motor powertrain, and CCS1 plus MCS (Megawatt Charging System) charging as standard. The H3-45 is Prevost's flagship highway coach, and the Quebec builder owned by Volvo Group pitches the electric version as the same coach drivers and technicians already know, with a different powertrain.

Prevost cites a 250-mile range, but the unveiling did not include production timing, pricing, or delivery availability.

</div>

## Background

The H3-45 is Prevost's flagship model and one of the most common highway coaches in North America, so the electric version is being pitched as the same vehicle operators and technicians already know, with only the powertrain changed. MCS, the second charging standard Prevost lists alongside the more familiar CCS1 connector, stands for Megawatt Charging System — a high-power standard intended for heavy-duty vehicles rather than passenger cars. Fitting both as standard means a coach can plug into ordinary CCS1 chargers or into megawatt-class points built for trucks and buses.

## Why it matters

For North American coach operators, the standard MCS port is the operationally significant detail: it gives the H3-45 Electric a path to high-power en-route charging rather than relying only on CCS1. That advantage is conditional on access to MCS charging sites and on a production and delivery schedule Prevost has not yet announced.

## What to watch

Watch for Prevost to announce production and delivery timing, pricing, and an independent range or charging test for the H3-45 Electric; the unveiling provided specifications but not those details.
