The US District Court for the District of Maryland ruled in favor of the Consumer Product Safety Commission (CPSC) in Amazon's lawsuit against the agency, holding that Amazon is a distributor of the goods sold on its marketplace rather than merely a logistics provider. As a result, Amazon must carry out recalls for more than 400,000 "Fulfilled by Amazon" third-party products the agency says pose a "substantial product hazard," including faulty carbon monoxide detectors and hair dryers without electrocution protection.

Amazon had sued the CPSC in March 2025 to overturn the recall order. The decision comes from a federal district court, so Amazon can still appeal.

## Background

The dispute began with a CPSC order requiring Amazon to recall more than 400,000 "Fulfilled by Amazon" items — goods sold by third-party sellers but stored and shipped through Amazon's warehouses — that the agency deemed a "substantial product hazard." Amazon sued the CPSC in March 2025, arguing that because it only stores and ships those goods it acts as a logistics provider rather than a distributor, and therefore is not the party responsible for carrying out recalls. The Maryland court rejected that distinction, which is what puts the earlier recall order back into effect.

## Why it matters

Amazon now has to notify buyers and manage recalls itself for the affected listings instead of leaving that to the third-party sellers behind them, and the CPSC gains a court-backed basis for treating large marketplaces as distributors — a precedent that applies to any platform hosting outside sellers. Sellers whose Fulfilled by Amazon inventory falls under the order will have recalls run through Amazon's process rather than their own.

## What to watch

Whether Amazon appeals the ruling to the US Court of Appeals for the Fourth Circuit, which would put the question of marketplace distributor liability before an appellate panel.
