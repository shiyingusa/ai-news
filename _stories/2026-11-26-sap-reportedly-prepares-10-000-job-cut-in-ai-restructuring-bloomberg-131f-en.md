SAP is preparing a plan to eliminate at least 10,000 positions — roughly 9% of its global workforce — as part of an effort to transform its software business for the AI era, Bloomberg reported on November 26, citing people familiar with the matter. The report, attributed to Christina Kyriasoglou and unnamed sources, describes planning rather than an announced program: SAP has not publicly confirmed the cuts, and the report gives no timeline, cost estimate, or breakdown of which units, regions or roles would be affected.

No figure beyond the "at least 10,000" minimum and the 9% share was disclosed.

## Verified and unconfirmed

- **Verified:** Bloomberg reported on November 26, citing people familiar with the matter, that SAP is preparing a plan to eliminate at least 10,000 positions — roughly 9% of its global workforce — as part of a restructuring of its software business for the AI era.
- **Not yet confirmed:** SAP has not publicly confirmed the plan, and the report does not establish a timeline, which units would be affected, or whether the 10,000 figure is a final number; secondary summaries say product development and sales could be hit, and one outlet, Zerohedge, called it a "second round" of AI restructuring, but neither detail is corroborated by SAP or by Bloomberg's published excerpt.

</div>

## Background

SAP SE is a German enterprise-software company whose core products are ERP (enterprise resource planning) systems — the software large organizations use to run finance, supply-chain, procurement and HR processes. The reported restructuring is framed as reshaping that business for the AI era, which for SAP means embedding AI features into its business-software portfolio while cutting at least 10,000 positions, about 9% of its global workforce. The plan has not been announced: Bloomberg's account rests on people familiar with the matter.

## Why it matters

At about 9% of global staff, the reported reduction would be large enough to touch many SAP teams at once, so employees and customers have a direct stake in whether the plan is confirmed — and because the reporting rests on unnamed sources with no unit or regional detail, neither group has official information yet on what would change.

## What to watch

Watch for SAP's own confirmation or denial of the plan, and for any statement from its employee representatives in Germany; the report did not say when an announcement is expected.
