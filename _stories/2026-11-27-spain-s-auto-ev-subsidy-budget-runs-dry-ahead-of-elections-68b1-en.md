Spain's Auto+ purchase-incentive programme for private buyers has used up its budget after the government allocated €350 million for electric cars and plug-in hybrids over a short period. According to electrive, the pot will not be refilled "for the foreseeable future" because parliamentary elections are approaching.

The upshot is that private buyers in Spain can no longer count on the national purchase subsidy when pricing an EV or plug-in hybrid. The report does not say whether applications already in the pipeline will still be paid.

</div>

<div class="fact-check" markdown="1">

## Verified and unconfirmed

- **Verified:** electrive reports that the Auto+ budget for private individuals is exhausted and that €350 million was allocated to purchase incentives for electric cars and plug-in hybrids.
- **Not yet confirmed:** the report cites no primary government document, so the exact remaining balance, the treatment of pending applications, and the claim that funding will not be restored for the foreseeable future rest on the outlet's reporting rather than an official statement.

</div>

## Background

Auto+ is Spain's purchase-incentive scheme for private individuals buying battery-electric cars and plug-in hybrids; it pays part of the upfront price, lowering the effective cost of the vehicle. Spain allocated €350 million to the program.

## Why it matters

Private buyers in Spain lose the national purchase incentive as a factor in their purchasing decision, effectively raising the net price of an electric or plug-in hybrid car relative to the subsidised level, while the report leaves open what happens to buyers who applied before the money ran out — a question worth resolving with a dealer or the administering body before committing to an order.

## What to watch

Watch Spain's parliamentary elections and the funding decisions of the government that follows them, since the report ties the decision not to replenish Auto+ to that vote.
