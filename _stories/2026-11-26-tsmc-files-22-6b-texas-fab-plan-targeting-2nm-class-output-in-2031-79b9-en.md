TSMC's Arizona subsidiary has applied to Texas's JETI incentive program for a $22.6 billion fab campus on a 3,232-acre site southwest of College Station in Brazos County — its first advanced-chip campus outside Arizona. The filing describes commercial operations beginning in 2031 on 2nm-class or more advanced chips, with full operations in 2032, six years after N2 entered volume production in Taiwan in Q4 2025.

Construction is projected to start in 2027, and the requested school property-tax limit is valued at about $354 million; the Texas Comptroller has recommended approval, but the school district and the governor still must grant it. Nothing in the filing commits TSMC to buy the land or to build.

</div>

## Verified and unconfirmed

- **Verified:** TSMC Arizona Corporation's JETI application to the Texas Comptroller sets out a $22.6 billion project on a 3,232-acre site, construction spending from 2027, commercial operations in 2031, about $354 million in school-tax relief, and roughly 2,099 new hires; the Comptroller has recommended the application for approval.
- **Not yet confirmed:** that TSMC will buy the land or build, that the school district and governor will approve the tax limitation, and that the budget is sufficient — Tom's Hardware estimates a single 20,000-wafer-per-month 2nm module costs $25 billion to $35 billion, above this plan's total.

</div>

## Background

Chip process names such as 2nm and A14 are generation labels rather than literal measurements, and TSMC began volume production of its first 2nm-class node (N2) in Taiwan in Q4 2025. Its only US advanced fabs today are in Arizona, where a second fab is scheduled to start 3nm volume production in Q3 2027, so the Brazos County site would be TSMC's second US advanced campus. The filing is a request under JETI, Texas's Jobs, Energy, Technology and Innovation program, which grants a limitation on school property taxes — the same mechanism AI Daily Radar's November 19 digest reported Tesla using for a proposed $3.1 billion polysilicon plant.

## Why it matters

For buyers counting on US-made leading-edge silicon, the schedule keeps the most advanced US output a generation behind Taiwan: Arizona's second fab starts at 3nm in Q3 2027, and by the time the Texas site runs 2nm in 2031, TSMC expects its next full node, A14, to have been in volume production since 2028. Because the application commits TSMC to neither land nor construction, capacity planners should treat the campus as an option rather than added supply.

## What to watch

Whether the Brazos County school district and the Texas governor approve the JETI tax limitation, and whether TSMC commits to the site ahead of the projected 2027 construction start.
