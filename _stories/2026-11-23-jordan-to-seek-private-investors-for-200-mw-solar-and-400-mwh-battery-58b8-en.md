Jordan's Ministry of Energy and Mineral Resources said at the inaugural Jordan-EU Investment Conference last week that it will invite private investors early next year to build a 200 MW solar plant and a 100 MW/400 MWh battery energy storage system (BESS). Both would run on a build-own-operate model, with state-owned National Electric Power Company buying the solar output and acting as beneficiary of the storage project.

The ministry puts the solar plant at roughly $100 million to $130 million of investment, with an initial prequalification request expected in February 2027, and the BESS at $70 million to $90 million, with prequalification expected in March 2027. Project locations have not been announced, and the two projects sit inside a wider package that also includes a 100 MW wind project, a 450 MW pumped-storage hydropower plant, shared infrastructure for green hydrogen and green ammonia, and natural gas projects.

## Verified and unconfirmed

- **Verified:** pv magazine, citing the ministry's conference announcement, reports the 200 MW solar and 100 MW/400 MWh BESS plans, the build-own-operate structure with NEPCO as offtaker and storage beneficiary, the $100–130 million and $70–90 million investment ranges, and the February and March 2027 prequalification dates.
- **Not yet confirmed:** The cost ranges are planning estimates rather than bid or contract prices; locations, tariffs, financing terms and the storage technology supplier are all undisclosed, and no project has been financed or built.

</div>

## Background

The projects use a build-own-operate model in which the state-owned National Electric Power Company buys the solar plant's output and acts as beneficiary of the battery system, following a pattern Jordan has used to bring in private power producers. Growth in the country's solar fleet has stalled: IRENA put Jordan's cumulative solar capacity at 2,195 MW at the end of 2025, the same figure it reported a year earlier. In September, the energy regulator approved the first 200 renewable energy systems under a revised grid-connection framework that varies the arrangements available by customer category and consumption profile.

## Why it matters

For developers and financiers, the concrete near-term step is prequalification: interested investors need to be ready to respond to a solar request in February 2027 and a storage request a month later, in a procurement where the offtaker is state-owned NEPCO rather than a private buyer. For scale, IRENA figures cited by pv magazine put Jordan's cumulative solar capacity at 2,195 MW at the end of 2025 — the same as a year earlier — so the proposed 200 MW plant would be a meaningful addition to a fleet that did not grow last year, while the 400 MWh battery would be one of the country's larger storage assets.

## What to watch

Watch for the ministry's prequalification requests for the solar project in February 2027 and the BESS in March 2027, and for the project locations and final investment terms, which have not been disclosed.
