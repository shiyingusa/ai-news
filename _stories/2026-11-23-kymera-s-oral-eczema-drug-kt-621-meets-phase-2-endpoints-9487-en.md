<div class="story-lead" markdown="1">

Kymera Therapeutics reported Monday that its oral STAT6 degrader KT-621 — a pill designed to break down the STAT6 protein — met all primary and secondary endpoints in the Phase 2 BROADEN2 trial in moderate-to-severe eczema, with statistically significant improvements over placebo across all three doses after 16 weeks. At the middle dose, patients had a peak 45% placebo-adjusted improvement in disease severity, and 62% reached EASI-75 (at least 75% improvement) versus 22% on placebo. Kymera said the drug was generally safe and well tolerated; conjunctivitis was the only side effect more common on treatment, at 20%, 13% and 11% for low, middle and high doses, all mild to moderate and none leading to discontinuation. The results are company-announced and have not been peer-reviewed or published in full.

</div>

<div class="fact-check" markdown="1">

## Verified and unconfirmed

- **Verified:** Kymera's announcement states that BROADEN2 met all primary and secondary endpoints, including statistically significant placebo comparisons at all three doses and a safety profile led by mild-to-moderate conjunctivitis.
- **Not yet confirmed:** Independent verification is absent because the data have not been peer-reviewed or published; the available report omits trial size, full safety data and durability beyond 16 weeks, and no regulatory timeline is given.

</div>

## Background

KT-621 is not a conventional drug that blocks a receptor: it is an oral "degrader" designed to eliminate STAT6, a protein in the type 2 inflammation pathway, and Kymera describes it as first-in-class, having begun first-in-human dosing in 2024. Earlier supportive evidence came at the 2026 American Academy of Dermatology meeting, where Kymera presented late-breaking Phase 1b data in moderate-to-severe atopic dermatitis showing deep target engagement, broad suppression of type 2 inflammatory biomarkers and clinical improvement in adults. The incumbent it is aimed at is Dupixent, the injectable biologic that has dominated eczema treatment; BROADEN2 is the first placebo-controlled test of whether a once-daily pill can match that class.

## Why it matters

For patients with moderate-to-severe eczema, the readout keeps an oral alternative to injected Dupixent (dupilumab) moving through development, a convenience advantage if later trials confirm it. The current evidence is mid-stage and company-reported, so clinicians cannot yet compare its risk-benefit profile with approved biologics.

## What to watch

Watch for peer-reviewed publication or conference presentation of the full BROADEN2 dataset, and for Kymera to disclose Phase 3 and regulatory plans; no date is given for either.
