<div class="story-lead" markdown="1">

China plans to launch Shenzhou-24 to the Tiangong station no earlier than Nov. 25 at 00:18 UTC from the Jiuquan Satellite Launch Center, with a three-person crew that includes Pakistan's Muhammad Zeeshan Ali as payload specialist — the first foreign national assigned to the station. Ali, who has trained in Beijing since April under a February 2025 China–Pakistan agreement, is to stay about a week and return to Earth aboard the Shenzhou-23 craft, while his colleague Khurram Daud remains on the ground as backup.

Tang Hongbo commands the mission and would become the first astronaut to visit Tiangong three times; flight engineer Wu Yi is making his first flight. The mission has not yet flown — this is a scheduled launch, and the China Manned Space Agency announced the crew names at a news conference the day before liftoff.

</div>

<div class="fact-check" markdown="1">

## Verified and unconfirmed

- **Verified:** CMSA named Tang Hongbo, Wu Yi and Muhammad Zeeshan Ali as the Shenzhou-24 crew at a pre-launch news conference, and the Chang Zheng 2F/G carrying the spacecraft rolled out to Site 91 on Nov. 17; Tiangong adjusted its orbit on Nov. 23 ahead of the arrival.
- **Not yet confirmed:** The Nov. 25 launch time is a target — the report says "no earlier than" — and Ali's roughly one-week stay, the docking time and his return date are mission plans rather than flown results; no independent confirmation of the schedule is given.

</div>

## Background

Tiangong has so far hosted only Chinese crews; the February 2025 agreement with Pakistan set up selection and training at the China Astronaut Research and Training Center in Beijing, which the two Pakistani astronauts began in April. Shenzhou-23 commander Zhu Yangzhu is to stay aboard for a further six months and become China's first astronaut to spend a year in orbit in a single visit.

## Why it matters

The short-duration payload-specialist slot is the arrangement CMSA is testing for foreign participation: Ali flies alongside an existing crew and departs on the outgoing Shenzhou-23 vehicle rather than serving a full expedition, so his week in orbit depends on that spacecraft staying docked and fit to return. A completed flight would give Pakistan its first national in orbit and hand other partner countries a concrete precedent for negotiating similar seats.

## What to watch

Whether Shenzhou-24 lifts off within the Nov. 25 window and docks with Tiangong, and when Ali returns aboard Shenzhou-23 — the report gives no docking or return date.
