Switzerland's Federal Roads Office (FEDRO) has awarded the 32 locations it tendered in January 2026 for truck charging along national roads to three companies, split into lots: Lot 1 to Fastned, Lot 2 to Obornes and Lot 3 to Milence. Together the sites are to host 82 charging stations for heavy-duty electric trucks. The report gives no charging capacity, construction start, completion date or pricing for the stations, so the network exists so far only as an award rather than working infrastructure.

## Verified and unconfirmed

- **Verified:** Switzerland's Federal Roads Office (FEDRO) awarded the 32 locations it tendered in January 2026 for 82 truck charging stations along national roads, split into three lots — Lot 1 to Fastned, Lot 2 to Obornes, Lot 3 to Milence — electrive reported.
- **Not yet confirmed:** the rollout timetable — first stations in 2030 and all 82 by 2035 — appears in other outlets' coverage (mobility-360, The EV Report, IN Power) but not in electrive's report, and no charging power, contract terms, pricing or site-by-site details were disclosed.

</div>

## Background

FEDRO tendered the 32 locations in January 2026 as part of a programme placed under Switzerland's Energy Strategy 2050 and the transport ministry's electromobility roadmap, which targets 82 fast-charging stations for trucks along the national road network by 2035. The first stations from this award are expected to open in 2030, so the sites announced now are a long-term build-out rather than near-term capacity.

## Why it matters

Because Switzerland divided the national-road corridor into three lots held by three different operators, hauliers planning electric routes will need accounts or roaming agreements with Fastned, Obornes and Milence rather than a single provider, and coverage will depend on all three building out their awarded sites.
