Electric vehicles outsold petrol and diesel cars combined in the European Union in October, InsideEVs reported, turning a 127,000-car deficit in the same month a year earlier into a lead of nearly 70,000 cars. The report presents the result as a single-month crossover and does not give total registrations, market-share percentages, or the underlying registration data the comparison rests on.

It also leaves the powertrain categories undefined — whether "EV" covers only battery-electric cars or includes plug-in hybrids is not stated — so the exact scope of the flip is not established by the account.

</div>

<div class="fact-check" markdown="1">

## Verified and unconfirmed

- **Verified:** ACEA's October registration release reports battery-electric cars at 23.4% of EU new-car registrations in January–October 2026, up from 16.4% a year earlier, with petrol and diesel combined at 27.7%, down from 36.5%; it also reports 279,799 battery-electric registrations in October alone, up 61.4% year on year.
- **Not yet confirmed:** That EVs outsold petrol and diesel cars combined in October by nearly 70,000 units, and that the prior-year gap was 127,000, rests on InsideEVs' report; the supplied ACEA figures give year-to-date shares and October BEV volume but no October petrol-plus-diesel total, and the year-to-date shares (BEV 23.4% vs petrol and diesel 27.7%) do not by themselves establish the October overtake. The term “EVs” is also undefined—battery-electric only or including plug-in hybrids.

</div>

## Background

AI Daily Radar's November 24 digest, citing figures from ACEA, the European automakers' association, reported that battery-electric cars took nearly 30% of EU passenger-car registrations in October — roughly 280,000 units, 61% more than in the same month a year earlier and above the 250,000-plus monthly pace pure electric cars had recently been sustaining. That established the volume behind the shift; the new comparison adds the other side of the ledger, where combined petrol and diesel sales held a 127,000-car lead a year earlier but trailed by nearly 70,000 cars in October. The earlier ACEA-based report gave no country-by-country or model breakdown, and the same level of detail is missing from the combined tally.

## Why it matters

For automakers and dealers selling in the EU, a month in which EVs are the largest group in the new-car market changes the volume base used for product planning and charging investment — but only if it persists. The report does not separate how much of the 197,000-car swing came from EV growth versus falling petrol and diesel sales, which is the first thing to check before treating October as a new baseline rather than an outlier month.

## What to watch

Watch the next monthly EU new-car registration figures to see whether EVs keep the lead, and whether the shift is driven by rising EV volumes or by a decline in petrol and diesel sales.
