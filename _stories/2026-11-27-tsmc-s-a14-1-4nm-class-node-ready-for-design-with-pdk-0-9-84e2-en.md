<div class="story-lead" markdown="1">

TSMC says its A14 (1.4nm-class) process is ready for customer design work, with version 0.9 of its process design kit ready for design-in and Cadence and Synopsys having completed full design-flow certification on an Arm Cortex-A725 CPU core using TSMC foundation IP. The node adds Boost Cells, which TSMC says deliver 14% higher performance at the same power or 23% lower total power at the same speed than N2P with NanoFlex; TSMC also claims 50% better energy efficiency, 22% higher drawn transistor density and 23% higher logic density than baseline N2P.

TSMC plans risk production in the second half of 2027 and mass production in the second half of 2028. It also outlined an A14-to-A13 migration path that it says can cut die area by about 6% without changing the A14 design flow, with A13 high-volume manufacturing set for the second half of 2029.

## Verified and unconfirmed

- **Verified:** TSMC said at its 2026 Europe OIP Ecosystem Workshop that A14 PDK 0.9 is ready for design-in and that Cadence and Synopsys completed full design-flow certification on an Arm Cortex-A725 core; the claimed 14% speed, 23% power, 50% energy-efficiency, 22% transistor-density and 23% logic-density gains are TSMC's own comparisons against N2P.
- **Not yet confirmed:** independent measurements of those gains, actual customer tapeout results or yields, A14 pricing, and whether the H2 2027 risk-production and H2 2028 mass-production schedules hold.

</div>

## Background

A process design kit (PDK) is the bundle of transistor models, design rules and standard-cell libraries a foundry gives customers so commercial EDA tools can simulate, place and route a chip; version 0.9 is the revision the industry conventionally treats as usable for real design work, which is why TSMC frames it as "ready for design-in" well before any silicon ships. A14 is the successor to N2P, TSMC's 2nm-class node with its NanoFlex cell architecture, and the performance, power and density figures in the announcement are all stated against that N2P baseline.

## Why it matters

Chip designers planning A14 products can now start implementation with Cadence or Synopsys flows that TSMC has certified end-to-end on an Arm Cortex-A725 core, reducing the risk that tool gaps delay a project. Teams using Siemens EDA have only partial certification—5 of 13 categories as of October 1—and must wait for transistor-level EM/IR certification targeted for the fourth quarter of 2026 before that flow covers the full sign-off chain.

## What to watch

Siemens EDA's transistor-level EM/IR certification is targeted for the fourth quarter of 2026; the next hard checkpoints after that are A14 risk production in the second half of 2027 and mass production in the second half of 2028.
