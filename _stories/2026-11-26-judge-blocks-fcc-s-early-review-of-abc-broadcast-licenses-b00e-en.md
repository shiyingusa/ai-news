US District Judge Loren AliKhan issued a preliminary injunction barring the Federal Communications Commission from conducting an early review of ABC's broadcast licenses, and rejected the agency's attempt to dismiss ABC's First Amendment lawsuit. In the ruling, the judge said ABC was "likely to succeed on their retaliation claim" — the network alleges the FCC "waged a retaliatory campaign" against it over content it broadcast. The dispute began after the FCC ordered ABC last April to file for license renewal early, even though Disney's licenses were not due for renewal until 2028. The injunction is a preliminary ruling, not a final decision on the merits of the case.

## Verified and unconfirmed

- **Verified:** US District Judge Loren AliKhan granted ABC a preliminary injunction barring the FCC from conducting its early review of ABC's broadcast station licenses while the case proceeds, and rejected the agency's motion to dismiss ABC's First Amendment retaliation lawsuit, according to the ruling linked in The Verge's report and matching accounts from CNN, The Guardian and the New York Post. The judge wrote that ABC was "likely to succeed on their retaliation claim" and that Disney's lawyers had "sufficiently cast the early license renewal proceedings as a retaliatory act."
- **Not yet confirmed:** This is a preliminary-stage ruling, not a final judgment on the merits, so the retaliation claim itself has not been proven; the ruling's exact scope and duration, whether the FCC will appeal, and any statement from the agency or Chair Brendan Carr were not established by the available reports. The reports also give no date for the next hearing or trial step.

</div>

## Background

US broadcast licenses are granted in eight-year terms and renewed by the FCC, so Disney's ABC stations were not due for renewal until 2028. The dispute escalated when the FCC ordered the network to file for renewal early — a step The Verge reported in April — and ABC sued, arguing the agency was retaliating against it for content it aired. A preliminary injunction freezes the licensing review while the First Amendment claim proceeds; it is an early-stage order, not a final ruling on the merits of the case.

## Why it matters

While the injunction stands, the FCC cannot use the license-renewal process to press ABC over its programming, and Disney keeps its current licenses on their original 2028 renewal timetable. The ruling also sets an early marker that a court found a retaliation claim against the agency strong enough to justify emergency relief, a point other broadcasters facing similar pressure will be watching.

## What to watch

Watch whether the FCC appeals the injunction to the DC Circuit, and how Judge AliKhan rules on the merits of ABC's retaliation claim as the case proceeds.
