A federal jury in New York on Tuesday convicted Huawei and its subsidiaries of conspiracy to steal trade secrets and bank fraud, while acquitting the Chinese telecom giant of the most severe charge, criminal racketeering conspiracy, according to the Justice Department and the Associated Press. The Verge reported that the verdict concludes a years-long case that accused Huawei of stealing source code and proprietary technology from companies including T-Mobile, Cisco and Motorola.

The charges stem from the DOJ's 2018 criminal indictment, which became public after the arrest of Huawei CFO Wanzhou Meng; the indictment against Meng was later dismissed, though she admitted misleading banks about Huawei's operations.

## Verified and unconfirmed

- **Verified:** The US Department of Justice states that a federal jury in the Eastern District of New York returned guilty verdicts against Huawei Technologies and three of its subsidiaries for sanctions violations involving Iran, lying to banks, money laundering and stealing trade secrets from American companies, following a trial the DOJ describes as three months long. AP reported the jury convicted Huawei of conspiracy to steal trade secrets and bank fraud, and acquitted it of the most serious charge, criminal racketeering conspiracy.
- **Not yet confirmed:** Sentencing date, the size of any penalty, and whether Huawei will appeal are not stated in the reports. The accounts also disagree on the trial's length — the DOJ says three months, while Yahoo's report says two months — and the Verge piece does not mention the racketeering acquittal or the specific counts, so the full count-by-count breakdown rests on the AP and DOJ accounts rather than a single reconciled record.

</div>

## The case behind the verdict

The conviction closes a prosecution that began in 2018, when the US Justice Department unsealed a criminal indictment against Huawei and its subsidiaries covering trade-secret theft, sanctions violations and fraud conspiracy. The charges surfaced around the December 2018 arrest of Huawei CFO Meng Wanzhou in Canada on fraud accusations; the indictment against her was later dismissed after she admitted to misleading banks about Huawei's operations — a 2022 Reuters report cited by The Verge describes that dismissal. The trade-secret thread long predates the trial: Huawei had been accused of taking source code and proprietary technology from several companies, including T-Mobile, whose dispute over a phone-testing robot was shown to jurors.
