Indium phosphide (InP), the compound semiconductor used to make the lasers and photodetectors that turn electrical data into light, is the binding constraint on the optical links that AI data centers are switching to, according to this Tom's Hardware explainer. Sumitomo Electric and AXT together account for almost 80% of global InP substrate manufacturing, with JX Advanced Metals adding roughly another 10%.

AXT makes most of its substrates in China, where exports now require government licences, and China produces around 70% of the world's refined indium, so controls bite on both the raw material and the finished wafer. Lumentum CEO Michael Hurlston said in July that its InP fabs were shipping more than 30% below customer demand despite rapid capacity expansion, and Coherent called InP capacity the "primary constraint" on its transceiver business.

The report adds that Nvidia has invested $2 billion each in Lumentum and Coherent, attaching multibillion-dollar purchase commitments and future capacity rights, while new substrate and laser capacity can take years to qualify.

## Background

Indium phosphide is a III-V compound semiconductor with a direct bandgap of about 1.34 eV, which lets it emit light efficiently; silicon's indirect bandgap mostly produces heat instead. That is why silicon-photonic systems, which can guide and modulate light using mature CMOS-style processes, still rely on InP-based lasers and other active optical components — demand that is growing as AI data centers move from copper links to optics.

## Why it matters

Because Nvidia has tied its optical-component supply to capacity rights and purchase commitments at Lumentum and Coherent, buyers outside those agreements may find remaining InP laser and transceiver capacity allocated first to Nvidia's accelerator buildouts. Moving to silicon photonics does not remove the dependency: silicon can guide and modulate light but has an indirect bandgap and does not emit it, so these systems still need a III-V laser, usually built on InP.

## What to watch

Watch Lumentum's and Coherent's upcoming quarterly results for whether InP fab output closes the gap with demand, and whether China's export-licensing regime for indium and InP wafers tightens or eases.
