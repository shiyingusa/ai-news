A global memory chip shortage has pushed companies around the world to set up procurement "war rooms" to secure supply, and some chip prices have risen as much as tenfold over the past year, the Wall Street Journal reported. Those component costs are feeding through into higher prices for electronics, the paper said. The report cites Blackmagic Design among the companies affected but gives few further specifics on which chip types, vendors or regions are hardest hit.

- **Verified:** The Wall Street Journal reports that a worldwide memory chip shortage has led companies to form dedicated procurement teams and that some chip prices have climbed as much as 10x in a year, driving electronics prices up.

## Background

Memory chips — DRAM used as working memory and NAND flash used for storage — are commodity components whose prices swing sharply with supply and demand, since fab capacity takes years to build and cannot be quickly expanded. Because they go into almost every PC, phone, server and consumer device, a squeeze on memory supply shows up in bill-of-materials costs across the electronics industry, and large buyers typically rely on long-term supply agreements and direct vendor relationships to secure allocation.

## Why it matters

Buyers of PCs, servers and consumer hardware should expect tighter allocation and higher quoted prices rather than list-price stability, which makes locking in contract volumes and auditing memory inventory an immediate procurement task rather than a later one.

## What to watch

Watch the next round of quarterly results and guidance from the major memory manufacturers for confirmation of how long contract prices keep rising, and whether the tenfold increases reported for some parts persist or ease.
