<div class="story-lead" markdown="1">

The attorneys general of Oregon and Virginia have sued several of Elon Musk's companies, alleging that the Grok chatbot was shipped with safeguards too weak to stop it from producing sexualized imagery of real people, including children. Wired reported the filings, which target Musk's companies rather than a single product team. The report does not specify which entities are named as defendants, in which courts the suits were filed, or what relief the states are seeking. The allegations have not been tested in court.

## Background

Grok is the chatbot and image generator from Elon Musk's xAI, and "nudification" refers to using such tools to produce sexualized images of real people without their consent. CNBC reported that Oregon and Virginia are the first states to sue over the Grok image generator, and according to Yahoo News the complaints allege SpaceX weakened industry-standard safeguards against nudification in order to set Grok apart from competing products. The suits name several of Musk's companies rather than only the AI developer.

## Why it matters

For companies running image or chatbot products in the United States, the suits signal that state attorneys general will pursue consumer-protection and child-safety theories directly against AI developers over outputs, not just over marketing claims—so operators should expect state-by-state standards for blocking sexualized depictions of real people, and should preserve logs and moderation records that show what safeguards were actually deployed.

## What to watch

Watch the public court dockets in Oregon and Virginia for the named defendants and claims, and for any motion seeking an injunction against Grok's image features or a substantive response from Musk's companies.
