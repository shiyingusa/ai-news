Kalshi Inc. says the U.S. Commodity Futures Trading Commission (CFTC), the federal regulator for derivatives markets, ordered it to stop trading on "mention markets" — event contracts that pay out based on what President Donald Trump says during public events. Bloomberg's Bobby Allyn reported the company's account on November 17, 2026. The report gives no detail on the order's scope, its legal reasoning, or how many contracts are affected, and it is not clear from the item whether the markets are already closed or whether open positions will be voided or settled. The CFTC's own statement on the action was not included.

</div>

## Verified and unconfirmed

- **Verified:** Kalshi says the CFTC ordered it to halt trading on markets tied to what President Trump will say at public events, as reported by Bloomberg's Bobby Allyn on November 17, 2026.
- **Not yet confirmed:** the order's scope, legal basis and date; whether the CFTC has publicly confirmed or characterized it; and whether trading has actually stopped or how existing positions will be resolved.

</div>

## Background

Prediction-market platforms like Kalshi can list event contracts by self-certifying them with the CFTC rather than seeking the agency's prior approval, provided the contracts comply with the Commodity Exchange Act. That self-certification route was already under pressure: CNBC reported on November 11, 2026 that the CFTC was investigating whether Kalshi improperly self-certified event contracts, and Bloomberg described the new takedown order as the latest sign that prediction markets face greater scrutiny after the midterm elections.

## Why it matters

Traders holding positions in these contracts face an immediate question the report does not answer — whether their markets settle at the last traded price, void at a refund, or resolve on some other basis — so anyone exposed should check Kalshi's own notices rather than assume a payout.

## What to watch

Watch for a public CFTC statement or filing describing the order, and for Kalshi's notice on how it will close out or settle existing positions in the affected markets.
