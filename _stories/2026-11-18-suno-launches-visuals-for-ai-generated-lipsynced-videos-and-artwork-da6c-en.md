<div class="story-lead" markdown="1">

Suno launched Visuals, a feature that turns Suno-generated songs into AI video clips that automatically lipsync to lyrics, and also generates prompt-based images for thumbnails or album artwork. It is available on web, iOS, and Android through a new Visuals tab in Create mode; on the web, users can also open it from the three-dot menu on a song in their library. The Verge reports that the layout differs between Suno's desktop and mobile interfaces. The report does not detail output resolution, export formats, or plan limits.

</div>

<div class="fact-check" markdown="1">

## Verified and unconfirmed

- **Verified:** Suno announced a feature called Visuals that turns Suno songs into AI-generated video clips that automatically lipsync to lyrics, and generates images from text prompts for thumbnails and album artwork; it sits in a new Visuals tab under Create mode and is also reachable from a song's three-dot menu in the library, per Suno's own blog post and The Verge.
- **Not yet confirmed:** The sources disagree on launch availability — The Verge says Visuals is live on web, iOS and Android, while Unite.ai reports the iOS and Android versions were slated for "the following few weeks" and were not part of the launch release. Unite.ai also says visual credits are purchased separately from song credits on a dedicated Visuals pricing page, a detail the primary source does not address, and no independent test of output quality or pricing figures has been published.

</div>

## Background

Suno's product has centered on generating songs, and Visuals is its first move into image and video output, so the new tools build on material users already have rather than only on fresh prompts. The feature sits inside Create mode, the same area where songs are made, and on the web it can also be launched from a song in a user's library — which is why turning an existing track into a lyric-synced clip is a one-step action rather than a separate workflow.

## Why it matters

Creators can now produce lipsynced clips and artwork inside Suno rather than moving songs to separate video or image tools, but they will need details on resolution, export formats, and plan limits before relying on it for published work.
