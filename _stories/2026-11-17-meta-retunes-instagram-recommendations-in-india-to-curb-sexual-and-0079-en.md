<div class="story-lead" markdown="1">

Meta said on Tuesday that it had modified Instagram's recommendation systems specifically for India to show less sexual and violent content, after New Delhi called for tighter content checks. The change affects the posts and short videos Instagram recommends beyond the accounts a user follows, though Meta's statement as reported by Reuters gave no figures on how much such content was reduced or how the tuning was measured.

The disclosure makes India a country-specific exception in how Instagram's recommendations are calibrated.

## Background

Instagram's recommendation systems are the algorithms that decide which posts and short videos, chiefly Reels, to surface to a user from accounts the user does not already follow — separate from content that appears simply because someone chose to follow an account. In India, platforms operate under intermediary rules that make them responsible for content on their services and expose them to government takedown and information requests, so changing the recommender itself, rather than removing individual posts, is a country-specific compliance step rather than a routine product tweak.

## Why it matters

Because the tuning is scoped to India's recommendation systems, an account's suggested reach can now differ by market: material Meta classifies as sexually suggestive or violent is less likely to be recommended to users in India while remaining eligible for recommendation elsewhere. Meta has not disclosed which signals or thresholds it changed, so creators and advertisers focused on Indian audiences cannot yet measure the effect except by comparing Indian and non-Indian recommendation impressions before and after the 17 November announcement.

## What to watch

Watch for a public response from the Indian government or a Meta transparency disclosure detailing the India-specific tuning, which would clarify whether regulators accept the change and how broadly it applies.
