A UK tribunal ruled that the Home Office must acknowledge it issued a secret "technical capability notice" (TCN) to Apple in connection with bypassing its encryption, rejecting the government's claim that disclosing the order would damage national security. The Financial Times' Tim Bradshaw reported the decision, which privacy rights campaigners called "a big win for accountability, transparency and online security." The ruling settles whether the government must confirm the notice exists, not whether the underlying demand on Apple is lawful, and it does not disclose the notice's operational content.

## Background

A technical capability notice is a UK order under the Investigatory Powers Act 2016, the surveillance law nicknamed the "snoopers' charter", that can compel a technology company to take specified steps to help government agencies obtain data. This dispute began when the Home Office issued Apple such a notice in January 2025 under Section 253 of the Act, reported to have required a backdoor into its encrypted iCloud backup system. Apple took the matter to the Investigatory Powers Tribunal, arguing the government should not be allowed to refuse to confirm whether such an order existed — the question the tribunal has now resolved against the Home Office.

## Why it matters

The decision removes one route by which the UK could refuse even to confirm or deny that it has served a TCN on a technology company, giving the target company and outside groups a basis to challenge that secrecy in future cases. Because the tribunal addressed only disclosure, companies that receive such notices still face the underlying demand itself, and the practical effect depends on whether the Home Office complies or appeals.

## What to watch

Watch whether the Home Office formally acknowledges the notice or instead seeks permission to appeal the tribunal's ruling, and whether the tribunal publishes its full reasoning.
