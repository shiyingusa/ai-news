<div class="story-lead" markdown="1">

Huawei announced that HarmonyOS, its Android-free operating system, has surpassed 100 million installations across smartphones, tablets and PCs, the South China Morning Post reported. The company says the platform offers more than 460,000 apps and services and has more than 12 million registered developers. The milestone is a vendor-reported installation count, not an active-user figure, and no per-device breakdown was provided.

</div>

<div class="fact-check" markdown="1">

## Verified and unconfirmed

- **Verified:** Huawei said in a statement on Tuesday that HarmonyOS, its Android-free operating system, has passed 100 million installations across smartphones, tablets and PCs, and that its ecosystem offers more than 460,000 apps and services from over 12 million registered developers — figures carried by the South China Morning Post and Xinhua, which also note the current generation followed HarmonyOS 5, introduced in October 2024, with versions 6 and 7 arriving afterwards.
- **Not yet confirmed:** All three figures come from Huawei itself; no independent measurement of installs, app counts or developer registrations is cited, and the reports do not specify the period over which the 100 million accumulated or how installations are counted (pre-installed devices versus upgrades versus active users). A separate report that HarmonyOS has overtaken iOS in China rests on a single outlet and is not corroborated by the sources above.

</div>

## Background

HarmonyOS is Huawei's in-house operating system, and the company describes it as Android-free — meaning the release in question is built to run apps made for Huawei's own runtime rather than depending on Android app compatibility, which is why app counts and developer numbers are the figures used to judge whether it can stand alone. The installation total spans smartphones, tablets and PCs, so it reflects an OS shared across several device categories rather than phones alone.

## Why it matters

For app makers targeting China, the install base makes HarmonyOS a separate platform to assess alongside Android, not an extension of it, because Huawei describes the OS as Android-free. The lack of active-user and device-mix figures means the practical addressable market for any single app remains unclear.
