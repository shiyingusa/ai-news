<div class="story-lead" markdown="1">

TSMC and Samsung Electronics have suspended some chip shipments to Chinese customers while they apply for licenses from the US Commerce Department, the Financial Times reported, citing unnamed sources. The pause follows new US export controls introduced by the department. The report does not specify which chips, which customers, or how large the affected volumes are, and neither foundry has publicly confirmed the halt.

</div>

<div class="fact-check" markdown="1">

## Verified and unconfirmed

- **Verified:** The Financial Times reported, citing unnamed sources, that TSMC and Samsung Electronics have paused some chip shipments to Chinese customers while they apply for licenses from the US Commerce Department, and that the halt follows new US export controls introduced by the department.
- **Not yet confirmed:** Neither TSMC, Samsung nor the Commerce Department has publicly confirmed the suspension; the report does not specify which chip types, process nodes or customers are affected, when the pause began, how many license applications are pending, or whether the shipments were halted voluntarily or under instruction. No independent outlet or company disclosure corroborates the pause.

</div>

## Background

US export controls run through a licensing system: a supplier needs Commerce Department approval before shipping controlled technology to a restricted buyer, and shipments can be held while an application is pending. AI Daily Radar's November 16 digest reported that Commerce issued an interim rule requiring licenses for AI chips when the hardware may provide compute to China or other restricted countries, extending controls beyond physical shipments to remote access. The Financial Times report says the foundries' halt follows newly introduced Commerce Department export controls, but it does not specify which chip types, customers or volumes are affected.

## Why it matters

Chinese buyers that rely on TSMC or Samsung for advanced or restricted-design chips should plan for delivery delays until license decisions are made, and should confirm with their suppliers which orders are actually held rather than assuming a blanket stop.

## What to watch

Watch for public statements from TSMC and Samsung confirming or denying the pause, and for Commerce Department license decisions that would let the held shipments resume.
