Germany's photovoltaic systems generated more than 100 TWh of electricity in a calendar year for the first time, according to the Fraunhofer Institute for Solar Energy Systems' Energy-Charts platform. The data showed 100.2 TWh by the morning of Nov. 27, up about 10 TWh from the 90 TWh recorded for 2025.

Of the total, 80.6 TWh was fed into the grid by plants paid under Germany's Renewable Energy Sources Act (EEG), 2.6 TWh came from other PV systems, and 16.9 TWh was consumed on site by the producers. Solar was the country's second-largest source of net generation so far this year, behind wind at 124.4 TWh and ahead of gas at 82.1 TWh; lignite (63 TWh) and hard coal (27.2 TWh) together produced less than solar.

The figures are running totals for the year to date, not a final full-year accounting.

## Verified and unconfirmed

- **Verified:** Fraunhofer ISE's Energy-Charts data, as reported by pv magazine, shows 100.2 TWh of German solar generation by the morning of Nov. 27, split into 80.6 TWh of EEG-supported grid feed-in, 2.6 TWh from other PV systems and 16.9 TWh of self-consumption, against 90 TWh for the whole of 2025.
- **Not yet confirmed:** the final full-year 2026 total, since the 100.2 TWh figure is a year-to-date reading taken in late November; the report also relies on a single data platform and does not give an independently audited breakdown, and the mid-November projection of the milestone by the solar and battery trade group BSB rested on the same Fraunhofer ISE numbers.

</div>

## Background

Germany's Renewable Energy Sources Act (EEG) is the support scheme behind most of the country's solar build-out: it pays guaranteed or auction-set rates for photovoltaic power delivered to the grid, which is why Fraunhofer ISE reports EEG-supported feed-in, other grid feed-in and self-consumption as separate lines rather than one number. The institute's public Energy-Charts platform compiles the metered generation data that this year's total and earlier annual totals are drawn from, so the milestone is measured on the same basis as previous years.

## Why it matters

Because lignite and hard coal together produced 90.2 TWh so far this year, solar has moved ahead of coal in Germany's generation mix, but the output is concentrated in sunny midday hours — the 16.9 TWh of self-consumption shows how much now sits behind the meter. Grid operators and battery developers face a growing midday surplus to absorb or curtail, and the year-on-year gain of about 10 TWh indicates the trend is continuing rather than a one-off weather effect.

## What to watch

Fraunhofer ISE's final 2026 generation figures on Energy-Charts after the year closes, which will confirm the full-year total and whether solar holds its place ahead of coal in the annual mix.
