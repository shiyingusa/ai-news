Unsealed documents in an Intel shareholder lawsuit, viewed by Wired, allegedly show the Trump administration pressuring Intel's board to accept a 10% U.S. government stake in exchange for releasing CHIPS Act funds. The suit, filed earlier this year by shareholder Richard Paisner, calls the arrangement unlawful; Intel was waiting on $8.9 billion when Commerce Department lawyer David Shapiro told its CFO not to make the "mistake of thinking that this is a negotiation" and that rejecting the offer would have "consequences that [he] cannot predict," according to internal notes cited in the documents. The complaint alleges the board "caved to the government's extortion" and says its legal advice was conflicted by outside counsel's pro bono promises to the president, which the report puts at up to $100 million in free services. The allegations have not been proven in court, and the report does not say whether Intel or the government has responded.

</div>

<div class="fact-check" markdown="1">

## Verified and unconfirmed

- **Verified:** Richard Paisner's shareholder suit challenges the U.S. government's 10% stake in Intel, and Tom's Hardware, citing Wired, reports that unsealed case documents include internal notes of Commerce Department lawyer David Shapiro warning that rejecting the offer would have "consequences"; the complaint also alleges a conflict of interest involving Intel adviser Skadden Arps (Bloomberg Law). The 10% government stake itself is confirmed (The Verge).
- **Not yet confirmed:** That Trump personally directed the pressure, that the stake was an unlawful quid pro quo for the $8.9 billion in CHIPS Act funds, and that Intel's outside counsel provided $100 million in free legal services — these rest on the complaint's allegations and Wired's review, not on a court finding or independent confirmation.

</div>

## Background

The CHIPS and Science Act, enacted in 2022, provides federal subsidies for building semiconductor plants on U.S. soil, and Intel was still waiting on $8.9 billion in awards when the arrangement took shape. In August 2025 Trump said the U.S. government should receive a 10% ownership stake in Intel, an unprecedented step for a domestic chipmaker, and the company's CEO, Lip-Bu Tan, was reported to have gone along with it. Shareholder Richard Paisner then sued Intel, arguing that trading government funds for an equity stake was unlawful; the unsealed filings now being reported come from that case.

## Why it matters

The suit is the formal legal challenge to the 10% stake, asking a court to declare unlawful the condition that tied $8.9 billion in CHIPS Act money to government ownership. For Intel, that means the board's negotiation and its outside counsel's role are now part of a public court record the company must defend.
