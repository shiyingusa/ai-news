<div class="story-lead" markdown="1">

Intel announced it will acquire Classiq, a quantum software company whose hardware-agnostic platform lets engineers design, synthesize, optimize, simulate and execute quantum algorithms at a higher level than Intel's own Quantum SDK. Intel says the deal, expected to close in the first half of 2027, would give it a complete stack from silicon spin-qubit chips and cryogenic control electronics to high-level software, and that it plans to make quantum processors using specialized 18A-Q and 18A-CC process variants.

No purchase price was disclosed, and Intel acknowledges commercial success will take time; the acquisition has not closed.

</div>

<div class="fact-check" markdown="1">

## Verified and unconfirmed

- **Verified:** Intel announced the pending acquisition of Classiq, targeted to close in the first half of 2027, and says Classiq's hardware-agnostic platform has existing enterprise customers and revenue. Intel also says more than 90% of the process equipment for its 18A-Q and 18A-CC quantum process variants is identical to that used for conventional semiconductors.
- **Not yet confirmed:** That the deal will close on that timetable, the purchase price, and Intel's claim that it will be the only company able to produce qubit chips and cryogenic control electronics at scale; Intel has not demonstrated a commercial quantum system or given a profitability date.

</div>

## Background

Classiq, founded in 2020, sells an enterprise-grade, hardware-agnostic quantum software platform that lets teams design and optimize quantum algorithms at a high abstraction level rather than hand-building circuits. Intel's own quantum work began in 2015 as a research collaboration with QuTech and produced silicon spin-qubit hardware such as the 12-qubit Tunnel Falls processor (2023) and the Horse Ridge cryogenic control chip (2020), but the company says the group remained "purely a research and development project" rather than a commercial activity.

## Why it matters

The specific gap being filled is abstraction: Intel's Quantum SDK includes a compiler, but Classiq's platform handles higher-level algorithm design, synthesis and AI-assisted optimization that Intel does not offer. For developers, the compatibility question is whether Classiq's hardware-agnostic output remains neutral across qubit vendors once it is part of an Intel end-to-end stack; the deal is not expected to close before the first half of 2027.

## What to watch

The targeted close in the first half of 2027 is the next checkpoint; before then, watch for Intel to detail how Classiq's platform will be integrated with its Quantum SDK and what happens to existing enterprise contracts.
