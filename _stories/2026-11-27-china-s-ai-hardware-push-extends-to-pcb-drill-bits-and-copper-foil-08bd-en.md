<div class="story-lead" markdown="1">

More than 20 Chinese PCB manufacturers announced major capacity expansions in the first half of 2026, and in September China's Ministry of Industry and Information Technology and National Development and Reform Commission published a five-year plan calling for advances in high-frequency, high-speed, densely layered circuit boards, copper-clad laminate, low-profile copper foil and the equipment that makes them, Tom's Hardware reported, citing SCMP. Guangdong Dtech, the world's largest PCB drill-bit maker by volume with roughly three in every ten bits made in 2025, said in its 2026 interim results that it has begun mass-producing bits for holes with aspect ratios above 40:1 and is working on 60:1, with monthly capacity potentially rising from 160 million bits in June to more than 235 million by year-end.

Defu Technology, historically a lithium-ion battery foil supplier, shipped about 700 tonnes of hyper-very-low-profile (HVLP) copper foil in September, including more than 180 tonnes of the HVLP4 grade used in AI server boards, and plans to add 50,000 tonnes of annual high-end PCB foil capacity across 2027 and 2028. The demand pressure is documented mainly through bank research rather than independent measurement: HSBC forecasts a 24% supply shortfall in PCB drill bits in 2027 and 55% annual demand growth over the next three years, while J.P. Morgan predicts a shortfall of qualified HVLP4 supply of about 700 tonnes a month in 2026, rising to 1,200 tonnes in 2027.

## Verified and unconfirmed

- **Verified:** Tom's Hardware, citing SCMP, reported that more than 20 Chinese PCB manufacturers announced expansion plans in the first half of 2026, and that China's MIIT and NDRC published a five-year plan in September targeting high-frequency, high-speed, multilayer PCBs, copper-clad laminate and low-profile copper foil; Dtech's own interim report states it has entered mass production of drill bits with aspect ratios above 40:1, and HSBC and KGI Securities research is cited for the layer counts and drilling difficulty.
- **Not yet confirmed:** the shortage forecasts (24% drill-bit shortfall in 2027, 700 to 1,200 tonnes a month of HVLP4, 36% annual growth in holes per board, a 78-layer Rubin Ultra backplane) are bank estimates, not measured outcomes; Dtech's climb to 235 million bits a month by year-end and Defu's 50,000-tonne capacity plan are company targets, and the report gives no prices, customer qualification results, or independent verification of either firm's output.

</div>

## Background

A printed circuit board is the physical layer that connects and routes signals between chips, memory and networking hardware: copper foil is bonded onto copper-clad laminate (CCL), a glass-fibre-and-resin sheet, and holes are then drilled through the stack to link the circuits. AI servers need far more of those layers than ordinary computers — HSBC estimates four to eight layers in a notebook board versus more than 20 for Nvidia's H100 generation — which turns the drills and foils that make them into scaling constraints rather than commodities. The capacity race is unfolding against continued US export controls on advanced chips: AI Daily Radar's November 19 digest reported that TSMC and Samsung had paused some chip shipments to Chinese customers while applying for US licences, according to the Financial Times.

## Why it matters

The AI supply-chain bottleneck is shifting to parts that export controls have largely ignored, and the leverage is accruing to Chinese suppliers: HSBC's shortfall forecast means drill-bit availability, not chip supply, could cap how many AI boards get built in 2027, and because Japanese and Taiwanese firms (Mitsui Kinzoku, Furukawa Electric and Co-Tech together held more than 60% of 2025 HVLP supply) still dominate the premium grades, buyers will need to qualify Defu's foil and Dtech's diamond-coated tools themselves before treating the new capacity as usable.

## What to watch

Whether Dtech actually reaches more than 235 million bits a month by year-end, which its full-year results should show, and whether J.P. Morgan's projected 700-tonne-a-month HVLP4 shortfall for 2026 persists as Defu's new foil clears customer qualification.
