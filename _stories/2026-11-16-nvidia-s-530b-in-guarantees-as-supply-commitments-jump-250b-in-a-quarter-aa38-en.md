<div class="story-lead" markdown="1">

Nvidia's fiscal second-quarter SEC filing, as parsed by SemiAnalysis and reported by Tom's Hardware, shows $530 billion in gross off-balance-sheet guarantees, up from $184 billion the prior quarter, plus $99 billion in equity investments, while supply and capacity commitments rose by nearly a quarter of a trillion dollars in a single quarter, mostly for memory. The filing breaks down as $366 billion in future commitments ($279 billion of it supply and capacity), $56 billion in additional commitments ($36 billion of AI cloud take-or-pay arrangements and $20 billion in data-center leases) and $108.5 billion in maximum gross exposure under guarantees, $105 billion of which is lease and credit support for SB Energy's PORTS Technology Campus in Ohio tied to an OpenAI affiliate.

SemiAnalysis estimates the scale of these commitments lets Nvidia pay $3.00–3.30 per gigabit of SK hynix HBM4 in 2027, versus $3.70–4.10 for first-tier customers including AMD, Amazon, Broadcom and Google. The account is second-hand reporting of SemiAnalysis and the SEC filing, and its description of Jensen Huang as Nvidia's former chief executive and Colette Kress as interim CEO is not established by the filing and should be treated as unverified.

</div>

## Verified and unconfirmed

- **Verified:** Nvidia's own SEC filing for the quarter ended July 26, 2026 reports roughly $530 billion in gross off-balance-sheet guarantees — $366 billion in future commitments (of which $279 billion is supply and capacity), $56 billion in additional commitments and $108.5 billion in maximum gross exposure under guarantees — plus $99 billion of equity investments. Nvidia's November 12, 2026 press release confirms Jensen Huang resigned and that CFO Colette Kress is interim CEO, which resolves the article's confusing reference to Huang as "former chief executive" while also calling Kress the current interim CEO.
- **Not yet confirmed:** the $184 billion prior-quarter comparison and the nearly $250 billion single-quarter rise in supply commitments are SemiAnalysis estimates, not figures the article ties to the filing; the HBM4 pricing gaps (about $3.00–3.30 per gigabit for Nvidia versus $3.70–4.10 for AMD, Amazon, Broadcom and Google in 2027), the alleged informal Nvidia–SK Group arrangement, Huang's reported August concerns about over-committing to wafers and memory, and Kress halving the Ohio/OpenAI commitment before the August 17 agreement all rest on SemiAnalysis analysis and Wall Street Journal reporting attributed to unnamed people.

</div>

## Background

Off-balance-sheet guarantees are commitments a company discloses in its SEC filings without recording them as debt on its balance sheet; they include take-or-pay arrangements, under which a buyer owes payment whether or not it ultimately uses the reserved capacity. For Nvidia, these figures show how much future demand it has locked in as fixed obligations rather than optional orders.

## Why it matters

If SemiAnalysis's pricing estimate holds, the roughly 20–25% discount Nvidia gets on 2027 HBM4 buys it a cost advantage its accelerator rivals cannot match on the same memory, and the guarantee book means Nvidia is now underwriting its own customers' ability to pay — the $105 billion Ohio lease and credit support only pays off if the OpenAI-affiliated tenant fills a campus planned for about $300 billion of Nvidia hardware. Buyers and partners should watch how much of that $530 billion sits with thinly capitalized cloud providers rather than investment-grade hyperscalers.

## What to watch

Nvidia's next quarterly SEC filing, to see whether gross guarantees keep climbing past $530 billion and whether the Ohio lease guarantee converts into installed capacity. SemiAnalysis also expects non-Nvidia customers to take over 70% of TSMC capacity in 2028, so HBM4 contract pricing reported for SK hynix's 2027 volumes will be the first check on whether Nvidia's procurement discount survives Huang's departure.
