Anthropic priced its initial public offering at $83 per share on Wednesday, according to CNBC, which cited a person familiar with the matter. That is above the $70-$78 range the company had been marketing to investors, and the report says the higher price puts the valuation above the $1.4 trillion Anthropic had previously been targeting — around $1.5 trillion on a fully diluted basis, which counts all shares and options, not just those sold in the offering.

The pricing figure and valuation rest on a single unnamed source; Anthropic has not been quoted confirming them, and the size of the offering, the total raised and the first day of trading are not established in the report.

</div>

## Verified and unconfirmed

- **Verified:** CNBC reported, citing a person familiar with the matter, that Anthropic priced its IPO at $83 per share on Wednesday, above the $70-$78 marketed range.
- **Not yet confirmed:** the resulting valuation figures ($1.4 trillion previously targeted, roughly $1.5 trillion on a fully diluted basis), since they come from the same single unnamed source and no official Anthropic or underwriter announcement is quoted; the report also leaves out the number of shares sold, total proceeds, and when the stock begins trading.

</div>

## Background

An IPO's marketed range is the price band underwriters set to gauge investor demand before shares are allocated, so pricing above it signals stronger demand than the banks had guided for. Three days before this pricing, AI Daily Radar's November 15 digest reported that CEO Dario Amodei had called buying compute Anthropic's "deepest business dilemma" on the eve of a planned record IPO, with reported commitments near $901 billion and the offering framed as a financial buffer against uncertain future revenue — context the pricing report itself does not address.

## Why it matters

Pricing above the marketed range means Anthropic raises more cash per share sold than the roadshow implied, and it sets a higher reference price for the first day of trading — investors allocated shares at $83 only come out ahead if the stock opens and holds above that level, so the debut becomes the near-term test of whether public buyers accept the $1.5 trillion figure.

## What to watch

The first trading session after listing, and whether the stock closes above or below the $83 offer price; formal confirmation of the pricing and valuation from Anthropic or its underwriters would also settle the details CNBC's single source leaves open.
