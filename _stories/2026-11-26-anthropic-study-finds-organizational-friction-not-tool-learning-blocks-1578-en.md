Anthropic examined nearly 300 AI deployments across 58 US companies and found that almost one in five still produces no business value. According to The Decoder's account of the study, the obstacles are heavy review workloads, internal bottlenecks and employee resistance rather than difficulty learning the tools, and the researchers trace the biggest hurdles to outdated company structures.

The coverage does not detail how business value was measured, how the deployments were selected, or whether the full study has been published.

## Background

The findings come from Anthropic's own "Working with AI" research, published a few days before this coverage; it is based on interviews with people at 58 companies that use Claude rather than a controlled productivity trial, and it separately reported few layoffs but reduced hiring. The study lands amid a vendor push to put AI inside tools employees already use — AI Daily Radar's November 18 digest reported Microsoft making Word, Excel and PowerPoint available inside Copilot and calling it "the new OS for work." Anthropic's interviews suggest that grafting AI onto existing roles and workflows this way is exactly where gains stay modest.

## Why it matters

For teams rolling out AI tools, the finding points away from more training and toward process work: review and approval capacity, ownership of bottlenecks, and how existing workflows absorb AI output. A deployment that adds checking work for reviewers can consume the time it saves, so the review step is where pilots most plausibly stall.
