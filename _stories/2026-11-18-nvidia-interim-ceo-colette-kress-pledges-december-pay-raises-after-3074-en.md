Nvidia held its first all-hands meeting since co-founder Jensen Huang resigned as CEO, and interim chief executive Colette Kress used it to promise staff higher pay and more equity, according to Wired. The increases are scheduled for December, well ahead of Nvidia's normal raise cycle; Kress said compensation and clearer word from the board were the most common topics in questions submitted before the meeting. The reassurance follows a letter from a director-level employee to the board and top executives warning that failing to reinstate Huang could cost the company "irreplaceable talent," and comes after the board said on the prior day's earnings call that it did not intend to bring Huang back. Huang has said he would be "honored" to return as CEO if the board asked, and reports describe a pro-Huang faction among directors.

</div>

<div class="fact-check" markdown="1">

## Verified and unconfirmed

- **Verified:** Nvidia's own press release, dated November 12, 2026, states that Jensen Huang resigned as President, CEO and a board member, that CFO Colette Kress was appointed interim CEO, and that Harvey C. Jones became the board's first-ever chair.
- **Not yet confirmed:** The compensation details rest on Wired's account of an internal all-hands — that Kress promised raises and more equity on an accelerated schedule, with the bumps set for December rather than the normal raise cycle. Also unconfirmed are the reported letter from a director-level employee warning of an exodus of "irreplaceable talent," the reported pro-Huang faction on the board, and whether Huang could be reinstated; the sources do not give the size of the raises or the equity grants.

</div>

## Background

Nvidia co-founder Jensen Huang resigned as CEO, and the board named executive vice president and chief financial officer Colette Kress as interim chief executive. On the earnings call the day before the all-hands, the board said it did not intend to reinstate Huang, even though a pro-Huang faction on the board is reportedly pushing for his return and Huang himself has said he would be "honored" to lead the company again if asked. The all-hands came after what the report describes as standout quarterly earnings, a moment when retention concerns could weigh on investors.

## Why it matters

Pulling raises and equity grants forward to December gives retention a concrete date and price: employees weighing outside offers now know what Nvidia will put on the table and when, months before the usual cycle. It also shows that with the board publicly ruling out Huang's return, compensation and board communication are the main levers Kress has to hold the staff Huang's departure put in play.

## What to watch

The higher pay and equity grants are set for December, ahead of Nvidia's normal raise cycle, so how broadly they are actually applied will be the first concrete test of Kress's reassurance. On leadership, the board said at its earnings call that it had no intention of reinstating Huang even as some directors reportedly push for a return, and Huang has said he would take the job if asked — the board's next formal statement is the clearest signal of how that question resolves.
