BYD confirmed on November 24 that it has entered exclusive negotiations to acquire Ineos Automotive's car plant in Hambach, France. If completed, the purchase would make BYD the first Chinese automaker to buy an existing European car plant, and would give it a second passenger-car factory in the EU after its Hungary site.

The plant was originally built by Mercedes for the Smart city car and later became the production home of Ineos' Grenadier, a Defender-style off-roader; the report links the move to Grenadier production shifting to the US. The companies have not disclosed a purchase price, the plant's future capacity, which BYD models would be built there, or a timeline beyond the fact that talks are exclusive.

## Verified and unconfirmed

- **Verified:** BYD itself confirmed it has entered exclusive negotiations to acquire Ineos Automotive's Hambach plant in France; Electrek reports the deal would be BYD's second EU passenger-car factory after Hungary and the first purchase of an existing European plant by a Chinese automaker.
- **Not yet confirmed:** the price, the plant's capacity, which models would be built there, and any closing date; the report also does not detail the terms under which Grenadier production would move to the US. Nothing indicates the transaction has been signed, and exclusive talks can still fall through.

</div>

## Background

The Hambach plant in eastern France was built by Mercedes-Benz to produce its Smart city car before Jim Ratcliffe's Ineos Automotive took the site over and used it to build the Grenadier, a combustion-engined off-roader styled after the original Land Rover Defender. Ineos is moving Grenadier production to the United States, which would leave the French site without its main product. Selling an existing plant lets a buyer skip the multi-year permitting and construction timeline of a greenfield factory.

## Why it matters

Buying an existing plant lets BYD skip the multi-year permitting and construction cycle of a greenfield site and inherit a trained workforce and supplier base in France, a faster route to local production than building from scratch. For European suppliers and Hambach's current employees, the practical question is whether output continues at similar volumes and on what timeline, none of which has been disclosed.

## What to watch

Watch for a definitive sale agreement replacing the exclusive talks, and for any BYD statement on investment, capacity or models for Hambach once terms are settled.
