Agile Robots has raised a $500 million Series F round at an $8.5 billion valuation, bringing total funding to $2 billion. SoftBank Group led the round, with NVIDIA, Hitachi Group and T.Capital also participating. The company, a 2018 spin-off from the German Aerospace Center, says it will use the money to expand production capacity and its presence in the United States, building on a Fürstenfeldbruck, Germany, plant opened earlier in 2026 with capacity for up to 10,000 legged and wheeled humanoid robots a year. Agile Robots says it has deployed 20,000 industrial robots to date for automotive, consumer electronics, logistics and other customers.

## Verified and unconfirmed

- **Verified:** The Robot Report reports a $500 million Series F led by SoftBank Group, with NVIDIA, Hitachi Group and T.Capital participating, at an $8.5 billion valuation and $2 billion total funding; Agile Robots says it has deployed 20,000 industrial robots.
- **Not yet confirmed:** The round's terms, revenue, current factory utilization and a U.S. site or timeline are not disclosed; the 10,000-unit annual figure is stated capacity at an existing German plant, not current output or capacity added by this round.

</div>

## Background

Agile Robots was founded in 2018 as a spin-off of the German Aerospace Center (DLR) and has since deployed 20,000 industrial robots with automotive, consumer-electronics, healthcare and logistics customers, selling robotic arms, mobile robots, hands and its Agile ONE humanoid along with the software and AI models behind them. Earlier in 2026 the company opened a production site in Fürstenfeldbruck near Munich designed to build up to 10,000 legged and wheeled humanoid robots a year — the kind of capacity this round is meant to extend, including in Germany and the United States. It also operates a portfolio of subsidiaries, including the AI robotics research platform Franka Robotics and Krause Automation, an industrial deployment specialist recently acquired from thyssenkrupp AG.

## Why it matters

For industrial customers, the near-term effect is supply and geography, not a new robot capability: Agile Robots says the round will fund production capacity and U.S. expansion, and its existing German plant is rated for up to 10,000 humanoids annually. Buyers evaluating deployments should ask for current output and delivery schedules rather than treating that figure as available inventory.

## What to watch

Watch for Agile Robots to name a U.S. production site and start date for the capacity this round is meant to fund; neither has been disclosed. Also watch the actual output from the Fürstenfeldbruck plant against its stated 10,000-unit annual capacity.
