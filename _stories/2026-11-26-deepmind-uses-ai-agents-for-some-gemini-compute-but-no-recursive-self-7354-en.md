Google DeepMind is using AI agents to run some of its own experiments for upcoming Gemini models, according to research chief Koray Kavukcuoglu, as reported by The Decoder. Kavukcuoglu said the lab remains far from recursive self-improvement — the idea that an AI system could autonomously improve itself in a compounding loop. For oversight, DeepMind deliberately leaves the agents' internal chains of thought untrained so that humans can review that reasoning. He also said he measures progress toward AGI by whether everyday users trust the systems rather than by benchmark scores.

## Verified and unconfirmed

- **Verified:** DeepMind research chief Koray Kavukcuoglu, as reported by The Decoder, said the lab uses AI agents to run part of its compute for Gemini experiments, that it intentionally does not train on the agents' chains of thought so they can be reviewed, and that the lab is still far from recursive self-improvement.
- **Not yet confirmed:** how much of DeepMind's compute the agents control, which agent systems are involved, and when this practice began; no independent verification or technical paper is cited, and the distance-from-recursive-self-improvement judgment is Kavukcuoglu's own assessment.

</div>

## Background

Recursive self-improvement describes a closed loop in which a model continuously upgrades itself; DeepMind research VP Koray Kavukcuoglu addressed that prospect in remarks on a company podcast reported by The Decoder. The oversight mechanism he described hinges on chain of thought—the intermediate reasoning a model produces before answering or acting—which DeepMind deliberately leaves untrained so reviewers can inspect agents' natural-language intentions before execution.

## Why it matters

DeepMind's reviewability claim rests on a specific choice — not training on the agents' reasoning traces — so teams that copy the practice gain a human-readable audit path only as long as they keep chains of thought out of training data; those that train on reasoning traces give up that inspection route.
