<div class="story-lead" markdown="1">

The California Department of Motor Vehicles barred Waymo's robotaxis from operating on state freeways, determining that the company's highway operations pose "an imminent hazard to public safety," according to a San Francisco Business Times report aggregated by Techmeme. The restriction is indefinite and stays in place until Waymo can show it has taken "effective corrective actions" to fix the cited deficiencies. The trigger was a viral video in which a Waymo vehicle failed to yield to highway patrol; Electrek described the footage as a Waymo attempting to pass a police blockade. Waymo responded by pulling its vehicles from highways nationwide, not just in California, so freeway routes are suspended in every market where it had been running them.

</div>

<div class="fact-check" markdown="1">

## Verified and unconfirmed

- **Verified:** The California DMV issued the freeway restriction and cited an "imminent hazard to public safety," and Waymo has stopped highway operations nationwide, per the San Francisco Business Times and Electrek reports aggregated by Techmeme.
- **Not yet confirmed:** No primary DMV order or permit filing is in the supplied material, so the precise deficiencies cited beyond the video, the formal legal form of the restriction, and how many vehicles or routes it covers are unstated. The two accounts also describe the footage differently — "failing to yield to the highway patrol" versus "trying to pass a police blockade" — and neither the video's date nor whether other state regulators have acted is confirmed.

</div>

## Background

Waymo's driverless rides in California operate under state permits, and freeway driving is a separate, more recently granted permission from the California Department of Motor Vehicles rather than part of its original city-street service. The DMV can act immediately when it judges a permit holder's operations to be an "imminent hazard to public safety" — the finding it cited here — with restrictions lifted only once the company shows it has taken effective corrective actions.

## Why it matters

Riders in Waymo's freeway-serving markets lose highway trips — notably airport runs and cross-city routes that rely on freeways — and will be routed onto surface streets or see those trips cancelled until the DMV lifts the order. Waymo's path back is procedural rather than timed: it must document corrective actions to the DMV's satisfaction, so operators and riders planning freeway-dependent trips should assume the suspension lasts beyond the next few days.

## What to watch

Watch for Waymo's submission of corrective actions to the California DMV and any subsequent DMV decision on reinstating freeway operations; no date for either step has been announced.
