<div class="story-lead" markdown="1">

The US Department of Energy has issued a seventh emergency order forcing Michigan's J.H. Campbell coal plant to keep running, 534 days after its planned retirement, according to Electrek. Electrek reports the orders have cost ratepayers $303 million so far and contributed to rising electricity prices. The outlet's headline says the latest order came even after a court found the practice illegal, but the source excerpt supplied here gives no details of that ruling — which court, when, or what it held.

</div>

<div class="fact-check" markdown="1">

## Verified and unconfirmed

- **Verified:** Electrek reports that DOE issued a seventh emergency order keeping J.H. Campbell open 534 days past its retirement date, and that the orders have cost ratepayers $303 million.
- **Not yet confirmed:** the court ruling referenced in the headline — neither the court, the date, nor the scope of the decision is described in the source text — and the $303 million figure and the link to higher electricity prices, which are Electrek's own accounting rather than an independent audit. DOE's stated justification for the seventh order is also not included.

</div>

## Background

The J.H. Campbell coal plant in West Olive, Michigan, was scheduled to shut down in May 2025, but the Department of Energy began issuing "emergency" orders to keep it operating. On September 11, 2026, the U.S. Court of Appeals for the D.C. Circuit vacated the first of those orders, with Michigan's attorney general describing it as arbitrary and illegal and the court finding DOE lacked authority to compel operation past the closure date. The order described in this story is a later one issued after that ruling.

## Why it matters

Ratepayers, not the plant operator, are absorbing the $303 million Electrek attributes to these extensions, and a seventh consecutive emergency order signals that the mechanism can be renewed indefinitely rather than treated as a short-term reliability stopgap — a precedent other plant owners weighing retirement dates and grid operators planning reserve margins now have to factor in.

## What to watch

Whether DOE issues an eighth extension when the current order lapses, and whether a court blocks the order while it is in force.
