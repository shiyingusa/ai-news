<div class="story-lead" markdown="1">

BMW has announced the iX5, its first fully electric vehicle built in the United States, claiming an estimated range of up to 456 miles along with ultra-fast charging and "Neue Klasse" technology — BMW's next-generation EV platform. The announcement, as reported by Electrek, is a preview rather than a full launch: no price, production start date, battery capacity or charging power in kilowatts was disclosed. The 456-mile figure is BMW's own estimate, not an EPA-certified rating, so the number that will appear on US window stickers is still unknown.

</div>

<div class="fact-check" markdown="1">

## Verified and unconfirmed

- **Verified:** Electrek reports that BMW says the iX5 is its first fully electric US-built vehicle, with an estimated range of up to 456 miles, ultra-fast charging and Neue Klasse technology.
- **Not yet confirmed:** price, production timing, battery size, charging power and the EPA-certified range all rest on BMW's announcement and were not provided. The source also contradicts itself on range — the article text and headline say 456 miles, while the page's own URL slug says 435 miles — so neither figure should be treated as final.

</div>

## Background

AI Daily Radar's November 16 digest reported that BMW had opened its battery assembly plant in Woodruff, South Carolina, and begun series production of high-voltage battery packs for the iX5, the model expected to lead BMW's U.S.-made electric lineup; that report noted the launch date and price were still undisclosed. "Neue Klasse" is BMW's name for its next-generation electric model line and the technology bundle that comes with it, which the iX5 is said to carry.

## Why it matters

For US buyers, the figure that will actually count is the EPA-certified range BMW must publish before the iX5 goes on sale, since that is what appears on the window sticker and what comparisons with rival EVs use; the 456-mile estimate is a manufacturer claim until then. Similarly, the "ultra-fast charging" promise can only be judged once BMW states a charging power in kilowatts and which charger networks support it.

## What to watch

Watch for BMW to publish EPA-certified range, pricing and a production start date for the iX5 at its US plant, which will settle the 456-versus-435-mile discrepancy.
