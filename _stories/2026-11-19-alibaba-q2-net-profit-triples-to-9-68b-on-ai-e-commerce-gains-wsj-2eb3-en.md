<div class="story-lead" markdown="1">

Alibaba reported fiscal second-quarter revenue of about $40.7B, up 10% year over year and above analyst estimates, with net profit roughly tripling to about $9.68B against an estimate of about $3.6B, the Wall Street Journal reported. The company attributed the improvement to better profitability in its AI and e-commerce businesses. Alibaba is also preparing a Hong Kong listing for its chip unit, T-Head. The report did not break out segment-level AI revenue or specify the size or timing of the T-Head listing.

</div>

<div class="fact-check" markdown="1">

## Verified and unconfirmed

- **Verified:** Alibaba reported fiscal second-quarter revenue up 10% year over year to about $40.7B, ahead of the roughly $40.41B consensus of three analysts surveyed by Zacks, and profit roughly tripled from a year earlier, per the Wall Street Journal's Tracy Qu and an Associated Press earnings snapshot. The Wall Street Journal also reported that Alibaba is preparing a Hong Kong listing for its chip unit, T-Head.
- **Not yet confirmed:** The two accounts differ on the profit figure — the Journal puts net profit at about $9.68B, while the AP snapshot cites $9.63B — and the Journal's claim that this beat a roughly $3.6B analyst estimate is not corroborated by the AP report, which gives no comparable profit consensus. Neither account breaks out how much of the profit gain came from AI versus e-commerce, and no timetable, size or valuation has been disclosed for the T-Head listing.

</div>

## Background

Alibaba's AI exposure runs mainly through its cloud unit, which rents out compute and serves its in-house Qwen models; AI Daily Radar's November 16 digest reported the launch of the flagship Qwen4-Max, a 2.4-trillion-parameter model that activates 71 billion parameters per token and was positioned just behind Kimi K3.1 Pro on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index. Separately, the Wall Street Journal report notes Alibaba is preparing a Hong Kong listing for T-Head, its in-house chip design unit, a move that would give the semiconductor arm its own public market valuation.

## Why it matters

The profit beat gives investors a concrete profitability signal from Alibaba's AI and e-commerce operations rather than only spending plans, and the planned Hong Kong listing of chip unit T-Head would create a separately traded vehicle through which the market could value that semiconductor business. Because the report gives no size, valuation or timetable for the T-Head offering, it remains a stated intention rather than a scheduled transaction.

## What to watch

Alibaba's chip unit T-Head has confidentially filed for a Hong Kong IPO and the Hong Kong stock exchange has confirmed Alibaba can proceed, so the next checkpoints are a public prospectus and offer terms; Bloomberg reported the debut could raise at least $2 billion.
