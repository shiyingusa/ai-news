Apollo is reportedly arranging $40 billion in chip financing for SpaceX through a special-purpose vehicle — a legal entity created for the transaction — so the GPUs stay off SpaceX's balance sheet and SpaceX rents them rather than owning them, Semafor reported, citing unnamed sources. The report says the financing could come to market as early as this week.

The number of GPUs, where they would be deployed and the rental terms were not disclosed, and neither Apollo nor SpaceX has confirmed the arrangement.

</div>

<div class="fact-check" markdown="1">

## Verified and unconfirmed

- **Verified:** Semafor's November 18 report, citing people it did not name, says Apollo is arranging $40 billion in chip financing for SpaceX through a special-purpose vehicle — a separate legal entity that holds the GPUs — so SpaceX can rent the hardware and keep the debt off its balance sheet, with the financing potentially coming to market as early as this week. TechCrunch separately reported that SpaceX disclosed in an October 26 regulatory filing a binding commitment for roughly $40 billion in financing for AI chips and data-center equipment, which the company called the "largest committed GPU financing."
- **Not yet confirmed:** Semafor's account rests entirely on unnamed sources; Apollo has not confirmed the arrangement, and no GPU count or model, rental terms, interest rate, investor list, or final closing date has been disclosed. Bloomberg reported on October 23 that the off-balance-sheet structure had been complicated by AI market turmoil and rising sector borrowing costs, and the sources do not reconcile that with the expectation of a deal this week.

</div>

## Background

A special-purpose vehicle is a separate legal entity created to hold one specific asset and the debt used to buy it, so the operator rents the asset instead of owning it and the associated obligation does not sit on its own balance sheet. The structure has become common in AI compute deals because GPUs are expensive, depreciate quickly and can be pledged as collateral to lenders. Semafor reported that the Apollo-arranged financing could reach markets as early as this week.

## Why it matters

The structure would let SpaceX add large-scale GPU capacity without recording the $40 billion as debt, a distinction that matters for a company already funding capital-intensive launch and satellite programs. Because the terms are undisclosed, it is not possible to say what SpaceX would pay to rent the chips or who bears the risk if the hardware loses value.

## What to watch

Whether the financing comes to market as early as this week, as Semafor reports, and whether Apollo or SpaceX confirms the vehicle's size and terms.
