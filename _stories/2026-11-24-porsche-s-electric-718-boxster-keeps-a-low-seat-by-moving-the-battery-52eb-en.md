Ars Technica drove a prototype of Porsche's first all-electric sports car, the 718 Boxster, which the company says will be fully revealed next year. The packaging is the distinctive part: instead of the skateboard-style pack under the floor used by nearly all modern EVs — which the report says raises seating by roughly 3 inches — Porsche put the battery behind the driver, powering a single motor that turns the rear wheels. The result, per the reviewer, is a seating position low enough to be eye-level with a Tesla Model Y door handle in traffic, preserving the mid-engined feel of the combustion Boxster. This is a manufacturer-hosted prototype drive, not a launch: no power, range, price, or on-sale date were disclosed.

## Verified and unconfirmed

- **Verified:** Ars Technica drove a 718 Boxster EV prototype on canyon roads near Los Angeles, and Porsche has placed the battery pack behind the driver with a single motor driving the rear wheels, the outlet reported.
- **Not yet confirmed:** power output, range, charging speed, price, and exact production timing — all absent from the report — and the handling and seating impressions come from a manufacturer-hosted drive of a prototype rather than an independent measurement.

</div>

## Why the seating position is the story

The 718 Boxster it replaces is a mid-engined sports car, with the engine carried behind the driver rather than under the hood. Most modern EVs instead stack cells into a flat "skateboard" pack spanning the floor, which raises the driver's hip point by roughly 3 inches — a trade-off that matters little in an SUV but flattens the sensation of speed in a low sports car. Porsche's solution for its first all-electric sports car was to move the pack behind the driver, which is what preserves the Boxster's characteristic seating position.

## Why it matters

For anyone shopping an electric sports car, this shows the battery layout, not just the motor, decides whether an EV can sit and feel like one: a floor-mounted pack adds height, while Porsche's behind-the-driver pack keeps the center of gravity and the driver low. Buyers should treat the driving impressions as provisional until Porsche publishes production specifications and an independent outlet tests a customer car.

## What to watch

Porsche says the full reveal of the production 718 Boxster comes next year; that event should bring the power, battery capacity, range, and pricing figures the prototype drive left out.
