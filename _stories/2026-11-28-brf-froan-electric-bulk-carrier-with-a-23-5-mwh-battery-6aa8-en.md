Electrek reports that a newly built bulk carrier, the BRF Froan, carries a 23,500 kWh (23.5 MWh) battery — presented as the largest pack yet fitted to an electric bulk cargo ship. The vessel is described as carrying coal, sugar and other bulk cargoes, the heavy, high-volume trade that has been hardest to electrify.

The report gives no battery supplier, route, range, charging arrangement or delivery date, and the "world's largest" description is the outlet's characterization rather than an independently verified record. No figures on how the ship is charged or how far it can sail on a single charge are included.

## Verified and unconfirmed

- **Verified:** Electrek reports that the BRF Froan is a new electric bulk carrier fitted with a 23,500 kWh (23.5 MWh) battery, and describes it as the largest such ship in the bulk cargo segment.
- **Not yet confirmed:** The battery manufacturer, the ship's operator, its routes and service area, charging method and power, range, and any independent confirmation of the "world's largest" claim — the report does not state a delivery or commissioning date either.

</div>

## Background

BRF Froan is a 129.9-meter bulk carrier — a ship built for unpackaged cargo such as coal, grain or sugar — that is heading to Norway to have its battery installed, according to Interesting Engineering. Operator Berge Rederi says shore-power facilities will be installed at both ports the vessel serves, letting it recharge there; the company estimates annual shore charging at 5,133,000 kWh.

## Why it matters

For operators and ports, a pack this size moves the binding constraint ashore: a 23.5 MWh battery has to be replenished at the berths the ship serves, so the port's charging capacity and the ship's schedule become as decisive as the battery itself. Charterers evaluating electric bulk tonnage will need to confirm charging arrangements at both ends of a route before treating the vessel as a drop-in substitute for conventional fuel.
