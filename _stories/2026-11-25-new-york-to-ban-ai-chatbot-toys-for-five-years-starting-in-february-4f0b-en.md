New York Gov. Kathy Hochul signed a bill that prohibits the manufacture, exchange, distribution and sale of toys powered by AI chatbots in the state for five years, with the ban set to start in February. The report ties the measure to new Common Sense Media research that it says reveals safety failures, including children's exposure to inappropriate content and risks to emotional development. ## Verified and unconfirmed

- **Verified:** NBC News reported that New York Governor Kathy Hochul signed a bill on November 25 barring the manufacture, exchange, distribution and sale of AI-chatbot-powered toys in the state, with the five-year ban taking effect in February; the same report says California Governor Gavin Newsom signed a four-year ban in September.
- **Not yet confirmed:** The safety evidence cited for the ban comes from Common Sense Media testing that secondary write-ups summarize as finding 27% of AI toy responses inappropriate for children, with no AI toys recommended for ages 5 and under — the underlying report itself was not reviewed here, and the outlets do not detail enforcement, penalties, or which toy categories are exempt.

</div>

## Background

AI chatbot toys are toys built around a conversational AI that children can speak with. New York's five-year ban follows California, where Governor Gavin Newsom signed a four-year ban in September, NBC News reported.

## Why it matters

Toy makers and retailers will have to stop manufacturing, distributing or selling AI chatbot toys in New York from February, and product teams may need to disable chatbot features or restrict those products for the state. California's four-year ban, signed in September, adds a second major-market compliance requirement.

## What to watch

The ban is scheduled to take effect in February. Watch for how New York enforces it and whether retailers pull AI chatbot toys from shelves.
