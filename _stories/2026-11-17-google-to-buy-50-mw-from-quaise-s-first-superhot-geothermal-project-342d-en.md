Quaise Energy, a Houston-based geothermal startup, announced Tuesday that Google has agreed to purchase 50 megawatts of carbon-free power from its first commercial project, called Project Obsidian, near Newberry in central Oregon. The deal gives the company a marquee first customer for its superhot geothermal approach, which targets far hotter rock than conventional geothermal plants. The project is still under development: the announcement carried no power-delivery date, no contract price, and no evidence yet that the technology performs at commercial scale.

</div>

<div class="fact-check" markdown="1">

## Verified and unconfirmed

- **Verified:** Quaise Energy said Google agreed to buy 50 MW of carbon-free power from Project Obsidian, its first commercial superhot geothermal project, near Newberry, Oregon, per the company's Tuesday announcement.
- **Not yet confirmed:** When the plant would deliver power, what Google will pay, whether the project has secured financing or permits, and whether superhot geothermal can produce power at commercial scale — none of this was disclosed, and the reporting rests on Quaise's announcement rather than independent operational data.

</div>

## Background

Superhot geothermal targets rock at 300–500°C, far hotter than the heated water that conventional geothermal plants tap, which is the temperature range Quaise says its plant is designed to exploit. Quaise unveiled Project Obsidian in April 2026 as what it calls the world's first superhot geothermal power plant, to be built on Bureau of Land Management land south of Newberry National Volcanic Monument in central Oregon. Local reporting on the Google agreement says electricity from the project is expected to begin flowing as early as 2030.

## Why it matters

For data-center operators shopping for round-the-clock carbon-free power, this is a contract for capacity that does not yet exist, so Google's 50 MW should be read as a bet on Quaise's technology rather than a secured supply; buyers evaluating similar deals should watch for construction milestones and performance guarantees before treating superhot geothermal as a deliverable option.

## What to watch

Watch for Quaise to publish a construction and financing schedule for Project Obsidian with a first-power date, and for Google to confirm the 50 MW purchase in its own energy procurement disclosures.
