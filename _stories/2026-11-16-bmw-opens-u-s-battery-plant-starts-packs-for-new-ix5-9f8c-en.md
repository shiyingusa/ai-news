BMW has opened its Woodruff, South Carolina, battery assembly plant and started series production of high-voltage battery packs for the new iX5 electric SUV. The iX5 is expected to be first in line among BMW's U.S.-made Neue Klasse models as the automaker ramps up vehicle production. The supplied report does not give pack output, cell suppliers, or the iX5's launch date and price.

## Background

A battery pack is not the same thing as a battery cell: cells are the individual energy-storing units, while the pack is the assembled high-voltage unit — cells, modules, cooling, electronics and housing — that bolts into the car. BMW's Plant Woodruff in South Carolina performs that assembly step, feeding packs into the company's U.S. vehicle production rather than shipping finished batteries in from abroad. The packs are destined for the iX5, the electric SUV built on BMW's Neue Klasse generation of models.

## Why it matters

For U.S. buyers waiting on the iX5, the Woodruff line is now the single point of supply for its battery packs: BMW says the plant assembles the high-voltage batteries for the new iX5, so a slow ramp or quality hold there translates directly into delayed customer deliveries rather than being absorbed by packs shipped from Europe. BMW's own pre-production testing began roughly a year before series assembly, which suggests the immediate constraint is line rate, not pack design.

## What to watch

The next checkpoint is the start of iX5 vehicle assembly at Plant Spartanburg, roughly 15 miles from Woodruff, which BMW has scheduled for late 2026, with customer deliveries reported to follow in early 2027. The first U.S. delivery figures and any announced iX5 pricing will show whether the Woodruff pack line is running at the volume BMW intends.
