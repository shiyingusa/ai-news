Aachen-based battery recycling startup Cylib is acquiring BASF's battery recycling business, a deal that covers two sites in Schwarzheide (Brandenburg) and BASF's North American recycling operations. The report describes a signed agreement, with Cylib co-CEOs Gideon Schwich and Lilian Schwich pictured at the signing alongside BASF battery materials president Daniel Schönfelder and senior vice president Lydie Derebreu.

The report does not give a purchase price, the recycling capacity involved, or the conditions and timing for closing.

## Verified and unconfirmed

- **Verified:** electrive reports that Aachen-based battery recycling startup Cylib is taking over BASF's battery recycling business under a signed agreement, including two facilities in Schwarzheide in Brandenburg and the North America business.
- **Not yet confirmed:** The purchase price and other deal terms, closing conditions or any required regulatory approval, the timeline for completing the transfer, and the effect on recycling capacity and staff at the Schwarzheide and North American sites; the report gives none of these, and no separate statement from BASF or Cylib was available to check the details against.

</div>

## Background

Battery recycling is the step in the EV supply chain that turns used or scrap cells back into usable battery materials, so control of recycling capacity affects who can supply recycled content to cell makers. BASF's recycling business being sold consists of two facilities in Schwarzheide, Brandenburg, and the North American operation; the buyer, Cylib, is an Aachen-based startup, making this a transfer from BASF to a younger specialist.

## Why it matters

The deal would turn Cylib from an Aachen pilot-plant startup that raised a €55 million Series A into the operator of BASF's existing recycling footprint, including two facilities in Schwarzheide and the North American business. For European automakers and cell makers, that moves an established black-mass supply source to a much younger company; no deal terms, closing conditions or capacity figures were disclosed, so contract continuity and future throughput at the BASF sites remain unresolved.

## What to watch

Watch for antitrust clearance and formal closing of the transaction, and for disclosure of the purchase price and the capacity Cylib gains at Schwarzheide and in North America, none of which the report provided.
