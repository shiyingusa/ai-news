Dongfeng Nissan opened the early order book on its NX7 plug-in hybrid on November 13, promising up to 320 km (~198 miles) of all-electric range from a 40 kWh lithium iron phosphate (LFP) battery at a price of roughly $20,000. Both figures come from the company as reported by Electrek; the item does not say which trim gets the full 320 km or the $20,000 price, which test cycle the range figure uses, or when deliveries start. A 40 kWh pack is large for a plug-in hybrid — comparable to the batteries in some small battery-electric cars — which is what makes the range claim plausible rather than incremental.

</div>

## Verified and unconfirmed

- **Verified:** Dongfeng Nissan opened early ordering for the NX7 PHEV, according to Electrek's report of the company's announcement, with a stated 40 kWh LFP battery and a target of up to 320 km of electric range.
- **Not yet confirmed:** the ~198-mile range and ~$20,000 price are manufacturer claims with no independent testing, and the sources leave out the test cycle used, the trim breakdown, the price of the long-range version, and the delivery date.

</div>

## Background

A plug-in hybrid (PHEV) pairs a battery and electric motor with a gasoline engine, so it can cover daily driving on grid electricity and fall back on fuel for longer trips — which makes battery size the main driver of how much driving is actually electric. LFP (lithium iron phosphate) is the cheaper, longer-lasting but less energy-dense battery chemistry, so combining a 40 kWh LFP pack with a roughly $20,000 price is the specific pairing Dongfeng Nissan is claiming. The Electrek report does not specify which test cycle the 320 km figure is measured on, so the "up to" range should be read as a manufacturer claim rather than a measured result.

## Why it matters

If the claim holds in production form, a 40 kWh battery at this price would let most Chinese buyers cover daily driving on electricity alone while keeping a gas engine for longer trips — a use pattern that depends on reliable home or workplace charging, since the large pack makes the car heavier and slower to charge from a standard outlet than a typical plug-in hybrid.

## What to watch

Watch for Dongfeng Nissan's full launch pricing and trim sheet, which should clarify whether the ~$20,000 price and 320 km range apply to the same version, followed by the first customer deliveries and independent range tests.
