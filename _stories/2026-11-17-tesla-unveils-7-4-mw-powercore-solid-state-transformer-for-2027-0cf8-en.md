<div class="story-lead" markdown="1">

Tesla has unveiled Powercore, a US-made solid-state medium-voltage transformer that also performs the inverter and breaker functions in a single unit. Tesla rates it at 7.4 MW (9 MVA) continuous at 34.5 kV, with grid-forming capability supporting up to 10 MVA of peak power — grid-forming meaning it can establish and hold voltage and frequency on a grid rather than merely following an existing one.

Tesla says the unit is scheduled to begin operating in 2027, the same year Heron Power — the startup founded by former Tesla top engineer Drew Baglino — plans to start volume production of its competing solid-state transformer, the Heron Link. The announcement covered no pricing, customers, or shipped units.

</div>

<div class="fact-check" markdown="1">

## Verified and unconfirmed

- **Verified:** Electrek reports that Tesla unveiled Powercore, describing it as one unit combining a solid-state medium-voltage transformer, inverter, and breaker; Tesla’s stated ratings are 7.4 MW (9 MVA) continuous at 34.5 kV and grid-forming support up to 10 MVA peak, with US manufacturing and a 2027 operating target.
- **Not yet confirmed:** Tesla has not shown shipped units, customer deployments, pricing, or independent test results; the 7.4 MW/9 MVA and 10 MVA figures are Tesla’s own claims, the exact 2027 start date is a schedule rather than a demonstrated milestone, and Heron Power’s 2027 volume-production target for Heron Link is likewise a company plan.

</div>

## Background

A conventional medium-voltage transformer steps voltage up or down with iron cores operating at the grid's 50 or 60 Hz; a solid-state transformer does the same job using power electronics that switch at far higher frequencies, which shrinks the hardware and lets the unit also absorb the inverter and breaker roles Tesla says Powercore handles. The competitive framing comes from Heron Power, which Electrek describes as founded by Tesla's former top engineer Drew Baglino and which plans to begin volume production of its own solid-state transformer, the Heron Link, in the same 2027 window Tesla has given for Powercore to start operating.

## Why it matters

For developers planning grid-scale storage or charging sites, Powercore collapses three pieces of equipment — transformer, inverter and breaker — into one enclosure, and its 800 V to 2,000 V DC input window lines up with DC-coupled battery architectures rather than requiring a separate step-up stage. The practical catch is timing: Tesla has given no price, no customer deployments and a 2027 operating date, while Heron Power targets volume production of the competing Heron Link the same year, so procurement teams cannot yet compare the two on cost or delivery and will be sizing interconnection plans around a product nobody has run on a live grid.

## What to watch

Whether Tesla names first Powercore deployment sites or publishes a firm delivery schedule ahead of its stated 2027 start of operation, and whether Heron Power confirms volume production of the Heron Link in the same year.
