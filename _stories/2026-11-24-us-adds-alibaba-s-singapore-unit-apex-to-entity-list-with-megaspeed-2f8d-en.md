The US Commerce Department added Apex Enterprise Solutions, a Singapore-based subsidiary of Alibaba Group, to its Entity List, alongside Singapore-linked distributor Megaspeed and five affiliates, Bloomberg reported, citing a filing. The listing puts the entities on a roster that requires US suppliers to obtain a license before exporting controlled goods to them, including advanced AI chips — a constraint on Alibaba's AI compute plans routed through Singapore.

The report did not give an effective date, the full list of items covered, or any comment from Commerce or Alibaba. The action came the same day Singapore Prime Minister Lawrence Wong said Singapore should not be used as a route to circumvent US chip export controls, as authorities probe Megaspeed's purchases of Nvidia chips.

</div>

## Verified and unconfirmed

- **Verified:** Bloomberg reports that a US Commerce Department filing adds Alibaba's Singaporean subsidiary Apex Enterprise Solutions, Megaspeed and five affiliates to the Entity List.
- **Not yet confirmed:** The specific conduct or rationale behind Apex's listing, the identities of all five affiliates, and the listing's effective date; Alibaba says Apex never diverted chips covered by US export restrictions and will take legal steps to protect its rights.

</div>

## Background

The Entity List is the US Commerce Department’s roster of foreign parties that need a license before receiving items subject to US export controls, including many advanced chips. The addition of Apex Enterprise Solutions (Singapore) Private Limited requires a US license for exports, reexports and transfers to the company of items covered by US rules, and Alibaba said on Nov. 24 that the designation was erroneous.

## Why it matters

For Alibaba, a Singapore-based procurement channel is now covered by US export licensing, so any American supplier shipping controlled chips or equipment to Apex needs a license first; distributors and cloud partners dealing with Apex, Megaspeed or the five affiliates should verify the listing before fulfilling orders, since Entity List obligations attach to the US exporter rather than the buyer.

## What to watch

Watch for Commerce's formal Federal Register notice naming the entities and stating the license requirements and effective date, and for the outcome of Singapore's probe into Megaspeed's Nvidia chip purchases.
