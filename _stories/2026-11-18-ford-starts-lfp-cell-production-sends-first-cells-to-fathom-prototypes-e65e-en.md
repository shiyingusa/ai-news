<div class="story-lead" markdown="1">

Ford has begun producing lithium iron phosphate (LFP) battery cells at its BlueOval Battery Park Michigan plant and is shipping the first production-grade cells to Kentucky for testing in prototypes of the planned $30,000 Fathom electric pickup. The cells are for prototype validation, not customer vehicles, so the Fathom's final price and launch timing remain unconfirmed. LFP is a lower-cost battery chemistry that Ford plans to use first in the affordable truck.

</div>

<div class="fact-check" markdown="1">

## Verified and unconfirmed

- **Verified:** Electrek reported that Ford's BlueOval Battery Park in Michigan is now producing its first LFP (lithium iron phosphate) cells and shipping them to Kentucky to power Fathom prototypes; an earlier Electrek report said the plant had begun assembling LFP prismatic cells for Ford's upcoming EV lineup.
- **Not yet confirmed:** How those prototype cells perform — capacity, range, charging — and when the Fathom actually launches and at what price. Electrek cites a $30,000 target, while other coverage cites $28,350, so the sticker is not settled; plant output volume and the extent of the CATL technology licensing behind the cells rest on outside reporting rather than a Ford disclosure.

</div>

## Background

LFP (lithium iron phosphate) cells are a battery chemistry that avoids nickel and cobalt, making packs cheaper and generally longer-lived than the nickel-rich chemistries many automakers used in their first EVs, at the cost of lower energy density — which is why the chemistry is increasingly used in lower-priced, shorter-range models. Ford built the BlueOval Battery Park plant in Michigan to make those cells itself rather than buy them from suppliers. The plant's first output is going into Fathom prototypes for testing, not yet into trucks customers can buy.

## Why it matters

Ford can now test the Fathom's battery pack with cells from its own Michigan line, giving the $30,000 program a domestic battery source to validate. The report does not show that the cells have passed vehicle validation or that the plant can supply customer volumes.

## What to watch

Watch for Ford to confirm a production date and final price for the Fathom, and for evidence that customer vehicles—not only prototypes—receive the Michigan-made LFP cells.
