<div class="story-lead" markdown="1">

Google is extending "Personal Intelligence" in Search's AI Mode — the personalization layer that lets the assistant draw on a user's own context — to Google Drive, Google Wallet and the Gemini app, TechCrunch reported. The point, per the report, is to exploit the data users already keep inside Google's ecosystem, so AI Mode answers can reflect documents and payment or pass information rather than only public web content.

The report does not say how the data is accessed, which countries or account types get the capability first, or what privacy controls let users disconnect a source. It therefore establishes the direction of the rollout but not the scope of availability.

</div>

<div class="fact-check" markdown="1">

## Verified and unconfirmed

- **Verified:** Google announced on Monday that Personal Intelligence in Search's AI Mode now extends to Google Drive, Google Wallet and the Gemini app, per TechCrunch and Google's own blog post, which says users must opt in to connect those services.
- **Not yet confirmed:** The rollout sequence and reach — Android Authority reports Wallet support arrives first, following Gemini connectivity earlier this month, with Drive and the Gemini app connections trailing — but Google's post and the coverage do not specify a full availability date, countries, or which account tiers qualify; they also leave out what data each integration can read, how long it is retained, and whether any independent privacy or security review has been done.

</div>

## Background

Personal Intelligence is Google's opt-in layer that lets AI Mode, the conversational search mode, answer using a signed-in user's own Google data rather than only the public web. According to Google's announcement and Digital Trends, the Wallet connection is read-only and arrives first, with Drive and the Gemini app following in the weeks ahead, and each connected app can be switched off at any time.

## Why it matters

For people who store documents in Drive and cards, passes or tickets in Wallet, the useful question shifts from "what can the assistant find" to "what of mine is it allowed to read" — so the account-level permissions attached to those Google products become the practical control surface, and users should check which sources they are willing to connect before relying on personalized answers.

## What to watch

Watch Google's own support documentation for the account types and countries where Personal Intelligence in AI Mode covers Drive, Wallet and the Gemini app, and for whether users can selectively disconnect each source.
