<div class="story-lead" markdown="1">

Wired reported that CreditNirvana, a platform that uses AI to run debt collection calls, carried a pair of serious vulnerabilities that would have given attackers access to 6.6 million accounts and 315,000 call recordings. The exposed data also included emails, texts and credit reports, according to the report.

The report describes the flaws and the scale of the data they reached but does not state that anyone actually exploited them, nor does it name who discovered the vulnerabilities or whether affected consumers have been notified.

</div>

<div class="fact-check" markdown="1">

## Verified and unconfirmed

- **Verified:** WIRED reported on November 18 that two vulnerabilities in the India-based AI debt collection platform CreditNirvana exposed roughly 6.6 million accounts and about 315,000 call recordings from its automated call center; security researcher Eaton Zveare is credited with the finding, and secondary reports (MLQ.ai, Credit & Collection News) repeat the same figures and date.
- **Not yet confirmed:** There is no evidence in the reports that anyone actually exploited the flaws or copied data — the exposure of emails, texts, call audio and credit reports is described as what attackers "would have" been able to reach. One secondary report says both flaws were fixed, but the available reporting does not give a clear remediation date, and no public statement from CreditNirvana itself is included.

</div>

## Background

CreditNirvana, a Perfios-owned company based in Bengaluru, sells an agentic AI platform for debt portfolio management and collections to banks, non-bank lenders, housing finance companies and asset reconstruction companies — clients whose workflows require storing borrower credit reports, communications and call recordings in one system. The company has marketed its Maestro platform as automating the entire debt-collection lifecycle, from predicting delinquencies to running recovery outreach, which is why a single platform holds both account records and recorded calls.

## Why it matters

Debt collection is a case where AI voice systems concentrate data by design: to negotiate and log calls, the platform holds call recordings alongside credit reports, emails and texts for millions of people, most of whom never chose to do business with the vendor itself. That means a flaw at one collection-technology provider can put records of people whose only connection to it is a debt they owed, and consumers have no direct way to check whether their file sat in the exposed set until the company or a regulator says so.

## What to watch

Watch for a statement from CreditNirvana and for breach-notification filings confirming the number of people affected and whether the flaws were accessed before being fixed.
