A leaked draft Ohio bill would expand the state's eminent-domain eligibility list to include an agency holding a certificate of environmental compatibility and public need from the power siting board for an electric transmission line and associated facilities or a gas pipeline, according to WCPO Cincinnati's reporting. The draft could also allow data centers to be classified as public utilities, which legal experts have warned could let a transmission developer condemn land for a data center or a facility that powers one. No bill number, sponsor, formal introduction, or legislative schedule has been reported.

## Background

Eminent domain is the government's power to take private land for public use as long as the owner is compensated, and in Ohio that authority currently reaches only entities owned or operated by municipal corporations, electric cooperatives, and agencies holding a certificate of public convenience and necessity from the Federal Energy Regulatory Commission. The leaked draft would add developers holding a certificate of environmental compatibility and public need from the state Power Siting Board for an electric transmission line or gas pipeline. It lands amid local resistance: AI Daily Radar's November 17 digest reported that Ohio voters passed midterm ballot measures restricting data-center development, putting three proposed Amazon projects within a plan worth more than $17 billion at risk.

## Why it matters

If enacted, the provision would give transmission and pipeline developers a potential route to acquire private land for projects that can serve data centers, leaving landowners in the path of a certified project facing condemnation. Developers would still need a power siting board certificate, so how that board treats data-center-linked applications would determine the provision's reach.

## What to watch

Watch for the bill to be formally introduced in the Ohio legislature, which would produce a bill number and sponsor, and for any committee hearing or Ohio Power Siting Board proceeding that tests the data-center language.
