<div class="story-lead" markdown="1">

BMW has begun assembling the iX5 electric SUV in the United States, the first EV the German automaker has built there, according to InsideEVs. The outlet reports the line is currently producing about 70 iX5s per day and describes that figure as a starting point rather than the intended full rate.

The report does not give the factory location, a price, or a date for when U.S.-built iX5s reach customers, and BMW has not been quoted with its own production figure.

</div>

<div class="fact-check" markdown="1">

## Verified and unconfirmed

- **Verified:** InsideEVs reports that BMW has started U.S. production of the iX5 and that it is the company's first U.S.-made EV, with output of roughly 70 vehicles per day.
- **Not yet confirmed:** The daily rate rests on the outlet's reporting rather than a BMW statement, and the report leaves out the plant, the planned full production rate, pricing, and when the vehicles go on sale.

</div>

## Background

BMW's Spartanburg, South Carolina, plant, which assembles the X3, X4, X5, X6, X7 and XM, is where the iX5 becomes the brand's first fully electric model built in the United States. AI Daily Radar's November 16 digest reported that BMW had opened a battery assembly plant in Woodruff, South Carolina, and started series production of high-voltage packs for the iX5 — the local supply step that comes before assembling complete vehicles.

## Why it matters

For U.S. buyers, the reported pace is the practical constraint: at about 70 vehicles a day, the iX5 stays a low-volume product during its ramp, so local assembly is unlikely to translate into wide dealer availability in the near term. Anyone tracking the model should treat the daily rate, not the start of production, as the number that determines how quickly cars reach showrooms.
