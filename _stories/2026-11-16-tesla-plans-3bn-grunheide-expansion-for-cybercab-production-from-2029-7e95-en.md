Tesla said on November 16 that it will invest €3 billion (about $3.3 billion) to expand Gigafactory Grünheide near Berlin, its only vehicle plant in Europe. The money covers a new production building roughly twice the size of the existing main hall plus supporting infrastructure such as an additional wastewater treatment plant; Tesla designs it to build up to one million Cybercab robotaxis a year once fully scaled, alongside continued Model Y output.

Tesla plans to file the building permit before the end of 2026, start construction in the first half of 2027 and begin Cybercab production in 2029. Separately, plant manager André Thierig said on November 11 that full 4680-format battery cell production at the site — using Tesla's dry-electrode process, targeting up to 18 GWh a year — is on track for mid-2027; electrodes are already made there but are currently shipped to Texas for cell assembly and returned.

All of this is an announced plan with future dates, not existing capacity.

## Verified and unconfirmed

- **Verified:** Tesla said on 16 November that it will invest €3 billion (about $3.3 billion) in expanding Gigafactory Grünheide, including a new production building roughly twice the size of the existing main hall, an additional wastewater treatment plant, and Cybercab manufacturing from 2029, with construction targeted for the first half of 2027 (electrive, drawing on Teslarati and Tagesspiegel). The site currently employs about 12,600 people and is ramping Model Y output toward 7,500 vehicles a week; plant manager André Thierig said on 11 November that full 4680 cell assembly on site is on track for mid-2027.
- **Not yet confirmed:** The €3 billion figure, the design capacity of up to one million Cybercabs a year and the 18 GWh annual cell target are all Tesla's own projections, relayed through Teslarati rather than an independent audit. The building permit application has not yet been filed — Tesla says it will submit it before the end of 2026, under a 2023 expansion application — so no approval exists, and the article reports the filing plan via a single Tagesspiegel report. Also unaddressed: whether European regulators will permit a steering-wheel-free robotaxi to operate, and any independent check on cost or timeline.

</div>

## Background

Grünheide is Tesla's only vehicle plant in Europe, and its battery work there has so far stopped short of building complete cells: electrodes produced on site are shipped to Texas for assembly, and Tesla's larger 4680-format cylindrical cell only reached commercial production at Giga Texas, appearing in a single Model Y trim in Europe and some U.S. configurations [tool-2-1]. An earlier, smaller expansion at the site had already set a target of 18 GWh of 4680 output for the first half of 2027, confirmed by plant manager André Thierig [tool-2-3]. The new €3bn package places the Cybercab vehicle programme alongside that unfinished cell plan rather than replacing it.

## Why it matters

If the cell line starts as scheduled, Grünheide would become the first plant in Europe to produce everything from battery cells to finished vehicles under one roof, lifting the European content of its Model Ys to roughly 90 percent and cutting the site's reliance on transatlantic and Chinese supply chains. The practical gating item is permitting: the Cybercab building depends on a new application to be filed before the end of 2026, building on a 2023 expansion application for one million vehicles a year overall, so approval timing — not the announced budget — determines whether the 2027 construction start holds.

## What to watch

Whether Tesla files the Grünheide building permit by the end of 2026 as stated, and how Brandenburg's approval process then schedules the H1 2027 construction start and the mid-2027 target for on-site 4680 cell production.
