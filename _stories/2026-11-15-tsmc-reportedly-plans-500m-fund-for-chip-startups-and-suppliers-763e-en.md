<div class="story-lead" markdown="1">

Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing Co. plans to create a $500 million venture fund to invest in promising chip startups, suppliers and other upstream partners, Nikkei Asia reported in an interview-based story. The fund is framed by TSMC as a way to back what one quoted executive called "the future's Nvidia" — that is, to hold stakes in the companies that could become the next major chip designer or equipment maker. The report gives no timeline for the fund's launch, no indication of whether it has been formally approved by TSMC's board, and no detail on investment size per company or the fund's structure. The $500 million figure is small next to TSMC's capital spending on fabs, so this reads as an ecosystem and relationship-building vehicle rather than a capacity commitment.

</div>

<div class="fact-check" markdown="1">

## Verified and unconfirmed

- **Verified:** Nikkei Asia (Cheng Ting-Fang) reports, in an interview-based article, that TSMC plans to create a $500 million venture fund to support promising chip startups, suppliers and other upstream partners, and quotes the company saying it "also want[s] to support the future's Nvidia."
- **Not yet confirmed:** TSMC has not been shown to have issued its own announcement of the fund; the $500 million figure, the fund's legal structure, investment targets, timeline and first deals rest on the Nikkei report alone, and the available excerpt is truncated, so the full scope of the quoted remarks is unknown.

</div>

## Background

TSMC is the world's largest contract chipmaker, or foundry: companies such as Nvidia and Apple design chips but outsource fabrication to TSMC's plants. Nvidia's rise as the leading supplier of AI accelerators made it one of TSMC's most important customers, which is the relationship the phrasing about backing "the future's Nvidia" points to — a startup that would design chips and need a foundry to build them.

## Why it matters

For chip startups and TSMC's upstream suppliers, the fund would add a strategic investor with deep visibility into manufacturing — one that can offer process know-how and priority access to capacity alongside cash, which specialist financial investors cannot. The caveat for anyone hoping to tap it: because the report describes only an intention, with no stated fund size per deal, eligibility criteria or investment mandate beyond "upstream partners," startups cannot yet plan around it until TSMC confirms the vehicle and its terms.

## What to watch

Watch for TSMC to formally confirm the fund's existence, size and structure — for example in a board resolution or earnings-call remarks — and for the first named portfolio companies, which would show whether the mandate leans toward design startups or toward equipment and materials suppliers.
