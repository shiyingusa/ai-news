Uber Eats customers in Spring, Texas, a Houston suburb, can now receive food orders by Zipline drone, according to TechCrunch's Kirsten Korosec. TechCrunch describes it as the first commercial launch under the Uber–Zipline arrangement, with dozens of additional cities planned to follow.

The two companies have set a target of one million drone deliveries per day by the end of 2029 — a stated goal, not a demonstrated volume. The report does not specify delivery fees, service hours, the size of the coverage zone, or how many orders have actually been flown.

## Background

In August 2026, Uber and Zipline announced a strategic partnership that targeted one million drone deliveries per day by the end of 2029, with Uber taking a strategic investment in Zipline and the two saying service would begin later that year in markets where Zipline already flies, including Dallas and Houston, before expanding into dozens of US cities. Zipline is a drone-logistics company that has previously run delivery operations in those Texas markets. The Spring launch is the first commercial service under that partnership, with Dallas named as the next city.

## Why it matters

For Spring residents, drone delivery is now a selectable way to receive an Uber Eats order rather than a demo — but only inside whatever delivery radius Zipline and Uber have defined, so customers elsewhere in the Houston area and in other markets still have no access. The practical test is whether the service holds up through weather, airspace restrictions and order volume before Uber and Zipline commit to additional cities.

## What to watch

Watch for the first named expansion city and any published delivery counts from Spring, which would show whether the 2029 volume target has a credible ramp behind it.
