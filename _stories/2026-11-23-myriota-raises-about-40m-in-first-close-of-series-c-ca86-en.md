Australia-based Myriota, which sells low-cost, low-power satellite connectivity for industrial IoT devices, raised roughly $40 million in a first close of its Series C round, the Wall Street Journal's Rhiannon Hoyle reported. The company said the money will go toward hiring staff and scaling its satellite constellation and operations. The report gives no investor names, no valuation, and no total target for the round, so the $40 million is a partial close rather than a completed raise; sensors in the field connect directly to Myriota's satellites rather than to terrestrial networks.

</div>

<div class="fact-check" markdown="1">

## Verified and unconfirmed

- **Verified:** Myriota announced on 24 November 2026 that it closed the first tranche of its Series C, raising AU$60 million (about US$40 million), led by Main Sequence Ventures, Hostplus and the National Reconstruction Fund Corporation, with Funds SA joining as a new investor, according to the company's own announcement; Myriota says the money will scale its satellite constellation and fund hiring and expansion, and Dealroom puts its cumulative funding above AU$160 million (US$107.5 million).
- **Not yet confirmed:** The final size of the round — the company says the first close came ahead of schedule and the round has yet to close, so the total remains unknown; SmartCompany reports a "$50 million raise" with AU$25 million from the NRFC, a figure that conflicts with the AU$60 million first close and that no source reconciles; the WSJ framing of Myriota as helping "bridge the gap between AI and the real world" is the outlet's characterization, and the figure of 100 new jobs rests on a single report.

</div>

## Background

Satellite IoT connects sensors in places with no cellular coverage — mines, farms, remote pipelines — by passing small amounts of data through overhead satellites instead of ground towers. Myriota, founded in Adelaide in 2014 by Alex Grant and David Haley, runs a constellation of pico-satellites (very small satellites) and sells satellite-only or hybrid satellite-cellular links for industrial devices, per its own site; Tracxn's company profile puts its funding before this round at about $80.3M across six rounds. The new money is a "first close" — an initial tranche of a round that later investors can top up before it is declared final.
