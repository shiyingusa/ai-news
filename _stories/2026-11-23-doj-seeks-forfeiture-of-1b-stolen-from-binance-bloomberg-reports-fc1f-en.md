<div class="story-lead" markdown="1">

The US Department of Justice is seeking forfeiture of more than $1 billion in cryptocurrency that hackers linked to North Korea stole from Binance Holdings, according to a court filing reported by Bloomberg on Monday. The filing is a legal request, not a final ruling or a confirmed recovery of the funds. The report does not say whether the cryptocurrency has been seized, where it is held, or when a court will act.

</div>

<div class="fact-check" markdown="1">

## Verified and unconfirmed

- **Verified:** Bloomberg reported Monday that a court filing shows the DOJ seeking forfeiture of more than $1 billion in cryptocurrency stolen from Binance by hackers linked to North Korea.
- **Not yet confirmed:** The filing's exact legal basis, the precise amount, whether the funds have been located or seized, and the court's response are not established in the report; no independent confirmation or ruling was cited.

</div>

## Background

Forfeiture is a legal process in which the government asks a court to transfer ownership of assets it says are proceeds of crime; it is a filing, not a criminal conviction, and the sums named in such complaints are what prosecutors claim rather than money already recovered. The action follows an earlier enforcement push against the same threat: AI Daily Radar's November 15 digest reported that the FBI and Justice Department had disrupted a North Korean social-engineering campaign they named FaceStealer, seizing cloud infrastructure holding 900 victim videos, with the FBI attributing roughly $400 million in stolen cryptocurrency to that operation. Monday's filing targets a larger sum, more than $1 billion, tied specifically to a hack of Binance.

## Why it matters

For Binance and its users, the filing creates an active US legal claim over more than $1 billion of stolen crypto, but it does not establish that the exchange or its customers will recover any assets. The report also does not say whether DOJ has located or seized the funds.

## What to watch

Watch the court docket for a ruling on the forfeiture request and for any DOJ statement confirming whether the cryptocurrency has been seized or recovered.
