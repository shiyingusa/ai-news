<div class="story-lead" markdown="1">

Tidal will ban music made entirely by AI starting December 9, removing its existing AI-labeled catalog — reported at 27 million to 29 million tracks — and expecting to reject 80,000 to 85,000 uploads a day, according to its music lead. The policy applies only to fully AI-generated music; artists can still use AI tools as long as the final track contains human elements. Tidal says AI-made tracks accounted for 43% of uploads over the past 90 days and more than 60% of new uploads on November 6, and that 96% of AI-labeled tracks received zero plays in that period.

</div>

<div class="fact-check" markdown="1">

## Verified and unconfirmed

- **Verified:** Tidal announced that it will ban fully AI-generated music on December 9 and says such tracks made up 43% of uploads over the previous 90 days and more than 60% of new uploads on November 6; it also says the ban covers only fully AI-generated music, not human-made tracks that use AI tools.
- **Not yet confirmed:** The exact deletion figure is inconsistent across reports — 27 million in Ars Technica versus about 29 million in The Verge — and Tidal's estimates of 80,000 to 85,000 daily rejections and a 96% zero-play share, along with how it will detect fully AI-generated tracks, have not been independently verified.

</div>

## Background

Tidal introduced its first AI music policy in July 2026, labeling and demonetizing tracks identified as entirely AI-generated while continuing to allow AI tools in music with human elements. The December 9th change keeps that human-versus-fully-AI distinction but replaces labeling with removal from the catalog.

## Why it matters

Anyone distributing fully AI-generated tracks through Tidal should plan for removal or rejection from December 9, while releases that use AI tools alongside human contributions remain allowed under the policy.

## What to watch

December 9, when the ban takes effect, and whether Tidal publishes enforcement or deletion numbers that match its pre-ban estimates.
