<div class="story-lead" markdown="1">

An unusually large supply of Nvidia GPUs, including thousands of B300 data-center chips, has reached the market after the U.S. restricted Chinese companies' remote access to AI chips, CNBC reported, citing three people familiar with the matter. Nvidia chip sellers are now seeking new buyers for the hardware, according to the report.

The account rests on unnamed sources, and the report gives no specifics on total volume, pricing, or which measure triggered the shift; it also does not include on-record confirmation from Nvidia or U.S. officials.

</div>

<div class="fact-check" markdown="1">

## Verified and unconfirmed

- **Verified:** CNBC published the report on Nov. 25, attributing it to three people it did not name; according to that account, Nvidia GPU sellers are seeking new buyers and an unusually large volume of the hardware, including thousands of B300s, reached the market after the U.S. tightened China-linked remote access.
- **Not yet confirmed:** Nvidia has not confirmed the volume, the B300 count or the link to the remote-access rule, and the report leaves out pricing, seller identities, buyer locations and whether any sale has closed.

</div>

## Background

AI Daily Radar's November 16 digest reported that the US Commerce Department issued an interim rule requiring export licenses for AI chips when the hardware may provide compute to China or other restricted countries, extending controls beyond physical shipments to remote access, with Bloomberg reporting the rule targeted access from Singapore, Malaysia, Thailand, Indonesia and Brazil; the available reporting did not specify an effective date or license criteria. The November 25 CNBC report describes sellers of Nvidia hardware seeking new buyers, activity that follows that tightening of remote-access rules. AI Daily Radar's November 24 digest separately reported that Commerce added Alibaba's Singapore-based unit Apex Enterprise Solutions and distributor Megaspeed to the Entity List, a listing that requires US suppliers to obtain a license before exporting controlled goods such as advanced AI chips to them.

## Why it matters

Buyers outside China may find more B300 systems offered on the secondary market, but they should verify each unit's provenance and export eligibility before purchasing: B300s have already been found inside a Chinese state-owned carrier's network, and US rules now pair remote-access limits with stronger due-diligence requirements aimed at chip smuggling.
