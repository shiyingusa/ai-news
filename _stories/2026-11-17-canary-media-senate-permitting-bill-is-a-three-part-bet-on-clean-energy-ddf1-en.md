## Background

The bill Canary Media is analyzing is the Bipartisan American Affordability and Jobs Act, introduced on September 30, 2026 by Senators Capito, Lee, Whitehouse and Heinrich; a "Manager's Amendment" released November 7, 2026 revised the text, and the legislation is still moving through Congress. Its premise is that federal environmental reviews — chiefly those under the National Environmental Policy Act (NEPA), the 1970 law requiring agencies to assess the environmental effects of major projects — can stretch for years, delaying energy and transmission projects as electricity demand rises. The Senate sponsors say the measure would give permitted projects certainty that they keep their permits absent extraordinary circumstances, violations of law or a court order, alongside targeted reforms for specific energy projects.

## Why it matters

Clean-energy developers would gain a new federal lever: under the bill text, the Energy Secretary could, at FERC's request, designate transmission projects as in the "national interest," though the practical effect on siting timelines remains unclear. Passage would likely also require making it easier for states to approve fossil-fuel permits, so project sponsors cannot yet treat faster reviews as a settled planning assumption.

## What to watch

Watch whether the Senate follows its key procedural vote to advance the bipartisan permitting bill with a final passage vote, which would send the measure to the House. Also watch whether Senate leaders keep the bill in the queue once the Ratepayer Protection Act and other pending legislation are resolved.
