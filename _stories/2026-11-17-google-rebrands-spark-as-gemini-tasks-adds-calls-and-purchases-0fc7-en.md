<div class="story-lead" markdown="1">

Google is rebranding its Spark AI agent as Gemini Tasks and making it free, with the mode powered by the new Gemini 4 Hydrogen model, according to The Verge. Tasks can create meal plans, manage calendars, and search email for recurring expenses.

For US users, the expansion adds agentic actions: calling businesses to compare prices and check inventory, buying items with Google Pay, and signing into accounts supported by Google Password Manager, plus assigning tasks by emailing gemini@google.com or by text.

</div>

<div class="fact-check" markdown="1">

## Verified and unconfirmed

- **Verified:** The Verge, citing Google's announcement, reports the Spark-to-Tasks rename, free access, the Gemini 4 Hydrogen model, and US availability of the call, Google Pay purchase and Password Manager sign-in capabilities.
- **Not yet confirmed:** Independent evidence that Tasks completes calls, logins and purchases reliably; the exact rollout date, availability outside the US, and any spending or account-access limits are not given.

</div>

## Background

Tasks is the new name for Spark, the agent mode that previously sat behind a paid Google AI Pro subscription; 9to5Google reports the renamed feature is now free for users in six countries, and Unite.AI notes that Google's product page applies an age restriction to the free tier. The agentic actions run on Gemini 4 Hydrogen, a model Google introduced roughly five days earlier and described as a fast, cost-efficient model built for reliable agentic workflows and everyday tasks.

## Why it matters

For US Gemini users, Tasks moves the assistant from answering to acting, so the practical boundary is what Google Pay and Google Password Manager expose. Before delegating a purchase or login, users should check which payment methods and accounts are saved there, because those are the funds and credentials the agent can use.

## What to watch

Watch Google's Gemini help center for the exact US rollout timing, any expansion beyond the US, and published limits on spending or supported sites; independent tests of call and purchase success rates would be the next check.
