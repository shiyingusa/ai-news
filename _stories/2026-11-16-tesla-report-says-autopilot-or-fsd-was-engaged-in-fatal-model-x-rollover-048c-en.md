Tesla's own report to federal regulators says Autopilot or "Full Self-Driving" was engaged when a Model X veered off Golden Gate Parkway in Naples, Florida, hit a traffic signal pole and overturned in September, killing a 50-year-old woman riding as a passenger, Electrek reports. Tesla flagged the crash itself through its telematics and says it has the car's event data recorder and video.

The public was told only that a car "veered off the road." Electrek frames the case as the latest in its investigation into fatal Tesla crashes it says are hidden in the company's NHTSA data; the source does not say whether NHTSA has opened a defect investigation or what, if anything, Tesla attributes the crash to.

## Verified and unconfirmed

- **Verified:** Tesla’s report to federal regulators, as reported by Electrek, states that Autopilot or Full Self-Driving was engaged when a Model X struck a traffic signal pole and overturned in Naples, Florida, in September, killing a 50-year-old woman passenger; Tesla also says it has the vehicle’s event data recorder and video.
- **Not yet confirmed:** Which system—Autopilot or FSD—was active and whether it was controlling the vehicle at impact, what caused the Model X to leave the road, and what the EDR and video show; those details rest on Tesla’s account and have not been independently established or publicly released.

</div>

## Background

Under a standing general order, U.S. automakers must notify NHTSA when a vehicle with an advanced driver-assistance system such as Autopilot or FSD is involved in a crash, and those filings feed the agency's public ADAS crash data. Electrek places this case in its September 1 investigation arguing that fatal Tesla crashes are difficult to identify in that data, noting that the public account here described only a car that "veered off the road."

## Why it matters

This crash enters NHTSA's public record only because Tesla's telematics flagged it and the company filed under the Standing General Order, which requires manufacturers to report crashes involving automated driving systems or SAE Level 2 driver assistance like Autopilot and FSD. The initial public account said only that the car "veered off the road," so without that filing, researchers and regulators counting ADAS-involved fatalities would not know the system was engaged — and a third-party tracker (Tesorb) alleges Tesla has in some cases submitted these reports months past the order's 1-to-5-day deadline, which would limit the data's usefulness for spotting patterns. The incident also falls within NHTSA's existing defect investigation opened in March 2026 into FSD behavior in degraded roadway conditions, the venue where Tesla's event data recorder and video from this Model X would carry weight.
