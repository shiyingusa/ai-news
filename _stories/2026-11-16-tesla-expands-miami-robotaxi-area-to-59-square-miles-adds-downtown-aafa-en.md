<div class="story-lead" markdown="1">

Tesla has nearly doubled the size of its Miami robotaxi service area to 59 square miles, added Downtown Miami to the coverage zone, and begun running rides 24 hours a day, according to a report by Not a Tesla App. The change means riders within the enlarged zone can now request a driverless Tesla at any hour, where previously coverage was smaller and time-limited.

The figures and the 24/7 schedule come from the outlet's reporting rather than a published Tesla statement, and no trip volumes, fleet size, or safety data were disclosed. Tesla's robotaxi service remains a limited-market offering confined to geofenced areas rather than a generally available product.

</div>

<div class="fact-check" markdown="1">

## Verified and unconfirmed

- **Verified:** Tesla's Miami robotaxi service map now extends east from the airport area to Downtown Miami and Biscayne Bay, according to reports citing the map itself (gettesla, Not a Tesla App). Robotaxi Tracker, which tracks the service, says Tesla has run public robotaxi rides in a limited western Miami-Dade zone since July 3, 2026, booked through the Tesla Robotaxi app and with no safety monitor on board.
- **Not yet confirmed:** The 59-square-mile figure and the roughly 31-square-mile starting point are Robotaxi Tracker's own map measurements, not Tesla's numbers — Tesla has not published an area figure. The claim of 24/7 availability rests on the outlet reports rather than a Tesla statement, and no fleet size, ride volume, safety or incident data, or regulatory approval details were provided; Miami Beach reportedly remains outside the service map.

</div>

## Background

A robotaxi service area is a geofence: the mapped zone in which a company has cleared its autonomous vehicles to pick up and drop off riders, so the square-mile figure describes coverage, not safety or performance. The item supplies no earlier Miami number or Tesla statement, so "nearly doubled" and the shift to 24/7 rides rest on the outlet's characterization of growth to 59 square miles.

## Why it matters

For riders in Miami, the practical change is boundary and clock: trips that start or end Downtown now fall inside the service zone, and late-night or early-morning requests are no longer outside operating hours — so anyone planning to rely on the service should check the current in-app coverage map before assuming a pickup point qualifies.

## What to watch

Watch for Tesla to confirm the 59-square-mile figure and the 24/7 schedule itself, or to publish an updated Miami coverage map; the absence of such confirmation would leave the expansion resting on a single secondary report.
