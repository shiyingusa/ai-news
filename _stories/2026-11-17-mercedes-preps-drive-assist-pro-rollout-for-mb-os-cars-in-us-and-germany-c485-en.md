<div class="story-lead" markdown="1">

Mercedes-Benz says it is getting ready to start rolling out Drive Assist Pro, its "collaborative" partially assisted driving system, to customers whose cars run the new MB.OS operating system in the US and Germany. Ars Technica drove the system at a Mercedes-hosted event in San Francisco and, in its headline assessment, found it handled complex traffic well; the publication had previously ridden along with an earlier demo. Drive Assist Pro is a Level 2 system, meaning it controls steering, acceleration and braking together while the human driver remains responsible for situational awareness and any consequences of losing it. The source gives no launch date, pricing, or list of which MB.OS models get the feature first.

</div>

<div class="fact-check" markdown="1">

## Verified and unconfirmed

- **Verified:** Mercedes-Benz is preparing to roll out Drive Assist Pro to US and German customers with MB.OS vehicles, and Ars Technica was given a behind-the-wheel demonstration of the system, per the article.
- **Not yet confirmed:** the rollout date, price, the specific models and trims that will receive the feature, and any independent measurement of its performance — the drive took place on a manufacturer-funded trip with flights and accommodation provided by Mercedes.

</div>

## Background

Drive Assist Pro sits in the automotive industry's "Level 2" tier of driver assistance: the car handles steering, acceleration and braking together, but the person behind the wheel stays responsible for watching the road — the category Tesla popularised with Autopilot and that lane-keeping systems have occupied since Toyota offered one in 2004. Mercedes positions it inside the broader MB.DRIVE portfolio, alongside a new Hands-free Assist for motorways and a Proactive Lane Change Assist, which the company says are coming to Germany and the US with availability varying by model.

## Why it matters

Because Drive Assist Pro is tied to MB.OS, the feature effectively splits Mercedes' fleet: only cars built on the newer software and electronics architecture can get it, so owners of older models should not expect a software update to add it. Drivers using it also stay legally and practically in charge — it is Level 2, not a hands-off system — so the practical benefit is reduced fatigue on long or congested drives rather than any transfer of responsibility to the car.

## What to watch

Watch for Mercedes to name the start date and the first MB.OS models and markets that receive Drive Assist Pro; neither has been disclosed. Independent hands-on testing outside a manufacturer-hosted event would be the first real check on the system's traffic behavior.
