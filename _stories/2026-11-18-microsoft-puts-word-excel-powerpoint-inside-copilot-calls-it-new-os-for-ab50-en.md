At its Ignite keynote, Microsoft announced general availability of Word, Excel, and PowerPoint inside Copilot, with CEO Satya Nadella calling Copilot "the new OS for work." Copilot users can create documents, spreadsheets, and presentations directly in Copilot, and the files remain editable in the corresponding Office apps.

Microsoft describes the shift as an inversion of the old model: agents drive the work, Office apps become plug-in components, and an organizational knowledge layer supplies context from email, meetings, calendars, chats, and files. Pricing, licensing, and regional availability were not specified.

</div>

## Verified and unconfirmed

- **Verified:** Ars Technica, reporting from Microsoft's Ignite keynote, says CEO Satya Nadella announced that Word, Excel and PowerPoint are now generally available inside Copilot, and that files created there stay editable in the standalone Office apps.
- **Not yet confirmed:** The "new OS for work" and "frontier ecosystem" framing is Microsoft's own positioning; the source gives no pricing, licensing terms, rollout dates or independent test of how well the agent-driven document creation works.

</div>

## Background

Until now, Copilot has worked much like Clippy did: an assistant layered on top of an app such as Word, which remained the main event. Microsoft's Ignite framing reverses that hierarchy, making Word, Excel and PowerPoint plugin components that an agent calls on as needed, with context supplied by an organizational knowledge layer that spans email, meetings, calendars, chats and files. Files created inside Copilot remain editable in their respective Office applications.

## Why it matters

For organizations using Copilot, work can start in Copilot rather than in individual Office apps, while the files it produces stay editable in Word, Excel, and PowerPoint. The practical governance question is which email, meeting, calendar, chat, and file data the organizational knowledge layer is allowed to draw on.

## What to watch

The next check for IT buyers is Microsoft's pricing, licensing, and regional-availability documentation for the Copilot-hosted Office apps, which the Ignite announcement did not provide.
