<div class="story-lead" markdown="1">

Ukraine's military used its own airborne communications network — drones carrying mesh radios that act as relay nodes — to guide missile and strike-drone attacks on the Kerch Strait Bridge on the nights of November 15 and 16, avoiding the military-grade Starlink satellite service, the Financial Times reported. According to an Institute for the Study of War update cited in accounts of the strike, the attacks collapsed one of the two steel arches supporting the bridge's roadway for automobile traffic; Russian sources claimed several hundred Ukrainian drones first overwhelmed air defenses, clearing the way for remote-controlled missile strikes that included Ukraine's homegrown Flamingo cruise missile. The relay method adapts a Russian battlefield technique for supporting long-range drone and missile strikes on Ukrainian cities. The reporting does not say how many relay drones were used, what radio range or latency the network has, or independently confirm that no Starlink link was used for guidance.

</div>

<div class="fact-check" markdown="1">

## Verified and unconfirmed

- **Verified:** The Financial Times reported that Ukraine used drones fitted with mesh radios as airborne relay nodes to extend the communications link between operators and remotely piloted missiles during the Nov. 15–16 Kerch Strait Bridge strike, and that the operation did not rely on Starshield, the military version of Starlink; the Institute for the Study of War assessed that the strike collapsed one of the bridge’s two steel roadway arches. Telegraph and RBC Ukraine reports likewise describe a drone-relayed independent network, with RBC calling the drones “flying modems.”
- **Not yet confirmed:** Whether Starlink or Starshield was actually disrupted during this strike—the FT headline says disruptions occurred “including during” the bridge attack, while the FT and Ars accounts say Ukraine deliberately avoided Starshield; also unverified are Russian claims that several hundred drones overwhelmed air defenses, the number, range and bandwidth of the relay drones, and any independent damage assessment beyond ISW’s.

</div>

## Background

Starlink terminals have been central to Ukrainian drone and artillery operations, but SpaceX restricts service to Ukrainian-held territory, which limits how far remotely piloted weapons can be guided into Russian-held or Russian territory. The relay technique reported here — drones carrying mesh radios, each acting as a node that extends the link between an operator and a missile or strike drone — was first used on the battlefield by Russia to support its own long-range attacks on Ukrainian cities, according to the Financial Times.

## Why it matters

For Ukraine's drone and missile units, the practical change is that long-range strike packages can be steered beyond line of sight without a satellite service that a commercial provider can restrict or switch off. The trade-off is that the relay chain becomes the dependency: reach now depends on keeping enough mesh-radio drones airborne between operators and the weapons, and every additional hop sits between the operator and the target.

## What to watch

Watch how quickly the damaged road span of the Kerch Bridge returns to service, and whether Ukraine's forces use the relay-drone method again in later long-range strikes — the clearest test of whether this is a one-off adaptation or a standing part of Ukrainian operations.
