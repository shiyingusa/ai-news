A Dutch court has ordered ten BYD subsidiaries, in interim proceedings, to stop building and selling the Ti7 in the EU, ruling that the model looks too similar to the JLR Defender. The order covers the company's EU operations rather than a single market, meaning the model cannot be produced or sold there while the case continues.

The main trial on the design claim is still pending, so the injunction is provisional rather than a final finding of infringement. No court document or filing was published with the report, and the ruling's duration and any appeal by BYD were not stated.

</div>

## Verified and unconfirmed

- **Verified:** A Dutch court, ruling in interim proceedings, ordered ten BYD subsidiaries to stop building and selling the Ti7 in the EU after finding the model looks too similar to JLR's Defender, electrive reported; the Guardian reported the ban takes effect one week after the ruling and that the court noted BYD "currently has no concrete plans to place the Ti 7 on the market in the EU." Automotive News likewise reported that the court found the Ti7 infringes the Defender's design.
- **Not yet confirmed:** No court document or judgment was published alongside the reports, so the injunction's precise terms — how long it lasts, whether it covers imports as well as sales, and whether BYD will appeal — are not established, and the main trial on the infringement claim is still pending, meaning the underlying design dispute has not been finally decided.

</div>

## Background

The order came in Dutch interim proceedings (kort geding), a fast-track route for urgent relief in which the court rules on a provisional basis while a separate case on the merits continues. The underlying claim is that the Ti7 — sold in China as the Fang Cheng Bao Tai 7 — copies the exterior design of the JLR Defender. The same dispute had already produced a Dutch ruling: as reported on 12 November, a court ordered Jaguar Land Rover to destroy design files it had seized from BYD or face a penalty of $553,850.

## Why it matters

The immediate consequence is commercial: BYD's EU arm cannot build or deliver the Ti7 until the provisional order is lifted, so customers and dealers with orders for the model face uncertainty about delivery, and BYD's options are to appeal the interim decision, redesign the disputed styling, or wait for the main trial. The case also signals that European courts are willing to use fast interim relief to remove a competitor's vehicle from the market before a full infringement ruling exists.

## What to watch

Watch whether BYD appeals the interim order and how the Dutch court rules in the main trial, which will decide whether the design claim holds beyond the provisional stage.
