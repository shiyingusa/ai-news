Tesla's report to federal regulators says Autopilot or Full Self-Driving was engaged at 74 mph when a Model Y left a parkway in El Dorado Hills, California, hit a tree and caught fire in September, killing three of the four people inside, according to Electrek. The detail was absent from earlier Sacramento-area coverage of the single-car wreck. The report establishes that a driver-assist system was active, not that it caused the crash.

</div>

<div class="fact-check" markdown="1">

## Verified and unconfirmed

- **Verified:** Electrek reports that Tesla's own submission to federal regulators states Autopilot or Full Self-Driving was engaged at 74 mph when a Model Y left a parkway in El Dorado Hills, California, hit a tree and caught fire; CBS Sacramento and ABC10 reported in September that three of the four occupants died and the fourth was hospitalized with major injuries, and that the CHP was investigating.
- **Not yet confirmed:** Electrek does not link or quote the filing directly, so which system (Autopilot or FSD) was active, whether it was operating as designed, and whether driver or system error caused the crash all remain unestablished; NHTSA's own investigation status and any findings are not reported. The source also does not give Tesla's explanation or comment on the crash.

</div>

## Background

The 74 mph figure comes from a regulatory reporting channel, not the initial local coverage: under NHTSA's Standing General Order 2021-01, automakers must report crashes involving automated driving systems or Level 2 advanced driver assistance systems, the category that includes Tesla's Autopilot and FSD. NHTSA amended the order in 2023 and again in April 2025, adjusting the reporting requirements while keeping ADAS- and ADS-involved crash reporting in place. Tesla's submission on the El Dorado Hills crash is one of those reports.

## Why it matters

The filing gives regulators and safety researchers a documented fatal crash in which Autopilot or FSD was active at 74 mph, while showing that local news coverage alone can omit driver-assist involvement.
