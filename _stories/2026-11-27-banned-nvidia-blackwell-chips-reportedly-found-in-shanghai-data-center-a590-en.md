<div class="story-lead" markdown="1">

Bloomberg reported that two Chinese companies installed Nvidia's Blackwell chips in a Shanghai data center linked to Alice Huang, and that a serial number on the hardware led investigators to Taiwanese manufacturer Gigabyte. Exporting such chips to China without a US license is illegal.

The report does not explain how the chips reached Shanghai, who Alice Huang is, or whether US authorities have opened a case, and Nvidia, Gigabyte and the companies involved have not confirmed the account.

</div>

<div class="fact-check" markdown="1">

## Verified and unconfirmed

- **Verified:** Bloomberg reported that two Chinese companies installed Nvidia Blackwell chips at a Shanghai data center tied to Alice Huang, and that a rack serial number traced through a public warranty database to Gigabyte; The Decoder notes that exporting such chips to China without a US license is illegal.
- **Not yet confirmed:** Independent confirmation of the installation, the exact chip model (additional reports identify a GB300 rack while the source says Blackwell broadly), how the hardware reached China, who purchased it, whether any US export license was sought, and responses from Nvidia, Gigabyte, or Alice Huang.

</div>

## Background

Under US export controls, Nvidia's Blackwell-generation data-center chips cannot be shipped to China without a license. AI Daily Radar's November 15 digest reported, citing The Wall Street Journal, that Nvidia chips were reaching Chinese firms through Southeast Asian data centers that exploited a gap in those sanctions; the November 26 digest reported, citing the Financial Times, that Nvidia had told manufacturing partners to stop AI-server shipments to Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand and Indonesia as Washington tightened rules on third-country routes. The Shanghai find shifts the focus from those overseas conduits to hardware installed inside China, traced through a serial number to Taiwanese manufacturer Gigabyte.

## Why it matters

The report indicates that chip provenance is traceable at the level of individual units: one serial number was reportedly enough to trace hardware back to its manufacturer. For resellers, intermediaries and data-center operators handling Nvidia accelerators in China, that makes serial-number records and end-user documentation the likely focus of export-control scrutiny, and it keeps a US license as the legal baseline for shipping Blackwell parts to China.

## What to watch

Watch for statements from Nvidia, Gigabyte and the US Commerce Department's Bureau of Industry and Security on whether an investigation has been opened, and for Bloomberg to publish the serial-number documentation behind its tracing.
