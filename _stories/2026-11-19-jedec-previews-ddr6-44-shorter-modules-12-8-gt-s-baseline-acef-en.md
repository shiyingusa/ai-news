JEDEC has previewed the DDR6 memory specification, which calls for 74mm modules—44% shorter than DDR5's 133mm—and a baseline transfer rate of 12,800 MT/s (12.8 GT/s). At that baseline JEDEC lists 153.6 GB/s per module versus DDR5's 38.4 GB/s, using a 96-bit data bus split into four 24-bit subchannels and 64 banks. The preview also cites 128Gb (16GB) dies and says DDR6 could scale RDIMMs to 1TB or more per slot, but it leaves the pin count, operating voltage and burst length unannounced and sets no launch date; some in the industry see 2029 as a realistic target.

</div>

<div class="fact-check" markdown="1">

## Verified and unconfirmed

- **Verified:** JEDEC published a preview of the DDR6 standard stating an initial RDIMM data rate of 12.8 GT/s, a 96-bit data bus split into four 24-bit sub-channels, 64 banks, 128 Gb (16 GB) dies and a pseudo-channel architecture, with modules 74 mm long versus 133 mm for DDR5, JEDEC's press release and Tom's Hardware report. Tom's Hardware's arithmetic puts baseline per-module bandwidth at 153.6 GB/s against DDR5's 38.4 GB/s.
- **Not yet confirmed:** pin count, nominal voltage, burst length and supported form factors, which JEDEC has not disclosed and the report marks with question marks; the 4X figure is a baseline-versus-baseline comparison, only a little over 2X against DDR5-9200 RDIMMs (73.6 GB/s); module capacities of 128–256 GB consumer and up to 1 TB per server slot are the report's projections, not JEDEC figures; and no launch date exists — the 2029 commercialization target is third-party expectation, with no silicon, pricing or independent measurement reported.

</div>

## Background

JEDEC is the industry body that writes the DDR memory standards; the current generation, DDR5, began at a 4,800 MT/s baseline with 288-pin, 133mm modules, and server RDIMMs later reached DDR5-9200. DDR6 departs from that lineage by widening the data bus to 96 bits split into four 24-bit sub-channels and by allowing dies to be stacked with through-silicon vias, the changes that underpin the claimed bandwidth and capacity gains.

## Why it matters

Server and PC platform designers face new board and slot designs rather than DDR5 compatibility: the module shrinks from 133mm to 74mm and the data bus widens from 64 to 96 bits, while JEDEC has not disclosed DDR6's pin count.

## What to watch

The next checkable step is JEDEC's full DDR6 standard: its November 18 preview left pin count, burst length and nominal operating voltage unspecified and says the standard is still in development. A public DDR6 timeline tracker projects standards work, validation and server and client platform adoption running through 2029, and no official DDR6 launch date has been set.
