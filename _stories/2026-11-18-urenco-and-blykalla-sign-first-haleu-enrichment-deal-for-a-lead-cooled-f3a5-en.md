<div class="story-lead" markdown="1">

Urenco has signed a contract to provide enrichment for the fuel of Blykalla's first US reactor, an agreement the two companies call the world's first HALEU enrichment contract for a commercial lead-cooled reactor. HALEU, or high-assay low-enriched uranium, is uranium enriched beyond the roughly 5% level used in conventional reactor fuel, and only a small number of Western suppliers are working to produce it. Blykalla is a Swedish developer of lead-cooled advanced reactors; the announcement gave no contract volumes, delivery dates or financial terms.

</div>

<div class="fact-check" markdown="1">

## Verified and unconfirmed

- **Verified:** World Nuclear News reports that Swedish reactor developer Blykalla and enrichment company Urenco signed an agreement under which Urenco will supply enrichment for the fuel of Blykalla's first US unit, and the outlet itself describes it as the world's first HALEU (high-assay low-enriched uranium) enrichment contract for a commercial lead-cooled reactor; the report is dated 17 November 2026.
- **Not yet confirmed:** The contract's volume, delivery schedule, price and financial terms are not disclosed, and neither the site nor the operating date of the US unit is given. The "world's first" framing is World Nuclear News's own characterization rather than an independently verified designation, and no enrichment deliveries are reported to have begun — Blykalla has previously said only that it aims for its first 140 MWt SEALER-55 commercial reactor to be operating in the early 2030s.

</div>

## Background

HALEU — high-assay low-enriched uranium — is the fuel category involved, and Blykalla’s SEALER is its lead-cooled reactor design. Under the arrangement, Blykalla supplies the uranium feed while Urenco provides enrichment at its Capenhurst site for the first US SEALER, with deliveries expected in the early 2030s.

## Why it matters

Fuel supply is a gating item for advanced reactor projects, because a developer cannot qualify fuel or lock in a licensing schedule without a contracted enrichment source. This deal gives Blykalla a named Western enricher for its first US unit and creates the first publicly cited precedent for a lead-cooled design, though the undisclosed volumes and dates mean other developers cannot yet benchmark terms or delivery schedules against it.
