Internal notes from an August 2025 call, described in a federal lawsuit, record Commerce Department lawyer David Shapiro telling Intel's chief financial officer that the offer of a roughly 10 percent US government equity stake was not up for bargaining: "Do not make the mistake of thinking that this is a negotiation," he said, adding that the President expected to announce a deal on those terms that Friday. According to a summary of his remarks prepared for Intel's directors, Shapiro acknowledged the board could decline, but noted that rejection "will have consequences that [he] cannot predict."

The reporting does not say what those consequences were described to be, what the government paid or what conditions came with the stake, and it offers no measure of the holding's effect on Intel's chipmaking capacity or output.

## Background

The stake at issue is not new: the US government's roughly 10 percent equity position in Intel was announced in August 2025, the same month Commerce Department lawyer David Shapiro pressed the company's CFO on the terms. What this report adds is the internal record of how the board was moved to accept — notes from a company call that now appear in a federal lawsuit. Intel is the largest US-headquartered maker of advanced logic chips, which is why the terms of government ownership bear directly on who controls domestic chipmaking capacity.

## Why it matters

The notes as described leave Intel's board with two recorded options: accept the government's terms on the administration's timetable, or accept consequences the official said he could not predict. For other chipmakers that depend on federal money or approvals, the practical concern is that the cost of the stake can be priced while the cost of refusing cannot, so boards should expect equity demands to arrive with a deadline attached rather than as something to negotiate.
