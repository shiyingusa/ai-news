Volkswagen Group underestimated demand for its new family of small electric cars, and buyers are now waiting well over a year for delivery. The ID. Polo, Cupra Raval and Škoda Epiq are assembled at the group's Martorell plant in Spain, which is already building about 1,000 cars per day. InsideEVs' headline cites waits of nearly two years, while the electrive report behind it says ID. Polo customers sometimes wait more than a year and that the other three models in the family also require patience; the two accounts are not reconciled and no order-book figures are published. Volkswagen now plans to scale up Spanish production faster, but a shortage of battery cells is also slowing output.

<div class="fact-check" markdown="1">

## Verified and unconfirmed

- **Verified:** InsideEVs reports that the Martorell plant in Spain, which builds the Volkswagen ID. Polo and Cupra Raval, is already producing 1,000 cars per day, and that production there is now to be ramped up faster.
- **Not yet confirmed:** The wait-time figures rest on the outlet's reporting rather than a cited Volkswagen statement, and the numbers do not fully agree — the headline says buyers face waits of nearly two years, while the article text says ID. Polo customers sometimes wait more than a year and that the other three models in the family also require patience. The attribution of the delays to a battery-cell shortage, the claim that Volkswagen underestimated demand, and any per-model wait times or delivery dates are not confirmed by the company in the report.

</div>

## Background

The ID. Polo and Cupra Raval belong to Volkswagen Group’s new family of affordable small electric cars, which the group is assembling at a Spanish plant. The plant has already reached 1,000 cars per day, but a shortage of battery cells is constraining how quickly output can be increased.

## Why it matters

For buyers, the practical consequence is that ordering one of these models now may mean waiting more than a year for delivery, and the bottleneck is not only the Martorell assembly line's throughput but also battery cell supply. Volkswagen Group's battery unit PowerCo has identified three cell production sites — Salzgitter (Germany), Valencia (Spain) and St. Thomas (Canada) — so the pace at which Spanish cell capacity comes online is the lever that determines whether those wait times shorten.

## What to watch

Watch for Volkswagen to publish revised delivery estimates or announce additional battery-cell capacity for Martorell. The quoted wait times only shorten if cell supply improves, not just assembly-line speed.
