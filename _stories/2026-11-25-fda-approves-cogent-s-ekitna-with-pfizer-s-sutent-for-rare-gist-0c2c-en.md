<div class="story-lead" markdown="1">

The FDA has approved Cogent Biosciences' drug bezuclastinib, which will be marketed as Ekitna, for use in combination with Pfizer's older cancer drug Sutent (sunitinib) in advanced gastrointestinal stromal tumors (GIST) — tumors that arise in the digestive tract, most often in the stomach. Cogent won the clearance on November 24, according to STAT, which points to the FDA's own approval notice.

The report does not give the trial results behind the decision, the approved dosing, the price, or when the combination will reach patients.

</div>

<div class="fact-check" markdown="1">

## Verified and unconfirmed

- **Verified:** The FDA granted Cogent Biosciences approval for bezuclastinib, marketed as Ekitna, in combination with Pfizer's Sutent for advanced GIST, per the FDA approval notice cited by STAT.
- **Not yet confirmed:** The efficacy and safety data supporting approval, the labeled dosing and any restrictions on which patients qualify, the price, and the launch date — none appear in STAT's brief item, which also describes the indication as "stomach cancer" while the FDA notice it links to specifies advanced gastrointestinal stromal tumors.

</div>

## Background

The approval is narrower than "stomach cancer" suggests: the FDA label covers adults with advanced gastrointestinal stromal tumors (GIST), a rare tumor type, whose disease has already been treated with imatinib. Sunitinib — the older Pfizer kinase inhibitor Cogent's drug is paired with — is already used in GIST after imatinib, and Cogent's own contribution, bezuclastinib, is also a kinase inhibitor. The decision follows Cogent's PEAK Phase 3 trial of the combination, detailed data from which the company presented at the 2026 American Society of Clinical Oncology meeting, and Cogent has been running expanded-access programs for GIST patients who meet disease-specific criteria.

## Why it matters

Patients with advanced GIST gain another combination option, pairing a newly approved targeted drug with sunitinib, a therapy that has been on the market for years. Whether clinicians can use it immediately depends on details the report does not provide — the prescribing information's dosing and patient-selection criteria, plus when Cogent actually begins shipping.

## What to watch

Cogent's launch and pricing announcement, and the full prescribing information for Ekitna, which will set out dosing and any conditions on use.
