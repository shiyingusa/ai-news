India's nuclear regulator has granted Nuclear Power Corporation of India permission for the First Approach to Criticality at unit 8 of the Rajasthan Atomic Power Project — the staged procedure of bringing a reactor to its first self-sustaining chain reaction. World Nuclear News reported the approval alongside the return to service of Kaiga unit 1, which had been shut for a refurbishment outage it described as record-breaking. Neither the date of first criticality for Rajasthan 8 nor the length of the Kaiga outage was given in the report.

</div>

<div class="fact-check" markdown="1">

## Verified and unconfirmed

- **Verified:** World Nuclear News reported that India's nuclear regulator gave NPCIL permission for the First Approach to Criticality at Rajasthan Atomic Power Project unit 8, and that Kaiga unit 1 restarted after its refurbishment outage.
- **Not yet confirmed:** that Rajasthan 8 has actually achieved criticality or been connected to the grid; the duration of the Kaiga outage, which the report calls record-breaking without giving a figure; and the timing of either plant's full commercial operation, which no source specifies.

</div>

## Background

Rajasthan Atomic Power Project unit 8 is a 700 MW pressurized heavy water reactor (PHWR) — a design using heavy water as both coolant and moderator — under commissioning by state-owned Nuclear Power Corporation of India (NPCIL), with commercial operation tracked for 2027. "First approach to criticality" is the commissioning step in which operators gradually raise reactivity to bring a reactor to its first self-sustaining chain reaction; the Atomic Energy Regulatory Board's permission for that step is not the same as the unit actually reaching criticality. Kaiga unit 1, the other unit in the report, had been offline for a refurbishment outage before returning to service.

## Why it matters

The approval lets NPCIL begin the physics testing that precedes criticality at Rajasthan 8, but it does not yet add generation capacity: the unit only supplies power after it reaches criticality and is synchronized to the grid, a sequence the report does not date. Kaiga 1's restart, by contrast, returns an existing unit to service after a longer-than-usual outage.

## What to watch

Watch for NPCIL or the regulator to confirm first criticality and grid synchronization for Rajasthan 8, and for disclosure of how long Kaiga 1 was actually offline. No date for either has been announced.
