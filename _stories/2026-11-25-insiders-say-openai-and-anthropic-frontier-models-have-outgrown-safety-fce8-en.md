<div class="story-lead" markdown="1">

Axios reported that numerous employees at OpenAI and Anthropic believe their employers' unreleased frontier models can no longer be safely controlled, according to The Decoder's account of the story. Both companies are said to be debating internally whether to pause releases or stop training altogether, and employee trust in leadership is described as eroding.

The report names no specific models, gives no firm number of people involved beyond "dozens" in The Decoder's framing, and carries no on-record response from either company or any account of a decision being taken.

</div>

<div class="fact-check" markdown="1">

## Verified and unconfirmed

- **Verified:** Axios reported, and The Decoder relays, that many staff at OpenAI and Anthropic doubt their own unreleased frontier models can still be safely controlled, and that internal discussions about pausing releases or training are underway.
- **Not yet confirmed:** which models are at issue, how many employees hold this view, whether leadership shares it, and whether any pause has been agreed or scheduled — all of this rests on unnamed insiders, and neither company's position is stated in the account.

</div>

## Background

Frontier models are the most capable systems a lab has built, and labs typically gate them with pre-release risk evaluations, red-teaming and staged access. An OpenAI spokesperson said the company has adopted new safety requirements that could slow the pace of model releases, according to reporting on the internal concerns, though the specific requirements were not detailed.

## Why it matters

The dispute is over whether to ship or even keep training the next frontier models, so teams planning to build on those models' successors have no reliable signal on timing: the report offers no model names, no schedule and no confirmation that either lab has changed course.

## What to watch

Whether OpenAI or Anthropic responds on the record to the Axios reporting, and whether either publishes an update to its safety framework or release plan that reflects the internal debate.
