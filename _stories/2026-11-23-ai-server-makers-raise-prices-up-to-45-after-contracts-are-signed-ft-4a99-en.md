<div class="story-lead" markdown="1">

Trading and financial-services firms buying data-center servers are reportedly being billed up to 45% more than the prices agreed months earlier, with memory-heavy CPU nodes doubling or tripling in cost while GPU servers rose 20–30%, according to the Financial Times. The FT attributes the increases to the DRAM shortage and cites unnamed people describing the gap between the initial quote and the final invoice, one calling a 25, 30 or 45 percent increase "disconcerting." Those buyers are reportedly absorbing the cost rather than walking away — "we've never said no," one person said, because "the compute is valuable to us" — and none of the companies the FT asked would confirm or deny whether their contracts contain clauses against price volatility. The report points to an existing test case: in June, XTX Finland Oy sued a Dell Irish subsidiary after Dell sought $62–70 million on top of an agreed $549 million for 1,680 servers, a dispute that is still ongoing.

</div>

## Verified and unconfirmed

- **Verified:** The Financial Times reported that data center server suppliers are seeking price increases of 25%, 30% or as much as 45% on contracts signed months earlier, with GPU servers up 20–30% and memory-heavy CPU nodes doubling or tripling in price; TrendForce and other outlets repeated the figures, crediting the FT. The one documented dispute is on the record: XTX Finland Oy is suing a Dell Irish subsidiary over an added $62–70 million on a previously agreed $549 million order for 1,680 servers, a case The Irish Times reported in June and that is still ongoing.
- **Not yet confirmed:** Every percentage figure rests on unnamed people described by the FT, with no customer, contract, invoice or specific server model and configuration published, and no supplier named in the FT account beyond the Dell litigation; whether buyers hold clauses capping price volatility is unresolved — the FT asked the companies it contacted and none confirmed or denied it.

</div>

## Background

AI servers combine CPUs, GPUs and large amounts of DRAM, the main memory used in servers, and memory-heavy CPU nodes are especially exposed when memory supply tightens; orders are often placed months before delivery, so vendors can face higher component costs before they ship. A June example of the same tension: XTX Finland sued a Dell Irish subsidiary after Dell sought $62 million–$70 million more on a $549 million order for 1,680 servers, and XTX reportedly paid $5 million extra for a clause meant to prevent such increases, the Irish Times reported.

## Why it matters

The consequence is contractual: XTX reportedly paid $5 million extra for a clause specifically meant to prevent price changes and is still in court, so buyers placing orders months ahead should treat escalation terms and delivery-timing conditions as a cost line, not boilerplate.

## What to watch

The Irish proceedings brought by XTX Finland Oy against Dell's Irish subsidiary, which the report says remain ongoing; a ruling would indicate whether an agreed server price is enforceable once memory costs spike.
