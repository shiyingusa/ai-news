<div class="story-lead" markdown="1">

OpenAI announced the first phase of its "embedded assessments" program, in which outside organizations — Apollo Research, METR, Transluce, and Goodfire — evaluate its models' alignment, safeguards, and monitoring systems from inside the company rather than through one-off external audits. The most specific commitment disclosed is that OpenAI will embed six Apollo Research evaluators to investigate whether its models engage in subversive behavior, such as deliberately deceptive or hidden-goal conduct. OpenAI has not published findings, a schedule for results, or which model versions are in scope, and the source report notes that restrictions on evaluator access and on what they can disclose raise questions about how independent the assessments can be. This is an announced program, not a delivered evaluation result.

## Verified and unconfirmed

- **Verified:** OpenAI announced a first phase of embedded assessments, naming Apollo Research, METR, Transluce and Goodfire as the outside groups that will independently evaluate its models' alignment, safeguards and monitoring systems, according to OpenAI's own announcement. The source item adds that OpenAI will embed six Apollo Research evaluators to investigate whether its models engage in subversive behavior.
- **Not yet confirmed:** The announcement as supplied discloses no findings, no timeline, and no specific models or systems covered, and no third-party confirmation of the arrangement's terms. The source item also says that restrictions on access and disclosure raise questions about how independent the evaluations will be — a characterization that, along with the figure of six embedded evaluators, rests on that single account rather than on any published evaluation result.

</div>

## Background

Third-party evaluation of frontier models usually happens either after a model ships — outside researchers probing it through a public API — or through one-off red-teaming exercises arranged around a launch. "Embedded" assessment is a different arrangement: outside organizations get access inside the lab, examining models and internal safeguards while development is still under way, which is what OpenAI describes this first phase as. The account of the arrangement also reports conditions attached to that access, including limits on what the embedded evaluators can see and disclose — the tension that makes the independence of such assessments a live question rather than a settled one.

## Why it matters

For AI safety researchers and enterprise buyers who treat third-party evaluation as evidence, the value of this program depends on two terms the announcement leaves open: what model weights, logs, and training details the embedded evaluators can actually inspect, and whether they may publish findings without OpenAI's approval. Because the assessments happen on OpenAI's premises under its agreements, the six Apollo evaluators' conclusions may function as an internal review unless those access and disclosure terms are made public and verifiable.
