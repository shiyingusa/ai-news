<div class="story-lead" markdown="1">

California's DMV ordered Waymo to stop driving its robotaxis on the state's highways immediately, and the company is now keeping the fleet to surface streets until it can convince the regulator that the vehicles no longer "pose an imminent hazard to public safety." The trigger was the software's tendency to drive into construction zones at speed; the San Francisco Business Times first reported the order.

It is a repeat problem: Waymo told NHTSA that six Phoenix robotaxis failed to recognize ramp-closure signs in April, and seven cars entered lanes with active construction in California in May, after which Waymo recalled about 3,800 highway-capable robotaxis in June and stayed off freeways until late July. A July software update was meant to improve recognition of freeway construction zones, prioritize them over other hazards, and reduce speed when the car thinks it may be inside one.

</div>

<div class="fact-check" markdown="1">

## Verified and unconfirmed

- **Verified:** The DMV ordered Waymo to suspend California highway driving until it demonstrates the cars are no longer an imminent hazard, as reported by the San Francisco Business Times and confirmed by Waymo's current surface-street-only operations; Waymo's June recall of roughly 3,800 highway-capable robotaxis and its July description to NHTSA of the construction-zone software fix are documented in the company's own regulatory filings.
- **Not yet confirmed:** The DMV has not publicly released the text of the order, so its exact scope and conditions are known only through reporting; Waymo has not said how many vehicles or which markets are affected by the current restriction, whether the July software update actually resolved the construction-zone behavior, or when it expects freeway service to resume.

</div>

## Background

The DMV order follows a pattern of construction-zone failures. Waymo robotaxis failed to recognize ramp-closure signs on six occasions in Phoenix in April, and seven vehicles entered lanes with active construction in California a month later. In June, Waymo recalled almost 3,800 robotaxis capable of highway driving and kept its California fleet on surface streets until late July, when highway operations resumed after a software update intended to improve recognition of freeway construction zones and reduce speed inside them.

## Why it matters

Riders in California lose highway routes, so trips that previously used freeways will take longer or fall outside Waymo's service area until the company satisfies the DMV — and any expansion that depends on freeway driving in the state is on hold.

## What to watch

Whether Waymo files a formal response or demonstration to the California DMV making the case that the construction-zone defect is fixed, and whether the regulator then lifts the highway restriction.
