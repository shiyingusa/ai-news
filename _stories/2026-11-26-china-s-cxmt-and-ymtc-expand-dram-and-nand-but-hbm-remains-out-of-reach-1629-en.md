China's two homegrown memory champions are scaling fast on commodity products but still cannot supply the AI-critical part. CXMT, the DRAM maker, reported a nine-month net profit of ¥132.0 billion ($19.6 billion) on revenue of ¥269.0 billion ($40.0 billion), versus a ¥5.28 billion loss a year earlier, and now runs three 12-inch fabs at roughly 100,000 wafers a month each, with three more planned that could push it past 600,000 wafers a month.

NAND maker YMTC posted ¥47.04 billion ($7.0 billion) in first-quarter revenue and ¥33.38 billion ($5.0 billion) in attributable profit, more than double 2025. Neither company produces high-bandwidth memory (HBM) — the stacked DRAM that AI accelerators depend on — in volume: CXMT's IPO prospectus lists no HBM project, and its HBM3E is only reported to be in risk production, an early run before the process is fully qualified.

The report notes that fab counts, products and financials come from filings and company statements, while capacities and HBM progress rest largely on anonymous reporting.

## Verified and unconfirmed

- **Verified:** Tom's Hardware, citing CXMT's Oct. 30 quarterly filing, reports a nine-month net profit of ¥132.0 billion ($19.6 billion) on revenue of ¥269.0 billion ($40.0 billion), against a ¥5.28 billion loss a year earlier; YMTC's Q1 figures were ¥47.04 billion in revenue and ¥33.38 billion in profit attributable to shareholders. The article states that no Chinese manufacturer can yet produce HBM in volume, and that CXMT's STAR Market prospectus lists no HBM project — Huawei designs its own HBM but does not disclose who manufactures it.
- **Not yet confirmed:** CXMT's HBM3E status rests on anonymous reporting — Pandaily reported that risk production has begun with samples under qualification at Alibaba's T-Head and Cambricon, but yields, volumes and final specs remain undisclosed; TechPowerUp reported CXMT still struggles with HBM3E module yield, leaving any mass-production date (reported targets cluster around 2027) unverified. Fab capacity plans, including the Shanghai and second Beijing fabs, and the identity of Huawei's HBM foundry are likewise unconfirmed; Tom's Hardware itself notes capacities, planned fabs, and HBM progress rest largely on anonymous sources.

## Background

High-bandwidth memory (HBM) is DRAM stacked in vertical layers and connected through silicon vias so an AI accelerator can move far more data per second than ordinary memory modules allow — it is the memory type that chips like Nvidia's GPUs are designed around. U.S. export rules currently bar Samsung, SK hynix and Micron from selling HBM into China, which is why a domestic HBM supply has become the key test for Chinese memory makers. Standard DRAM and NAND flash are older commodity businesses where CXMT and YMTC have spent years catching up on capacity and process technology; CXMT in particular has no access to EUV lithography, the tooling that leading-edge DRAM makers rely on to shrink cells.

## Why it matters

Because Washington bars Samsung, SK hynix and Micron from selling HBM into China, domestic AI accelerator designers — Huawei designs its own HBM but does not say who manufactures it — have no confirmed volume supplier of the memory their chips need, which keeps HBM the binding constraint on China's AI hardware output even as commodity DRAM and NAND capacity surges. For memory buyers, the concrete near-term signal is supply: CXMT's Shanghai fab is scheduled to take delivery of tools in the second half of 2026 with volume in 2027, and YMTC's third Wuhan fab is expected to reach about 50,000 wafers a month by 2027, with roughly half of that output earmarked for DRAM rather than NAND.

## What to watch

CXMT's president says the company's first 4F² product, a server DDR5 RDIMM, is due by year-end 2026 — a test of how far it can scale without EUV lithography. Whether CXMT's reported HBM3E risk production converts into qualified mass output, which the report flags as possible in 2027, is the other checkable milestone.
