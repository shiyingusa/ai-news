<div class="story-lead" markdown="1">

OpenAI chief scientist Jakub Pachocki posted a proposal on November 22 for AI companies to adopt common limits on advanced model development, using a cross-company metric such as perplexity — a next-token prediction score where lower is better — as an initial threshold, and to commit not to cross it until stronger monitoring exists. He disclosed internal figures showing GPT-3.5 at about 0.99 log perplexity and GPT-4 at about 0.91, with new cybersecurity and math capabilities appearing below 0.8 and breakthroughs on some open math problems around 0.76; he expects 0.68–0.7 could bring more transformative but unspecified abilities.

Pachocki warned that pretraining could advance rapidly as early as 2027 while alignment and behavior monitoring lag, and proposed shared evaluation, transparency and red-team standards before deployment. OpenAI CEO Sam Altman endorsed the article, but no lab has announced adoption and the proposal sets no binding rules.

</div>

## Verified and unconfirmed

- **Verified:** OpenAI chief scientist Jakub Pachocki published a post proposing that leading AI labs agree on measurable limits for advanced model development, built around perplexity as a cross-company tracking metric — TechCrunch and ForkLog both reported the post on Nov 22.
- **Not yet confirmed:** The specific internal figures (log-perplexity of about 0.99 for GPT-3.5 and 0.91 for GPT-4, capability jumps below 0.8, breakthroughs near 0.76, transformative capability at 0.68–0.7), Sam Altman's endorsement, and the 2027 acceleration warning come only from the post as relayed by GeekPark/CnBeta; no other lab has adopted the proposal or published comparable numbers, which AI Weekly notes leaves the "common limits" idea without a second signatory, and no independent test confirms the thresholds.

</div>

## Background

AI Daily Radar's November 16 digest reported that OpenAI had started a first phase of "embedded assessments," bringing Apollo Research, METR, Transluce and Goodfire inside to independently evaluate its models' alignment, safeguards and monitoring systems; that digest noted OpenAI disclosed no models, timetable or conclusions, and that limits on access raised questions about how independent the reviews could be. Pachocki's proposal would push that kind of pre-deployment evaluation from a single company's internal program toward shared cross-company standards, anchored to a pretraining metric (perplexity, a measure of how well a model predicts the next text token) that outside firms could track against one another.

## Why it matters

For frontier labs, the proposal would turn perplexity-style pretraining metrics into a pre-deployment gate, requiring agreed red-team and monitoring milestones before a model crosses a set threshold. Because it is voluntary and no company has signed on, it does not yet change any release schedule or safety requirement.

## What to watch

Watch for any AI company formally committing to the proposed common limits or publishing a concrete threshold and evaluation protocol; no adoption or timetable has been announced.
