Tesla announced on November 16 that it had expanded its Miami Robotaxi service area to about 59 square miles from roughly 31, a 92% increase that Teslarati describes as roughly double the previous geofence. The new map adds Downtown Miami, Brickell, Wynwood, Little Havana and Hialeah, and riders can hail Model Y vehicles through the Tesla Robotaxi app around the clock, a schedule in place since mid-November.

Tesla began unsupervised Robotaxi rides in Miami on July 3. The article contradicts itself on Miami Beach: one passage says it is now included, while a later passage says it remains outside the service area.

</div>

<div class="fact-check" markdown="1">

## Verified and unconfirmed

- **Verified:** Teslarati, citing Tesla’s November 16 announcement and a new service map, reports that the Miami geofence grew to about 59 square miles from roughly 31, and that Downtown, Brickell, Wynwood, Little Havana and Hialeah are now inside it; Tesla began unsupervised Miami rides on July 3 and has run 24-hour service since mid-November.
- **Not yet confirmed:** The article contradicts itself on Miami Beach—first listing it among newly included areas, then saying it remains outside—and the 59-square-mile figure and exact boundary come from Teslarati’s reading of Tesla’s map, not an independent measurement. The sources do not include independent data on ride volume or service quality.

</div>

## Background

Tesla began unsupervised Robotaxi rides in Miami on July 3, 2026 — its first market outside Texas and California — inside a geofence of roughly 25 square miles covering West Miami, Doral, Sweetwater and Miami International Airport; a smaller expansion later took it to about 31 square miles. AI Daily Radar's November 16 digest already reported the near-doubling to 59 square miles and the shift to 24-hour service, figures that came from Not a Tesla App's reporting rather than a published Tesla statement. Neither that coverage nor this one included trip volumes, fleet size or independent safety data.

## Why it matters

Riders in the expanded zone can now book 24-hour driverless Model Y trips into Downtown and Brickell that were previously outside the boundary, while Tesla gains access to higher-demand corridors and more real-world driving data. The Miami footprint is now roughly comparable to Waymo’s approximately 60-square-mile Miami service area, though the article says Waymo’s area already includes beach coverage.

## What to watch

Watch whether Tesla extends the geofence to Miami Beach; Elon Musk has said beach coverage is “coming soon,” but no date is given.
