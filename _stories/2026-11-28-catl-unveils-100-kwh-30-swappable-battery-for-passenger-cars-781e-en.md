<div class="story-lead" markdown="1">

CATL has introduced a new battery pack for its battery-swapping ecosystem in China, dubbed the "30#" battery block, with a capacity of 100 kWh. According to the company's announcement, the pack can be swapped in under a minute, and it can alternatively be fast-charged at a conventional charging station rather than being confined to swap stations.

CATL did not disclose the pack's chemistry, which vehicle models will accept it, when it will enter service or what it will cost, so the sub-minute swap time and 100 kWh figure remain company claims rather than independently measured results.

</div>

<div class="fact-check" markdown="1">

## Verified and unconfirmed

- **Verified:** CATL unveiled a 100 kWh passenger-car battery pack called “30#” for its swapping network in China, which the company says can be exchanged in about a minute and can also be fast-charged at ordinary charging stations, electrive reported on 27 November; Chinese and specialist outlets covering the same launch (CarNewsChina, Interesting Engineering) attribute the 99-second swap figure, a 700 km CLTC range and a lifetime warranty for packs in the network to CATL.
- **Not yet confirmed:** everything beyond capacity and the two use modes rests on CATL's own presentation — the 700 km CLTC range, 99-second swap, 600 kW peak discharge, a 3-second 0–100 km/h sprint and a 27-year locking-mechanism lifespan have not been independently tested, and the reports leave out battery chemistry, which vehicle models are compatible, pack price, and when or where the pack will be deployed; the sources also conflict on swap time, with electrive saying “under a minute” while the other outlets report 99 seconds, which is just over one minute.

</div>

## Background

Battery swapping exchanges a depleted pack for a charged one at a dedicated station rather than waiting at a charger, which requires packs built to a shared size and connector and cars designed around that standard. CATL already operates a battery-swapping ecosystem in China, and the "30#" is a new pack added to that line-up rather than a standalone product — the company positions it as dual-use, since the same pack can also fast-charge at ordinary stations. Because the format only works if carmakers build vehicles to accept it, the pack's reach depends on which automakers adopt CATL's swap standard.

## Why it matters

A swappable pack that also accepts standard fast charging removes the main practical objection to swap-based EVs — that drivers are tied to a proprietary station network — which matters most for fleet operators weighing CATL's swapping system against conventional charging; whether that flexibility reaches buyers depends on which models CATL certifies for the pack, details it has not yet published.
