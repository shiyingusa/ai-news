## Verified and unconfirmed

- **Verified:** Comac's 19-seat CE-25A electric aircraft completed its first manned flight, according to electrive, which reports the aircraft is a joint development by Comac, CATL, Shanghai Jiao Tong University and other partners. The flight took place on 14 November 2026 at Binzhou in Shandong Province, as reported by Global Times and other outlets covering the event.
- **Not yet confirmed:** The reported specifications — roughly 249 miles (400 km) of range, 186 mph (300 km/h) cruising speed and a maximum takeoff weight near 22,046 pounds — come from press coverage of the flight rather than an independently measured test, and electrive's report gives no flight duration, altitude or battery details. Certification timetable, production plans and entry-into-service date are not stated in any supplied source.

</div>

## Background

The CE-25A is Comac’s first electric commercial aircraft, designed primarily for short-haul intercity transport rather than long-haul routes. It is a 10-tonne-class design carrying 19 passengers, with stated targets of a 300 km/h cruise speed, 2,400 m cruise altitude and 400 km range. Those figures frame the first piloted flight as a test of a regional-aircraft-sized battery-electric design, whose range is constrained by how much energy batteries can store per kilogram compared with jet fuel.

## Why it matters

The flight is the first public indication that the CE-25A's electric powertrain carries people aloft, which matters most to battery and airframe engineers working on aircraft of this size, where battery weight is the binding constraint. Anyone weighing the aircraft for actual routes still lacks the endurance, payload and certification data needed to judge it.
