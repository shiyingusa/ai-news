Anthropic began trading on Nasdaq under the ticker ANT at $111.50 a share on Nov. 19, up from its $83 IPO price, giving the AI developer a market value of about $2 trillion. The company raised roughly $81 billion in the listing, which CNBC described as the largest IPO on record and which Reuters said pushed Anthropic past SpaceX's market value. Anthropic's IPO filing showed $16.3 billion in revenue and an $80.7 billion net loss for the first half of the year, most of it a non-cash accounting charge the company says does not reflect its business. The opening price is a first trade, not a closing valuation; the source did not report where the stock finished the session.

## Verified and unconfirmed

- **Verified:** Reuters reported that ANT opened at $111.50 after pricing at $83 and reached a market value of about $2 trillion; CNBC reported the same opening price and called it the largest IPO on record.
- **Not yet confirmed:** the final proceeds and share count, which reports give as "more than $80 billion" and $81.3 billion, and the first-day closing price, which the source did not include.

## Background

Anthropic's listing follows the S-1 prospectus it filed roughly a month earlier, which disclosed $16.3 billion in revenue and an $80.7 billion net loss for the first half of 2026 — most of the loss an accounting charge — alongside hundreds of billions of dollars in long-term compute commitments. AI Daily Radar's November 15 digest reported that, days before the planned IPO, CEO Dario Amodei described buying compute as the company's "deepest business dilemma," with commitments said to total nearly $901 billion, and that the offering was intended partly as a financial buffer given uncertainty over whether future revenue would justify those costs. The debut puts a public-market price on those obligations: the shares priced at $83, a level the reports tie to a valuation of about $1.5 trillion, before opening at $111.50.

## Why it matters

Anthropic now has to report quarterly, so the figures in its S-1 — $16.3 billion in first-half revenue against an $80.7 billion net loss, mostly an accounting charge, plus large multi-year compute commitments — become recurring public disclosures rather than a one-time filing. Its main infrastructure partners, Amazon and Google, keep earning from AI capacity whether or not Anthropic holds its lead, so the listing raises capital without shifting bargaining power to the lab. Investors will also test whether its safety positioning and public backlash against data centers constrain growth, a question Anthropic previously answered only in private rounds.

## What to watch

Watch the first session's closing price for ANT and the final prospectus figures for shares sold and gross proceeds, which will show how much of the opening gain held.
