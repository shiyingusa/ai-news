San Diego's city council voted Monday evening to end its contracts with two surveillance equipment vendors, Flock and Ubicquia, according to the San Diego Union-Tribune report cited by Ars Technica. Flock, a license-plate-reader startup with an estimated $8.3 billion valuation, loses one of the largest cities to cut ties with it so far; Ubicquia supplied the hardware behind the city's "smart streetlight" sensors. The reports do not specify the vote margin, how many devices are affected, or the date the equipment will go dark. The decision follows a broader run of city-level contract cancellations Ars Technica documented in August 2026.

## Background

Flock Safety is a license-plate-reader (ALPR) vendor: its cameras log passing plates and let police search the resulting database, while Ubicquia supplies the "smart streetlights" that package cameras and other sensors into street lighting. San Diego's police department cleared a City Council committee in October 2023 to seek approval for an Ubicquia contract covering both smart streetlights and ALPR, so Monday's vote reverses a deployment that had been moving forward for roughly three years. The decision also comes during a wider legal fight over these systems: in mid-November the EFF asked an appeals court to hold that searching a networked ALPR system is a Fourth Amendment search requiring a warrant, a question no court has yet decided.

## Why it matters

For San Diego residents, the vote ends a data-collection arrangement with the two vendors — including Flock's automated license plate readers, which log vehicle locations for police queries — though the reports do not say how quickly the systems stop operating. For other municipalities weighing renewals, San Diego becomes a large-city precedent that cancellation is politically survivable, a concern vendors have to answer in contract negotiations.

## What to watch

Watch for the city's formal wind-down schedule — when San Diego's Flock readers and Ubicquia streetlight nodes are actually switched off or removed — and whether Flock or Ubicquia publicly contests the termination.
