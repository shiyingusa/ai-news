Battery-electric cars accounted for nearly 30% of EU passenger car registrations in October, with 280,000 units registered — 61% more than in the same month a year earlier, according to figures from ACEA, the European automobile manufacturers' association. The October total is above the 250,000-plus monthly pace the report says pure electric cars have recently been sustaining across the bloc. The report does not give a country-by-country or model breakdown, and does not state the absolute October 2025 comparison figure behind the 61% growth rate.

## Verified and unconfirmed

- **Verified:** electrive reported, citing ACEA (the European Automobile Manufacturers' Association, which the search result confirms represents 17 major European car, truck, van and bus makers), that 280,000 battery-electric cars were registered in the EU in October, 61 per cent more than a year earlier, and that BEVs took nearly 30 per cent of the overall passenger car market.
- **Not yet confirmed:** no ACEA release or dataset was available to check the figures independently, and the report gives no country or brand breakdown, no total market volume, and no clarity on whether the scope is the EU alone or the wider EU+EFTA+UK grouping ACEA often uses; the headline rounds the share to 30 per cent while the article text says "nearly 30 per cent."

</div>

## Background

ACEA, the European Automobile Manufacturers' Association, publishes monthly registration figures for new passenger cars across the EU, and those counts are widely used as a proxy for sales because a car is registered when it is delivered. Battery-electric cars spent years as a single-digit sliver of that market before moving into the mainstream, so the monthly share has become the standard gauge of how quickly the EU fleet is turning over to electric drive. The October figure is a single month's snapshot rather than a year-to-date total, and the report does not give the overall size of the EU market that the roughly 30 per cent share is measured against.

## Why it matters

At roughly 280,000 registrations in a single month, the EU is adding BEVs at an annualised pace above 3.3 million, which is the volume figure charging-network operators and grid planners use to size new installations — so October's number matters less as a record than as evidence that monthly demand is running above the 250,000-plus level the market has recently held.

## What to watch

The next ACEA monthly registration release, covering November, to see whether the near-30% share holds or whether October was lifted by a one-off surge before year-end fleet compliance buying.
