The Childlight Institute in Edinburgh reports that reports of AI-generated child sexual abuse material rose from about 4,700 in 2023 to more than 400,000 in 2025, an increase of over 8,400 percent. Abusers are using "nudify" apps — consumer image tools that generate undressed bodies from ordinary photos — for blackmail, according to the report.

Childlight CEO Paul Stanfield called on governments to classify the problem as a preventable public health emergency. The figures come from the institute's own report and have not been independently verified.

<div class="fact-check" markdown="1">

## Verified and unconfirmed

- **Verified:** Edinburgh's Childlight Institute published a report estimating that reports of AI-generated child sexual abuse material rose from roughly 4,700 in 2023 to more than 400,000 in 2025, an increase of over 8,400 percent, and its CEO Paul Stanfield called on governments to treat the problem as a preventable public health emergency; The Guardian covered the surge on 16 November 2026, citing the same Childlight data.
- **Not yet confirmed:** the 4,700 and 400,000 figures are Childlight's own counts and have not been independently audited, and the item does not make clear whether they represent reports received, distinct files found, or victims identified; the claim that abusers use nudify apps for blackmail rests on the report rather than documented cases in the source, and a separate earlier figure cited in commentary — a 1,325 percent rise between 2023 and 2024 — covers a different time window and should not be read as contradicting or confirming the two-year total.

</div>

## Background

Child sexual abuse material (CSAM) is the legal term for imagery documenting the sexual abuse of children; "nudify" apps are tools that use image-generation models to digitally remove clothing from a photo of a real person, and they have become a route for producing such material from ordinary pictures. The figures in the Childlight Institute report are counts of reports — flags raised to hotlines and authorities — rather than verified tallies of unique images, so they measure how often this material is being reported. Childlight is an Edinburgh-based research institute focused on child sexual exploitation and abuse.

## Why it matters

For platform operators and the makers of consumer image-editing tools, the report supplies the kind of documented harm figures regulators cite when drafting mandatory reporting and takedown duties — and it locates the misuse in consumer nudify apps rather than in frontier model APIs, where most safety monitoring is concentrated.

## What to watch

Whether any national government formally adopts Childlight's proposed "preventable public health emergency" classification, and whether the institute's next reporting cycle restates the 2025 figure at the same scale.
