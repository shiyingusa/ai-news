Prosecutors told a federal court that they have found hundreds of audio files on the personal computer of Abdulrahman Alfozan, a former OpenAI employee arrested last month for hiding a covert recording device behind a conference-room television at the company, Bloomberg reported. The filing says dozens of the recordings capture OpenAI meetings and conversations from September 2026, many involving discussions that "may be trade secrets," and that Alfozan kept AI-generated transcripts, uploaded some files to Dropbox, and filed them by date and content.

Prosecutors also say that while he was recording and cataloguing those meetings he was in frequent contact with people affiliated with the Saudi government, and that his seized phone held images of a confidential "strategic partnership" document. Alfozan denies stealing trade secrets for Saudi Arabia, and his lawyer says he has no covert ties to the Saudi government; the October 27 indictment charges him with interception of oral communications rather than trade-secret theft or economic espionage.

Prosecutors are urging a judge to deny bail, arguing he is a flight risk, and a hearing on his requested $1 million bond was set for Tuesday, with further charges possible as the investigation continues.

## Verified and unconfirmed

- **Verified:** According to a prosecutors' court filing reported by Bloomberg, investigators found hundreds of audio files on former OpenAI employee Abdulrahman Alfozan's personal computer, including dozens of recordings of OpenAI meetings or conversations from September 2026, some of which prosecutors say involve discussions of what may be trade secrets; the filing also says he kept AI-generated transcripts, uploaded some files to Dropbox, and was in frequent contact with people affiliated with the Saudi government. Alfozan was arrested in October after a hidden recording device was found in an OpenAI conference room, and the October 27 indictment charges him with interception of oral communications — not trade-secret theft or economic espionage (Bloomberg, PBS).
- **Not yet confirmed:** Whether the recordings actually contain trade secrets, whether Alfozan passed anything to the Saudi government, and what he intended to do with the material — all of which he denies, with his lawyer stating he has no special or covert ties to the Saudi government. Prosecutors have not said what he might have wanted to do with the recordings, no additional charges have been filed, and the decision on his request for $1 million bond was still pending at a hearing set for Tuesday.

</div>

## Background

The case began earlier: Alfozan was arrested after a covert recorder built on a Seeed Xiao ESP32-S3 microcontroller — a low-cost hobbyist development board — was found hidden behind the television in OpenAI's executive conference room days after it was planted, and a federal indictment dated October 27, 2026 charged him with interception of oral communications. That charge is narrower than theft of trade secrets or economic espionage, which is why prosecutors' account of what was actually captured bears directly on whether more serious counts follow.

## Why it matters

The filings suggest the exposure may reach past the conference room: prosecutors say the recovered material includes discussions of OpenAI's partnerships and capabilities plus a confidential partnership document, and they have not said where copies of the recordings went. Employees whose September meetings took place in the affected rooms have no way to know from the public record which conversations were captured.

## What to watch

Prosecutors are urging the judge to keep Abdulrahman Alfozan detained, with a bail hearing set for Tuesday, November 24, where his request for a $1 million bond could be denied. The indictment so far charges only interception of oral communications, so watch whether investigators determine where the recordings were sent and add trade-secret theft or economic-espionage counts.
