<div class="story-lead" markdown="1">

Nuro has begun testing Lucid Gravity robotaxis on public roads in the San Francisco Bay Area with no one in the driver's seat, starting in four Peninsula and South Bay cities using a fleet of more than 100 Gravity SUVs that Lucid has delivered for the program. It is the largest step so far in the Uber-Nuro-Lucid partnership.

At the same time, the public launch that was originally promised for 2026 is now expected in the first quarter of 2027. The report describes an active driverless test, not a service the public can hail.

</div>

<div class="fact-check" markdown="1">

## Verified and unconfirmed

- **Verified:** Nuro is running Lucid Gravity SUVs without a safety driver on public roads in four Bay Area cities, and Lucid has delivered more than 100 vehicles for the program, per Electrek's report.
- **Not yet confirmed:** The Q1 2027 public launch window, the original 2026 target, and the specific cities involved rest on this single report; no company statement on permits, service area, pricing, or an exact launch date is included.

</div>

## Background

The program is a three-party arrangement: Nuro is testing autonomous driving in Lucid Gravity electric SUVs, with Uber intended to distribute rides through its app once the service opens publicly. Driverless testing is a distinct stage from a commercial robotaxi service: the vehicles operate on public roads with nobody in the driver's seat, but ordinary riders cannot yet hail them. The public launch has slipped from an original 2026 target to the first quarter of 2027.

## Why it matters

Bay Area riders hoping to hail a driverless Uber will have to wait: the partnership's public service is now pointed at Q1 2027 rather than 2026, even as the vehicles accumulate driverless miles. For Lucid, the program is a concrete outlet for more than 100 Gravity SUVs at a time when the company needs volume for its vehicle line.

## What to watch

Watch whether the public Uber launch holds to the Q1 2027 window, and whether Nuro widens driverless testing beyond the four Peninsula and South Bay cities before then.
