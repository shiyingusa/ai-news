At least two Ukrainian explosive naval drones struck the Russian missile corvette Merkury while it was docked in the Libyan port of Benghazi on November 22, sinking it, according to the Associated Press. Ukraine's Security Service said seven drone boats were used, launched from a mother ship, and President Volodymyr Zelenskyy released video that he said showed the drones approaching and detonating against the burning corvette and against a Russian dry cargo ship, MV Angara, which the drones also struck. Zelenskyy said a Ka-27 combat helicopter aboard the Merkury was destroyed and that the Angara was carrying weapons. The operation, reported alongside strikes reaching from the North African coast to the Gulf of Finland, extends Ukrainian drone attacks on Russian shipping far beyond the Black Sea.

## Verified and unconfirmed

- **Verified:** The Associated Press reported that the Merkury sank in port after being hit by at least two Ukrainian naval drones on November 22, and Zelenskyy published footage showing drone boats approaching and exploding against both the warship and the cargo ship.
- **Not yet confirmed:** the Security Service's account of seven drones launched from a mother ship, Zelenskyy's assertion that the Angara was transporting weapons, the extent of damage to the cargo ship, and any casualty figures; the report cites no Russian acknowledgement and no independent battle-damage assessment.

</div>

## Background

A naval drone of the kind used here—an uncrewed surface vessel, or USV—is a small, fast boat that carries explosives and is steered remotely or runs autonomously to its target, making it far cheaper than the warship it attacks. A corvette like the Merkury is a comparatively small, heavily armed warship. Benghazi is held by the Libyan National Army, which the report says receives material support from Russia, explaining why a Russian naval vessel was docked there.

## Why it matters

The sinking shows that small, explosive uncrewed surface vessels (USVs — remotely steered or autonomous boats packed with explosives that detonate on impact) can be carried by a mother ship and used to reach a warship inside a foreign port thousands of kilometres from Ukraine, so navies can no longer treat a friendly or allied harbour as a safe berth. The practical response for naval operators is to extend port and anchorage defences to small surface targets and to disperse valuable ships rather than concentrate them alongside.

## What to watch

Watch for Russian confirmation or independent satellite imagery and battle-damage assessments of the Merkury's wreck in Benghazi, which would test Ukraine's claim of a sinking rather than damage.
