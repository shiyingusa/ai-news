<div class="story-lead" markdown="1">

Tesla has expanded its Dallas robotaxi service area by 83% to roughly 150 square miles, adding the suburbs of Carrollton, Addison and Richardson, according to Not a Tesla App. The report does not say when the new boundary took effect, how many vehicles operate in the enlarged zone, or whether rides there are open to all users or gated by a waitlist.

The expansion comes from a Tesla-focused outlet rather than a company announcement, so the new map should be treated as reported rather than company-confirmed. No safety, utilization or wait-time figures for the Dallas operation were included.

</div>

<div class="fact-check" markdown="1">

## Verified and unconfirmed

- **Verified:** Not a Tesla App reported that Tesla's Dallas robotaxi service area grew 83% to 150 square miles, adding Carrollton, Addison and Richardson; EVwire's account of the same change describes the geofence moving from roughly 81 square miles north past I-635 toward Addison and Richardson.
- **Not yet confirmed:** No Tesla statement, map or filing confirming the new boundary was cited by any of the reports, all of which repeat the same figures, so the 150-square-mile area, the roughly 81-square-mile starting point and the 83% figure rest on secondary reporting rather than company data; the reports also give no vehicle count, no detail on whether rides in the new zone are driverless, and no independent verification of service quality there.

</div>

## Background

Tesla began public Cybercab rides in Dallas earlier, and trackers estimated its Dallas zone at about 80 square miles after an August 29, 2026 enlargement running from the Park Cities and Oak Lawn west toward Irving and south through downtown. Growing that zone to 150 square miles adds Carrollton, Addison and Richardson to the north. Waymo has run fully driverless commercial service in Dallas since February 2026, so Tesla is expanding inside a market that already has a competing paid driverless service.

## Why it matters

If the reported boundary holds, riders in Carrollton, Addison and Richardson can now request trips that previously fell outside the Dallas service area — a practical test of whether Tesla's coverage can grow into suburban trips rather than staying inside a dense core. Because the report gives no fleet size or pickup-time data, a larger map does not by itself mean shorter waits in the added zones.

## What to watch

Whether Tesla confirms the 150-square-mile boundary on its own robotaxi coverage information, which would move the change from third-party reporting to a company-stated service area. Also watch for the next Dallas-area expansion notice or any published vehicle count for the zone.
