<div class="story-lead" markdown="1">

The Trump administration is considering requiring US companies to publicly disclose on their websites when they use Chinese AI models, Reuters reported, citing unnamed sources. According to the report, the goal is to discourage companies from adopting Chinese models rather than to ban them outright. No draft rule, responsible agency, timetable, definition of which models count as "Chinese," or penalty for non-compliance has been reported, and the measure has not been formally announced.

</div>

<div class="fact-check" markdown="1">

## Verified and unconfirmed

- **Verified:** Reuters, citing unnamed sources, reported that the administration is weighing a requirement for US companies to disclose Chinese AI model use on their websites, with the aim of discouraging that use.
- **Not yet confirmed:** Whether any rule will be proposed or finalized, which agency would write or enforce it, when it might take effect, what counts as a Chinese model, and whether disclosure would be mandatory or voluntary — all of this rests on unnamed sources and no official document.

</div>

## Background

The report is the latest in a run of US proposals aimed at curbing AI traffic between the two countries. AI Daily Radar's November 17 digest reported that Nvidia had privately warned customers the proposed Remote Access Security Act could expose them to US legal action over Chinese firms reaching its AI chips remotely, for example through overseas data centres or cloud access — the mirror-image concern of Chinese models running inside US companies. Both measures remain proposals rather than enacted rules.

## Why it matters

If adopted, the requirement would turn a procurement decision into a public-facing statement: a company running a Chinese model — including inside a vendor's product — would be expected to name it on its own website, visible to customers, partners and competitors. Firms currently weighing Chinese open-weight models on cost or capability would have to factor that exposure into the choice, and would need to know which models are embedded in the software they buy, not just those they deploy directly.

## What to watch

Watch for whether the proposal moves beyond press reports into an official instrument — a Federal Register notice, Commerce Department action, or White House announcement naming the agency and the disclosure mechanics.
