Amazon is warning New York City customers that orders could cost up to $5 more each if the City Council passes a bill requiring the company to directly hire delivery workers, the New York Post reported. The company is publicly campaigning against the legislation, but the report does not give the bill's text, its current status in the Council, or the basis for Amazon's estimate. The $5 figure is Amazon's own projection, not an independently verified surcharge, and no vote date is specified.

</div>

<div class="fact-check" markdown="1">

## Verified and unconfirmed

- **Verified:** Amazon is running a campaign telling New York City customers their orders could cost up to $5 more apiece if the City Council passes Intro 518, a bill that would require the company to directly hire last-mile delivery workers instead of relying on contracted delivery firms, the New York Post reported on November 19. The $5 figure comes from Amazon itself, and Quartz reported the company also said the bill could force it to stop working with small delivery firms and consider moving operations out of the city.
- **Not yet confirmed:** The $5 per-order estimate is Amazon's own projection; the reports give no methodology, no independent cost analysis, no indication of whether the charge would hit every order or only some, and no effective date. The bill's current legislative status and any vote schedule are also not established in the available reporting, and an analysis published by the business-advocacy site progresschamber.org arguing that consumers would bear the cost echoes Amazon's position rather than testing it.

</div>

## Background

The bill is not new: NY1 reported in April that it already had majority support in the City Council and was awaiting a committee vote before a full Council decision, and that it would require companies like Amazon to hire contracted delivery drivers directly rather than through third-party firms. As of early November, the mayor's office and the Council were still negotiating amendments to the measure, according to the New York Times. Amazon's public campaign against the cost of the proposal therefore lands while the bill's final wording and timing remain unsettled.

## Why it matters

If the bill passes as described, it would make Amazon the direct employer of the workers who deliver its packages in New York City, and Amazon is telling customers that the change could add up to $5 to an order. That surcharge is Amazon's own estimate; the report does not say when it would apply, whether it would cover all orders, or how the figure was calculated.

## What to watch

Watch for a City Council committee hearing or vote on the bill; the New York Post report does not give a timetable.
