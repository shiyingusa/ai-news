Tiberius Aerospace, a "defense-as-a-service" startup that uses AI to design long-range supersonic artillery munitions, has raised an $85M Series A at a valuation above $1B, according to a Wired report by Isabella Ward summarized by Techmeme. The company is led by Chad Steelberg and Andy Baynes, Google and Apple alumni whom Wired describes as applying experience from running software companies and designing the iPhone to manufacturing lethal munitions. The report does not name the investors, describe the AI models or design pipeline, or give a timeline for test firings or production, so the funding and valuation figures rest on the company's own account rather than an independent filing or audit.

</div>

<div class="fact-check" markdown="1">

## Verified and unconfirmed

- **Verified:** Tiberius Aerospace announced on 17 November 2026 an $85 million Series A co-led by European investor Lakestar and US national-security investor Rochefort Asset Management, valuing the company at more than $1 billion after the round, according to the investors' joint announcement and Tiberius's own blog post; the company says its GRAIL platform links requirements, engineering, suppliers, compliance and manufacturing, and that the funding will add manufacturing partners for its SCEPTRE artillery round.
- **Not yet confirmed:** The performance claims behind the pitch — long-range supersonic flight, AI-driven design speed, cost per round and any delivery timeline — rest on the company's and investors' statements with no published independent test data; the $1B+ valuation is investor-stated rather than independently marked, and the sources do not disclose the round's terms, the split between primary and secondary capital, or whether the munitions have been fired in a live trial.

</div>

## Background

Tiberius Aerospace emerged from stealth in May 2025 and had previously raised only a $4.5 million founders' round, backed by Never Lift Ventures, Blacklake, Firedrop VC, Beaten Zone Ventures and other strategic investors, according to the company's own announcement and press coverage of it. Its first product, Sceptre, is a liquid-fuelled ramjet — an air-breathing engine that compresses incoming air using the munition's own forward speed — packaged into a 155mm precision-guided artillery shell to extend its range, which is the hardware its "defense-as-a-service" pitch is built around. The $85 million Series A is a roughly nineteen-fold step up from that founders' round and the first outside capital the company has disclosed since launching Sceptre.

## Why it matters

The round prices an AI-first munitions designer at unicorn scale before it has publicly demonstrated a working round, which matters to smaller defense suppliers competing for the same procurement budgets as established artillery manufacturers: investors are now willing to fund design-stage defense hardware at valuations previously reserved for companies with fielded products. Because no range, speed, cost-per-round or manufacturing data has been published, buyers and partners have no independent basis for judging whether the AI-designed munitions perform as claimed.
