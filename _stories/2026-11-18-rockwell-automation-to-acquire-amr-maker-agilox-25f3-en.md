<div class="story-lead" markdown="1">

Rockwell Automation has signed a definitive agreement to acquire AGILOX, an Austrian developer of autonomous mobile robots (AMRs — self-driving warehouse robots that move pallets and other loads). Rockwell said the deal will more than double the size of its AMR business and complement its existing OTTO Motors line; financial terms were not disclosed.

AGILOX will join Rockwell's Intelligent Devices operating segment, and the deal is expected to close in the second quarter of Rockwell's fiscal 2027, subject to regulatory approval. AGILOX says it has deployed more than 2,500 AMRs since it was founded in 2017, has over 250 employees, and operates in Europe, North America and China, serving automotive, third-party logistics, food and beverage, and pharmaceutical customers.

## Verified and unconfirmed

- **Verified:** Rockwell said it signed a definitive agreement to acquire AGILOX and that AGILOX will become part of its Intelligent Devices segment, with closing expected in fiscal 2027's second quarter pending regulatory approval, The Robot Report reported; AGILOX's own count of more than 2,500 deployed robots, 250-plus employees and operations in Europe, North America and China comes from the company.
- **Not yet confirmed:** The purchase price, whether the OTTO and AGILOX robot lines will be integrated or share fleet software, and Carlyle's claim that AGILOX quadrupled revenue since its 2021 investment all rest on the companies' statements without independent verification; completion of the deal also depends on regulators.

</div>

## Background

Autonomous mobile robots (AMRs) are self-driving platforms that haul material around factory and warehouse floors. Rockwell entered this market by acquisition: OTTO Motors came with its 2023 purchase of Ontario-based Clearpath Robotics, a deal Rockwell valued at $609 million at the time, and OTTO's flatbed robots now anchor its material-handling lineup. AGILOX, founded in 2017 in Austria, builds a different style of AMR with integrated lifting mechanisms and omnidirectional drives, and investment firm Carlyle took a majority stake in the company in 2021.

## Why it matters

The two portfolios overlap but are not interchangeable: OTTO is known for flatbed AMRs and an autonomous forklift (its OTTO 1500 carries up to 1,900 kg), while AGILOX builds AMRs with integrated lifting mechanisms and omnidirectional drives that move sideways and diagonally in tight aisles. Rockwell did not say how it will integrate the platforms or whether AGILOX and OTTO robots will eventually run as one fleet, so customers planning mixed deployments should seek clarity on fleet software and support commitments before the deal closes.

## What to watch

Watch for the regulatory clearance that would let the deal close in the second quarter of Rockwell's fiscal 2027, and for any purchase price disclosed in Rockwell's regulatory filings, since the announcement left the terms undisclosed.
