Lenovo reported a $640 million net loss for the September quarter, far wider than the $38 million analysts had expected, and its first operating loss since 2017, Bloomberg's Yuan Gao reported. Revenue rose 48% year over year to $30.2 billion, beating estimates on solid AI hardware demand, but accounting charges and rising PC component costs drove the loss.

The report does not break out how much of the loss came from the accounting charges versus component costs, and it does not cite Lenovo's own filing.

</div>

<div class="fact-check" markdown="1">

## Verified and unconfirmed

- **Verified:** Bloomberg's Yuan Gao reported that Lenovo's September-quarter revenue rose 48% year over year to $30.2 billion, above estimates, and that the company posted a $640 million net loss and its first operating loss since 2017.
- **Not yet confirmed:** The size of the accounting charges versus the component-cost impact, the segment breakdown, and whether the $38 million consensus figure was an expected loss or an expected profit; these rest on the Bloomberg account alone, with no primary filing cited.

</div>

## Background

Lenovo's September quarter is the second quarter of its fiscal year, which ends in March, so these results land mid-cycle for PC makers. Component costs have been climbing across the industry: at Apple's earnings earlier in November, John Ternus said "looking beyond the December quarter, we see the pricing for memory purchases continuing to increase," after Apple blamed higher memory prices for shrinking margins.

## Why it matters

Lenovo's sales grew sharply while its profit collapsed, which puts the pressure on the cost side rather than on demand: a PC maker of Lenovo's scale is absorbing memory and other component price increases faster than it is passing them through. Buyers planning fleet refreshes should budget for firmer unit pricing rather than expecting discounting to offset component inflation.

## What to watch

Lenovo's next quarterly report, covering the December quarter, will show whether the accounting charges were one-off and whether component costs have peaked. Memory and panel contract pricing for the same period is the input to track alongside it.
