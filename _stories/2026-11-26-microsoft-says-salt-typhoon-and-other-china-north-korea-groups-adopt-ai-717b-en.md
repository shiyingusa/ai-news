<div class="story-lead" markdown="1">

Microsoft says state-backed hacking groups in China and North Korea, including Salt Typhoon, have added ARTEX — described by The Record as a powerful AI penetration-testing tool — to their attack toolkits, according to a new Microsoft report. The Record's Jonathan Greig reports the finding as an adoption trend among government-backed intrusions rather than a single incident.

The account gives no technical detail on how ARTEX was used, what it targets, who built it, or how many intrusions it appeared in. The attribution and the tooling claim rest on Microsoft's own research, which the article summarizes but does not reproduce in full.

</div>

<div class="fact-check" markdown="1">

## Verified and unconfirmed

- **Verified:** Microsoft has published a report stating that state-backed groups in China and North Korea, Salt Typhoon among them, are using the AI penetration-testing tool ARTEX, as reported by The Record.
- **Not yet confirmed:** The available account does not say who developed ARTEX, whether it is custom-built or adapted from commercial tooling, which victims or sectors were targeted, how many intrusions are involved, or what indicators of compromise defenders should look for; those details rest on Microsoft's report and were not independently verified.

</div>

## Background

ARTEX is an open-source, multi-agent LLM penetration-testing framework, written in Go with a Next.js frontend, that can use any AI model to automate network reconnaissance, credential attacks and web application exploitation, according to Microsoft. AI Daily Radar's November 16 digest, citing a TechCrunch report on an FBI warning, noted that Artex AI was used to breach networks in under three hours at a U.S. IT company and in South Korea's financial sector, while cautioning that the speed claim and attribution were unverified outside that report. Microsoft had separately reported in October that the financially motivated group Storm-0940 used ARTEX in a ConnectWise supply-chain compromise; the new report extends documented use to state-backed groups including Salt Typhoon.
