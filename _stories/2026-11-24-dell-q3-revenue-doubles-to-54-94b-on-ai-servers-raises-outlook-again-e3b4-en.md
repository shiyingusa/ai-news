Dell reported third-quarter revenue of $54.94 billion, up 103% from a year earlier and above the $49.32 billion analysts had estimated, Reuters reported. The company also raised its full-year revenue and profit forecasts for the third time this year, after what it described as record quarterly results driven by surging demand for AI servers.

The numbers suggest data-center spending on AI hardware is still accelerating rather than flattening. The report, as summarized, gives no split between AI server revenue and Dell's other infrastructure and PC businesses, and no figures for the new guidance ranges.

</div>

<div class="fact-check" markdown="1">

## Verified and unconfirmed

- **Verified:** Reuters reported that Dell's fiscal third-quarter revenue was $54.94B, up 103% year over year and above the $49.32B analyst estimate, and that Dell raised its annual revenue and profit forecasts for the third time this year.
- **Not yet confirmed:** The report gives no dollar figures for the new annual guidance, no AI-server revenue or order backlog numbers, and no margin detail; the attribution of the quarter to "surging AI server demand" is Dell's and Reuters' framing rather than an independently audited breakdown, and secondary reports summarizing the same forecast raise describe it as a Thursday announcement, conflicting with Reuters' Tuesday.

</div>

## Background

AI servers are data-center systems built around GPUs or other accelerators to train and run AI models, and demand for them has become a central driver of server makers' sales. The quarter also lands in a tight supply environment: AI Daily Radar's Nov. 23 digest, citing the Financial Times, reported that some buyers of data-center servers were billed up to 45% more than their originally agreed prices amid a shortage of DRAM memory chips.

## Why it matters

Dell's third guidance raise this year, on revenue that more than doubled to $54.94B, signals that AI server demand is still outrunning supply rather than levelling off — and the bottleneck is spreading down the component stack. Dell'Oro Group recorded a 182% year-over-year jump in worldwide data-center semiconductor and component revenue in Q2 2026 across accelerators, memory, storage, CPUs and high-speed networking, the same inputs Dell's server backlog consumes. Buyers planning 2027 data-center capacity should treat lead times and component pricing as the binding constraint on delivery schedules, not Dell's assembly capacity.

## What to watch

Dell's next quarterly report will be the check on whether it delivers the full-year revenue and profit targets it just raised, and whether AI server orders are still growing into that guidance. Watch also for any disclosure that separates AI server revenue from the rest of its infrastructure segment, which this quarter's headline figures do not provide.
