<div class="story-lead" markdown="1">

Anthropic is telling prospective investors in its initial public offering that it expects to price shares at the high end of its $70–$78 marketed range or above, according to Bloomberg, which cited unnamed sources. The report gives no valuation, share count or pricing date. In a separate Bloomberg report on the same day, an SEC filing indicates that Amazon and Google will hold nearly 15% and 14% of Anthropic's outstanding capital stock respectively after the IPO and a private placement — about 28% combined. That stake figure comes from the filing itself, while the pricing guidance rests only on people familiar with the marketing process.

</div>

<div class="fact-check" markdown="1">

## Verified and unconfirmed

- **Verified:** Anthropic's updated IPO filing lists 825 million shares to be sold at a marketed price range of $70 to $78 per share, according to CNBC and Zacks, both citing the November 10 filing; Techmeme relayed Bloomberg's November 17 report on how that offering is being marketed.
- **Not yet confirmed:** That Anthropic is telling prospective investors it expects to price at or above the top of that range — Bloomberg attributes this to unnamed people familiar with the matter, and the item gives no final price, no valuation, no pricing date and no independent confirmation that demand actually supports a raise of the range.

</div>

## Background

AI Daily Radar's November 15 digest reported that, days before a planned record IPO, CEO Dario Amodei called buying compute Anthropic's "deepest business dilemma," with commitments near $901 billion and the listing framed partly as a financial buffer against uncertain future revenue. The marketed terms now in front of investors cover 825 million shares at $70 to $78 each, a range that on a fully counted basis would value the five-year-old company at roughly $1.26 trillion to $1.41 trillion, according to the New York Times.

## Why it matters

For anyone allocated shares or tracking the listing, the practical takeaway is that demand is being signaled above the marketed ceiling, so the clearing price may exceed $78 rather than settle mid-range — but that price is only fixed at the pricing announcement, and the guidance itself comes from unnamed sources, not a filing. The same-day filing disclosure that Amazon and Google will together hold roughly 28% of outstanding stock after the offering is the more durable detail: two large customers will also be among the largest shareholders, a governance fact investors cannot change by buying in.

## What to watch

The final IPO price when it is set: whether it prints at $78, above it, or below the guided level, and whether the concurrent private placement in the SEC filing closes on the disclosed terms.
