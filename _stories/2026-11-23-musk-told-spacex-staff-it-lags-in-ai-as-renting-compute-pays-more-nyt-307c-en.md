<div class="story-lead" markdown="1">

Elon Musk told SpaceX employees that the company is falling behind in AI and that renting out computing capacity from its data centers to outside customers — including Anthropic — is more lucrative than using that capacity to improve SpaceX's own products, the New York Times reported on November 23, citing unnamed sources. According to the report, the rentals are making it harder for SpaceX's own AI effort, which must compete for the same compute it sells. Neither SpaceX nor Anthropic has publicly confirmed the remarks or the customer relationship, and the report provides no figures for rental revenue, contracted capacity, or which other buyers are involved.

</div>

<div class="fact-check" markdown="1">

## Verified and unconfirmed

- **Verified:** The New York Times reported on November 23, citing unnamed sources, that Elon Musk told SpaceX employees the company is falling behind in AI and that renting data-center computing to competitors, including Anthropic, is more lucrative than using it to improve SpaceX's own products; Quartz and Fortune have likewise reported that SpaceX leases compute capacity to AI companies.
- **Not yet confirmed:** SpaceX has not publicly confirmed the internal meeting, Musk's remarks, or which companies are customers — the Times account rests entirely on people it does not name, and no contract terms, capacity figures, or revenue numbers have been disclosed. Quartz's headline names Google as a customer alongside Anthropic, while the Times summary names only Anthropic, so the full customer list remains unconfirmed.

</div>

## Background

Renting out data-center capacity is lucrative because outside AI developers are committing to enormous compute deals: AI Daily Radar's November 17 digest reported that Anthropic — named in the Times report as one of the customers renting SpaceX capacity — was planning a program called Anthropic Direct that would lease 875,000 Google TPUs for $7.2 billion a year through 2045, based on Financial Times reporting on internal documents. SpaceX has meanwhile been expanding its own AI footprint, disclosing in April an option to acquire the code-generation startup Cursor for $60 billion or pay $10 billion for a partnership, The Guardian reported.

## Why it matters

SpaceX's internal AI teams would be bidding for capacity against paying outside customers, so internal projects can be deprioritized whenever an external contract pays more — a dynamic the report says is already slowing the company's own AI work. For AI labs, it means an additional supplier of data-center capacity outside the major cloud providers.
