<div class="story-lead" markdown="1">

The EU Council appears to be siding with the European Parliament in favour of turning proposed binding electric-vehicle quotas for corporate fleets into voluntary targets, according to electrive. The European Commission had included a draft passage in its 'Auto Package' that would have required member states to set concrete mandates for ramping up electric company cars from 2030; the Parliament has already downgraded those quotas to voluntary targets, and the Council now seems to be following that approach.

Nothing has been adopted: the report describes a Council signal rather than a formal negotiating position, and the final shape of the rules will only emerge from the legislative process.

</div>

<div class="fact-check" markdown="1">

## Verified and unconfirmed

- **Verified:** The European Parliament's press service reports that MEPs backed draft rules covering corporate cars and vans of large businesses (more than 250 employees and turnover above €50 million), adopted 89 votes to 29 with four abstentions, that would replace binding quotas with aspirational national targets ranging from 67% to 95% for 2030 and 2035; electrive reported on 27 October that the EVP group had secured a majority against binding quotas.
- **Not yet confirmed:** That the Council has formally endorsed a voluntary approach — the report says only that the Council "seems to be following suit" and cites no Council document or vote, and the final scope, target levels and legal force will not be settled until member states and Parliament conclude negotiations.

</div>

## Background

Company cars were one of the levers the European Commission built into its "Auto Package": a draft clause would have obliged member states to set concrete mandates for ramping up electric company cars from 2030. The European Parliament has already moved to turn those binding quotas into voluntary targets, making the Council's position the second of the two legislative steps that would precede any final text.

## Why it matters

Corporate fleets are a major channel for EV uptake, so whether the 2030 obligation on member states is binding or voluntary determines whether fleet operators face a legal requirement or a discretionary national target. Automakers and fleet managers planning 2030 compliance should track the Council's formal position, since a voluntary framework would leave the level of ambition to each member state.

## What to watch

Watch for the Council to formalise its position on the Auto Package and for the resulting negotiations with the Parliament, which will show whether the voluntary-target approach survives into the final text.
