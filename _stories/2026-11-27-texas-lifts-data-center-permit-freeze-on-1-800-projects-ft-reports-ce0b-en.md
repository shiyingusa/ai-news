Texas has resumed approving data-center projects after an August freeze, acting on a directive from outgoing Governor Greg Abbott's office that affects more than 1,800 projects and 474 gigawatts of power requests, Tom's Hardware reported, citing the Financial Times. The state grid operator, ERCOT, has finished gathering information on how data centers affect local communities, the report said.

Developers are rushing to get approvals before Governor-elect Gina Hinojosa, who has pledged to rein in data-center construction, takes office in January. The report did not say how many permits have been issued since the resumption or how quickly ERCOT will process the 474 GW of requests.

</div>

<div class="fact-check" markdown="1">

## Verified and unconfirmed

- **Verified:** Tom's Hardware, citing the Financial Times, reported that Governor Greg Abbott's office confirmed the Electric Reliability Council of Texas (ERCOT) had finished gathering information on how data centers affect local communities and that the state has resumed approving data center projects; the August halt covered more than 1,800 projects and 474 GW of power requests, figures IBTimes also reported when the freeze was ordered. ZeroHedge, citing Politico, said the Texas Commission on Environmental Quality had returned to normal permit review in November and that the first permit issued after the freeze appears to have gone to SpaceX's $24 billion Starbase project.
- **Not yet confirmed:** No ERCOT or governor's office document is quoted, and the report gives no count of permits approved since the resumption, no timeline for clearing the backlog, and no statement from Governor-elect Gina Hinojosa's office beyond her earlier pledge to rein in construction; the characterization that developers are "rushing" to beat her January inauguration rests on FT's reporting, and the SpaceX-first-permit detail rests on a single Politico account relayed by ZeroHedge.

</div>

## Background

ERCOT, the nonprofit that operates most of Texas's power grid, must review the large new electricity loads data centers request before they can connect, which is why the state's permit pause could halt projects even where land and financing were in place. The freeze began in August, when Governor Greg Abbott's office stopped approvals and left 474 gigawatts of power requests subject to the moratorium, and in October the state suspended 17 more construction permits and shut an automated portal that had kept approving them. The reversal comes as Governor-elect Gina Hinojosa, who won in November, has called for a pause on new data centers until the state adopts protections for host communities; the Texas Tribune reported that approvals could stay paused until legislators pass new laws next year if she keeps those campaign promises.

## Why it matters

Developers can now seek approvals again, but the relief may be short-lived: Hinojosa takes office in January and has pledged to curb construction. The report also cautions that new power requests will not be processed quickly, pushing some developers to build their own generation, with gas turbines among the options.

## What to watch

Hinojosa's first moves on data-center permitting after she takes office in January, and ERCOT's processing pace for the 474 GW of requests.
