<div class="story-lead" markdown="1">

Nvidia has told its manufacturing partners to stop shipping AI servers to Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand and Indonesia, according to the Financial Times, which cited unnamed sources. The report links the move to a tightening round of US export controls intended to stop Chinese buyers from reaching AI computing power through third countries.

Neither Nvidia nor the US government has publicly confirmed the instruction, and the report as summarised does not specify an effective date, which server configurations are covered, or whether the halt is a formal licence requirement or voluntary compliance by Nvidia and its contract manufacturers.

</div>

<div class="fact-check" markdown="1">

## Verified and unconfirmed

- **Verified:** The Financial Times reported, citing unnamed sources, that Nvidia told its manufacturing partners to halt AI server shipments to Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand and Indonesia, and tied the move to tightening US export controls meant to keep AI computing power out of Chinese hands. Reuters and Channel NewsAsia both carried the story attributing it to the FT, rather than to their own reporting or to Nvidia.
- **Not yet confirmed:** Nvidia has not publicly confirmed any such instruction, and no US agency has been cited as publishing a measure that names these four countries. The identity of the manufacturing partners, which chips or server configurations are covered, whether the halt applies to orders already placed, and its duration are all left out of the reporting. One aggregator account said the halt reaches server makers including Dell and Supermicro and follows a US AI chip rule that took effect on November 19, but that detail rests on a single outlet and is not corroborated by the FT summary or the wire pickups.

</div>

## Background

The four countries named are the ones U.S. officials have focused on most in recent weeks. AI Daily Radar's November 16 digest reported that the Commerce Department issued an interim rule requiring export licenses for AI chips when the hardware could provide compute to China or other restricted destinations, extending controls beyond physical shipments to remote access and specifically naming Singapore, Malaysia, Thailand, Indonesia and Brazil. Two days earlier, AI Daily Radar's November 15 digest noted a Wall Street Journal investigation finding that Nvidia chips had been reaching Chinese companies through large Southeast Asian data centers — the channel the reported shipment halt appears intended to close.

## Why it matters

The four named countries host assembly and distribution capacity that has served buyers across the region, so a halt would push server makers and their customers to reroute orders or re-qualify supply elsewhere; firms with pending AI server orders in these markets should ask suppliers whether shipments are frozen and on what legal basis before committing to delivery schedules.

## What to watch

Watch for public confirmation or denial from Nvidia and for any formal rule or licence guidance from the US Commerce Department's Bureau of Industry and Security covering these destinations.
