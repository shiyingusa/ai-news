Senate Banking Committee Chairman Tim Scott said in a statement that Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang will testify publicly before the panel on December 3, Bloomberg reported. According to Bloomberg, the hearing will focus on oversight of US export controls — the rules governing which advanced chips, including Nvidia's AI accelerators, can be sold to which countries. No witness list beyond Huang, hearing title, or committee notice has been released, and no testimony or policy outcome exists yet; the December 3 appearance is scheduled, not held.

- **Verified:** Committee Chairman Tim Scott's statement, as reported by Bloomberg, says Huang will appear publicly before the Senate Banking Committee on December 3, with the hearing centered on oversight of US export controls.
- **Not yet confirmed:** The full witness list, the hearing's formal title and scope, and whether the date will hold; Bloomberg also describes Huang as "newly reinstated" CEO without explanation. ## Background

Nvidia's compliance with US export controls has been a running story: AI Daily Radar's November 15 digest reported a Wall Street Journal investigation finding that Nvidia AI chips reached Chinese companies through large Southeast Asian data centers that exploited a gap in US sanctions. AI Daily Radar's November 17 digest then reported that Nvidia privately warned customers the proposed Remote Access Security Act — aimed at Chinese firms reaching its chips remotely, via overseas data centers or cloud access — could expose those buyers to US legal action; that bill is still a proposal, not law. Both threads feed the enforcement question now going before the Senate Banking Committee, whose chairman announced the December 3 public session.

## Why it matters

A public Senate hearing puts Nvidia's compliance with chip export controls on the record under oath, giving lawmakers a venue to press for tighter enforcement and giving Nvidia's customers and investors a dated signal on whether licensing rules for AI chips in markets such as China face new restrictions.

## What to watch

The December 3 hearing itself: the released witness list and Huang's prepared testimony, and whether senators announce follow-up legislation or request specific enforcement actions against chip shipments.
