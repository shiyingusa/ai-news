<div class="story-lead" markdown="1">

Meta declined a UK Financial Conduct Authority (FCA) request to jointly test its personal agent Muse before launching it in the UK on Tuesday, WIRED reported. The FCA warned that AI agents could become “another force of attack,” according to the report. The account does not detail the testing the regulator sought, Meta’s reasons for refusing, or any specific vulnerability in Muse.

</div>

<div class="fact-check" markdown="1">

## Verified and unconfirmed

- **Verified:** Wired reported that the FCA said Meta declined its request to jointly test Meta's Muse personal agent before launching it in the UK, and warned that agents create "another force of attack."
- **Not yet confirmed:** Meta's account of why it refused, the specific attack scenarios the FCA is warning about, and the exact UK launch date and availability — the report says only "Tuesday."

</div>

## Background

Muse is Meta's personal AI agent, which can browse the web, connect to a user's apps and carry out multi-step tasks rather than only answering questions. Meta made it available to adults in the UK in late November 2026, and AI Daily Radar's 24 November digest reported that Politico described it as the first AI agent from a Big Tech company to launch in the country, with UK regulators saying they would watch the rollout closely.

## Why it matters

The FCA’s complaint signals that it will treat agent security as a supervisory issue even when a company proceeds without the testing it requested, leaving Meta’s UK launch under regulatory scrutiny from the start.

## What to watch

Watch for any FCA statement or formal review of Muse’s UK rollout, and for whether Meta agrees to post-launch testing or discloses what the FCA asked to examine.
