Sierra, the AI customer-support company led by former Salesforce co-CEO Bret Taylor, is in talks to raise about $1.5 billion at a valuation of roughly $30 billion, according to The Information, citing unnamed sources. That would roughly double the $15.8 billion valuation it carried in May. The reported round rests partly on revenue Sierra disclosed in October: more than $400 million in annualized revenue, meaning revenue booked over the past month extrapolated across a year. Neither Sierra nor its investors have announced the round, so the size, valuation and terms remain unconfirmed.

</div>

<div class="fact-check" markdown="1">

## Verified and unconfirmed

- **Verified:** Sierra's previous round was $950M at a $15.8B post-money valuation, announced May 4, 2026 and reported by CNBC, up from roughly $10B in the prior fall; CNBC also reported in May that Sierra's annualized revenue had reached about $200M, double two quarters earlier (CNBC, techstartups.com, cryptobriefing.com).
- **Not yet confirmed:** The ~$1.5B raise at a ~$30B valuation rests only on The Information's unnamed sources, and Sierra has not confirmed the talks, the amount, or the price; the $400M+ annualized revenue figure cited for October is the company's own claim with no independent audit, and no terms such as investor names, closing date or whether the round is signed have been disclosed.

</div>

## Background

Sierra sells AI agents that take over customer-service conversations for enterprise clients; its "annualized revenue" figure of $400M+ disclosed in October is a run-rate that extrapolates recent sales across a year, not audited full-year revenue. The company was last valued at $15.8B in a May funding round, so the ~$30B figure now under discussion would roughly double its private-market price in about six months. Founder Bret Taylor is a former Salesforce co-CEO who also co-founded the workflow software company Quip.

## Why it matters

At ~$30 billion, Sierra would be valued at roughly 75 times its stated annualized revenue, a multiple that other enterprise AI agent vendors and their buyers can expect to be measured against; procurement teams weighing multi-year agent contracts should note that the headline figure is annualized revenue disclosed by the company, not an audited annual result.

## What to watch

Watch for an official confirmation from Sierra or its lead investors, or a regulatory filing, that pins down the final round size and valuation rather than the reported ~$1.5 billion and ~$30 billion figures.
