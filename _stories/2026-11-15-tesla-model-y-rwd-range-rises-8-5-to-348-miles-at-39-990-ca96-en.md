Tesla has raised the EPA-estimated range of the Model Y Rear-Wheel Drive in the U.S. by 8.5%, from roughly 321 miles to 348 miles, while keeping the starting price at $39,990. The change is a specification update to an existing trim rather than a new model or platform. The figures come from Not a Tesla App, a Tesla-focused outlet, which reports the updated rating without citing a Tesla statement or EPA filing directly; the underlying documents would be Tesla's own online configurator and the EPA's published range data.

</div>

## Verified and unconfirmed

- **Verified:** Not a Tesla App reports that Tesla raised the Model Y RWD's EPA-estimated range by 8.5% to 348 miles in the U.S. while keeping the $39,990 starting price unchanged.
- **Not yet confirmed:** The figures rest on this single Tesla-focused outlet's report; the item supplies no Tesla configurator listing, EPA filing or Tesla statement, and does not specify the model year affected, whether the gain comes from a hardware, software or testing change, or whether other Model Y trims received similar updates.

</div>

## Background

The Model Y RWD is the entry-level, rear-wheel-drive version of Tesla's mid-size SUV. Its EPA-estimated range is the U.S. window-sticker figure calculated under standardized test procedures, so it is meant for vehicle-to-vehicle comparison rather than as a guarantee of real-world driving distance.

## Why it matters

For U.S. buyers, the cheapest Model Y now carries about 27 more miles of EPA-estimated range for the same $39,990, which narrows the gap between the entry trim and pricier long-range versions without adding cost. Buyers should treat the 348 miles as a standardized EPA test figure rather than a guaranteed real-world highway result, and should confirm the number on Tesla's configurator at the point of order.

## What to watch

Watch for Tesla's configurator and the EPA's published fuel-economy data to confirm the 348-mile rating, and for whether other Model Y trims receive similar range updates at unchanged prices.
