New York Governor Kathy Hochul announced that the state has completed its Master Plan for Responsible Advanced Nuclear Development, a roadmap intended to reach a target of 5 GW of new nuclear generating capacity. The announcement covers the planning document and the capacity goal; it does not describe individual projects. No sites, reactor designs, construction schedules, cost estimates or funding mechanisms were included in the report of the announcement, so the 5 GW figure remains a state target rather than a committed build programme.

## Verified and unconfirmed

- **Verified:** World Nuclear News reports that Hochul announced completion of the Master Plan for Responsible Advanced Nuclear Development and that it sets a 5 GW new nuclear capacity target.
- **Not yet confirmed:** which reactor technologies or project sites the plan favours, the timeline for construction, the cost, and any siting, permitting or procurement measures — the report gives none of these details.

</div>

## Background

"Advanced nuclear" covers reactor designs beyond today's large light-water plants, including small modular reactors and designs cooled by liquid metal, gas or molten salt. The 5 GW figure is New York's stated capacity target, and the master plan is meant to set out how state agencies and outside stakeholders would reach it; the American Nuclear Society's Newswire dates the plan's introduction by Governor Hochul to November 18. The state has also been funding supporting infrastructure: World Nuclear News reported that the New York Power Authority awarded USD10 million to develop a nuclear workforce across the state.

## Why it matters

For reactor vendors and utilities eyeing New York, the plan establishes an explicit state capacity goal they can position against, but because no sites, offtake arrangements or incentive mechanisms have been named, it functions as a policy signal rather than a procurement path developers can act on yet.
