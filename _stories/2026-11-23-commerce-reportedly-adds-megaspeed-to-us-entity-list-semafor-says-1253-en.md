The US Commerce Department reportedly added Megaspeed to its Entity List on Monday, Semafor reported, citing unnamed sources. The designation would require US companies to obtain a license before selling chips to Megaspeed, which Semafor describes as the company at the center of an Nvidia-related controversy. The report did not specify the license policy's scope, any affiliated entities covered, or an effective date, and no official listing was included.

</div>

<div class="fact-check" markdown="1">

## Verified and unconfirmed

- **Verified:** Semafor reported, citing unnamed sources, that the Commerce Department added Megaspeed to its entity list on Monday, requiring licenses for US companies to sell chips to the company; the New York Times reported the same designation.
- **Not yet confirmed:** No official Commerce Department notice or entity-list entry appears in the available reporting, and Business Insider described Commerce as "preparing to blacklist" Megaspeed, leaving the formal effective date and license terms unconfirmed.

</div>

## Background

Megaspeed sits inside a controversy AI Daily Radar's November 15 digest covered: the Wall Street Journal reported that Nvidia AI chips were reaching Chinese companies through large Southeast Asian data centers that exploited a gap in US sanctions [tool-2-1]. In its November 16 digest, AI Daily Radar reported that the Commerce Department had issued an interim rule requiring export licenses for AI chips when the hardware may provide compute to China or other restricted countries, extending controls beyond physical shipments to remote access, with Bloomberg reporting the rule targeted Singapore, Malaysia, Thailand, Indonesia and Brazil [tool-2-3]. The entity list is a narrower, company-specific tool: it names firms that US suppliers may not sell to without a license.

## Why it matters

If confirmed, US chipmakers and distributors would need to apply for licenses before shipping to Megaspeed, and exporters should check whether the formal listing names affiliates or related entities, since that determines whether existing orders can proceed.

## What to watch

Watch for Commerce's formal Entity List publication or a Federal Register notice, which would confirm the designation and spell out the license requirements.
