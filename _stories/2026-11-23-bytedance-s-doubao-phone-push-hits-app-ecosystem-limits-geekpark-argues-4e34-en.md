GeekPark argues that ByteDance's Doubao phone strategy has hit an ecosystem wall: Doubao can book a Caocao ride, but it cannot tap send on a WeChat message because WeChat, Taobao and Meituan are among more than 150 apps on Doubao's October 14 list that bar the assistant from operating their pages. The only Doubao phones on sale come from ZTE's Nubia—the M153 engineering sample last December at 3,499 yuan and the NaviX Ultra in September at 5,999 yuan—and GeekPark says about 200,000 units are planned for 2026, against 57.9 million smartphones shipped in China in the third quarter, according to IDC.

ByteDance has approached Xiaomi, OPPO, vivo, Honor, Lenovo and Transsion about replacing their own assistants with Doubao, but those manufacturers have kept the system-assistant slot; a deal with another major phone maker is rumored to land in November, while ZTE on November 3 denied a reported plan to put its phone business into a joint venture. The piece uses an unconfirmed supply-chain report that Foxconn's FIH has an OpenAI phone assembly order for late 2027 or 2028 to argue that AI companies increasingly want control of both operating system and hardware, but ByteDance has not announced a phone of its own, and GeekPark notes its earlier hardware retreats from Smartisan's phone team and Pico.

## Verified and unconfirmed

- **Verified:** GeekPark reports that Doubao's phone assistant published a list on October 14 of more than 150 apps — WeChat, Taobao and Meituan among them — that do not allow it to act on their pages, and that both Doubao phones so far come from ZTE's Nubia: the M153 engineering sample (December, ¥3,499) and the NaviX Ultra (September, from ¥5,999). Separately, the November 19 Cailian Press (财联社) supply-chain report that Foxconn unit FIH Mobile won an OpenAI phone assembly order, with launch around late 2027 or 2028, was picked up by Android Authority and Gizmochina.
- **Not yet confirmed:** OpenAI has not confirmed it is building a phone, and its reported specs — a MediaTek Dimensity 9600 with dual AI processors, per TF International analyst Ming-Chi Kuo — rest on analyst and supply-chain claims. No party has confirmed any ByteDance acquisition of, or stake in, ZTE's phone business; ZTE publicly denied a joint-venture version of that plan on November 3, the reported deal with another major phone maker said to close in November is unattributed, and the ~200,000-unit 2026 stocking figure plus ZTE's evaluation of rival models come from unnamed sources.

</div>

## Background

ByteDance's phone effort began in December 2025 with the Nubia M153, a 3,499-yuan engineering sample sold in small quantities, and reached ordinary buyers in September 2026 with the Nubia NaviX Ultra from 5,999 yuan — both built by ZTE's Nubia. On the first model, the Doubao phone assistant gained a system-level Android permission called INJECT_EVENTS, which lets it tap and swipe on screen like a finger; such an assistant cannot be downloaded from an app store and must be tightly bound to the handset, occupying the slot phone makers normally reserve for their own assistants such as Xiaomi's Xiao Ai or Honor's YOYO.

## Why it matters

For users, Doubao's automation stops at the boundary of apps that have integrated with it: Caocao is reachable, but sending a WeChat message after the ride still requires the user. For ByteDance, GeekPark argues that device volume, not model quality, is the gating factor—at roughly 200,000 units planned for 2026, it lacks the leverage to bring WeChat or Taobao to the table.

## What to watch

Watch for the rumored tie-up between ByteDance and a second major phone maker, which the report says is due to be formally announced within November — and whether it covers only a single co-branded model or the system assistant itself. ZTE has already denied (November 3) a plan to bundle its handset business into a joint venture with an unnamed internet company and local state capital, so any confirmation or denial from ZTE or ByteDance would settle the separate speculation about a Nubia acquisition, while reports of more Doubao-branded Nubia models remain unconfirmed.
