electrive's Martin Westerhoff has driven the new Mercedes-Benz GLA 350 4MATIC electric, a compact all-wheel-drive SUV built around an 800-volt architecture, an 85-kWh battery and a two-speed transmission — a combination the review says delivers high efficiency and short charging stops. Everyday-practicality items include a large front luggage compartment (a "frunk"), a two-tonne towing capacity and Mercedes' new MB.OS operating system.

- **Verified:** electrive's test drive confirms the GLA 350 4MATIC electric exists, drives, and carries an 800-volt system, 85-kWh battery, two-speed transmission, front storage compartment, two-tonne towing rating and MB.OS, as reported by the reviewer who drove it.
- **Not yet confirmed:** pricing, market launch timing and availability, plus the efficiency and short-charging-stop claims, which come from a manufacturer-organised first drive rather than standardised or independent testing.

## Background

The GLA is Mercedes-Benz's smallest SUV, and this generation is offered as a fully electric model rather than a combustion-engined one, launching as the rear-drive GLA 250+ and the all-wheel-drive GLA 350 4MATIC, which produces 260 kW, reaches 100 km/h in 5.4 seconds and shares an 85 kWh nickel-manganese-cobalt battery with the 250+. An 800-volt architecture lets the pack take on high charging power at lower current than the 400-volt systems common in earlier mainstream EVs, which is the basis for the short charging stops the review describes. The GLA also runs Mercedes' new MB.OS software stack; AI Daily Radar's 17 November digest reported that Mercedes was preparing to roll out its Drive Assist Pro Level 2 assistance system to MB.OS cars in the US and Germany.

## Why it matters

An 800-volt system in a compact SUV matters because it lets the car accept high charging power for longer on chargers that can supply it — most rivals in this class still run 400-volt packs — while the two-tonne towing rating keeps the car usable for trailer duty that many electric compacts forgo. Buyers should treat both as conditional: charging speed depends on finding compatible high-power DC hardware, and the headline efficiency of a two-speed, all-wheel-drive setup still needs an independent consumption test to confirm.

## What to watch

Mercedes-Benz has not yet published pricing or launch timing for the GLA 350 4MATIC electric, so those announcements are the next checkable step. Once cars reach independent testers, measured consumption and a published charging curve will show whether the efficiency and short-stop claims from this first drive hold up.
