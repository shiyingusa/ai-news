<div class="story-lead" markdown="1">

New Harvard research, reported by Wired's Molly Taft, finds that the 403 U.S. data centers operating between May 2024 and April 2025 consumed as much electricity over the year as the entire state of Michigan. The count deliberately excludes facilities that run their own off-grid gas plants, so the total is a floor rather than a full accounting of data-center power demand. The item is a short re-report of the Wired story rather than the study itself: no total in terawatt-hours, per-facility breakdown, or methodology is given in the source, and the findings have not been independently checked.

</div>

<div class="fact-check" markdown="1">

## Verified and unconfirmed

- **Verified:** Wired reports that Harvard researchers counted 403 U.S. data centers operating between May 2024 and April 2025 whose combined annual electricity use matched Michigan's, with off-grid gas-powered facilities left out.
- **Not yet confirmed:** the underlying study's methodology, its absolute consumption figures, how facilities were identified and matched to grid data, and whether the 403 figure is a complete census — all of that rests on Wired's summary of a paper the source does not link to directly or quote in detail.

</div>

## Background

Data-center electricity use is hard to pin down because operators generally do not disclose facility-level power consumption, so estimates tend to rest on utility filings or company-wide totals rather than individual sites. The Harvard-led study, published November 16 in Nature Sustainability, instead combined satellite imagery, modeled demand and regional grid data to estimate the footprint of 403 grid-powered hyperscale data centers — the large purpose-built campuses used for cloud and AI workloads — from May 2024 to April 2025. The authors put their consumption at 68–99 terawatt-hours, nearly 2% of U.S. electricity, with an estimated 25.7 million metric tons of CO₂ attributed to the facilities.

## Why it matters

Because the tally omits sites with their own off-grid gas generation, grid-connection and utility load data used by regulators and planners will understate how much power the data-center buildout actually draws — a caveat worth carrying into any capacity forecast built on interconnection records.

## What to watch

Watch for publication of the full Harvard paper with its per-facility and state-level electricity figures, which would let outside researchers test the Michigan comparison and the effect of excluding off-grid gas plants.
