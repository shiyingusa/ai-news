<div class="story-lead" markdown="1">

Tesla has removed the entry-level all-wheel-drive version of the Model Y from its configurator, raising the cheapest way to get a dual-motor Model Y from $41,990 to $49,990 — the price of the Premium trim, which is now the AWD entry point. The rear-wheel-drive base Model Y is unchanged at $39,990. According to electrive, the discontinued trim had been on sale for just over nine months. The report does not say why Tesla made the change or whether equipment on the remaining AWD trim differs from the version it replaces.

</div>

<div class="fact-check" markdown="1">

## Verified and unconfirmed

- **Verified:** electrive reports that the base Model Y AWD no longer appears in Tesla's configurator, that AWD now starts with the Premium trim at $49,990, and that the RWD base model still starts at $39,990.
- **Not yet confirmed:** Tesla's rationale for dropping the trim, whether any official company statement exists, and whether destination fees, tax credits or equipment changes affect the comparison between the old $41,990 AWD and the $49,990 Premium AWD. The report also gives no sales or production figures for the discontinued trim.

</div>

## Background

Tesla sells the Model Y in the U.S. in a ladder of drivetrain and trim combinations: a rear-wheel-drive base model, and dual-motor all-wheel drive that until now also had a cheaper entry trim. That entry AWD version had been in the configurator for just over nine months before it was removed, leaving AWD available only from the Premium trim upward. AI Daily Radar's 15 November digest reported that the rear-wheel-drive Model Y's EPA-estimated range rose about 8.5% to 348 miles while its price stayed at $39,990 — the same figure the configurator still shows, so the change reported here is confined to the AWD side of the lineup.

## Why it matters

Buyers who want all-wheel drive now pay at least $8,000 more than before, and the AWD premium over the $39,990 rear-wheel-drive model widens from roughly $2,000 to $10,000 — a gap large enough to push some cold-weather buyers toward the RWD car or a rival brand's AWD offering, so anyone who had configured the $41,990 trim should expect to re-quote before ordering.

## What to watch

Watch Tesla's US configurator for whether a cheaper dual-motor option returns, and the company's next quarterly delivery report for whether the trim change shifts the mix toward rear-wheel drive.
