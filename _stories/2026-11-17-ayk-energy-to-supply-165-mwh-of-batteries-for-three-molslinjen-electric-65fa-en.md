AYK Energy is supplying battery systems totalling 165 MWh for three new all-electric fast ferries operated by Danish shipping company Molslinjen, with 55 MWh installed on each vessel. The catamaran ferries are being built by Incat Tasmania, the Australian yard known for large aluminium fast-ferry hulls. The report gives no contract value, delivery schedule, battery chemistry or charging specification, so the order is a disclosed supply agreement rather than a system already in service.

## Verified and unconfirmed

- **Verified:** electrive reports that AYK Energy will supply 165 MWh of battery systems in total, split into 55 MWh per vessel, for three all-electric catamaran ferries operated by Molslinjen and built by Incat Tasmania.
- **Not yet confirmed:** delivery timing, contract value, battery chemistry and cell suppliers, charging power and shore-side infrastructure, and the routes or in-service dates for the vessels; these rest on the trade report alone, with no independent confirmation or technical datasheet.

</div>

## Background

Marine battery installations on commercial vessels must be type-approved by a classification society such as DNV before they can enter service, and the packs in this order are AYK Energy's DNV type-approved Polaris system, according to trade-press coverage of the deal. That certification carries extra weight because these Incat-built catamarans are all-electric: the battery is the vessel's sole power source rather than a hybrid supplement, so the 55 MWh on each ship has to cover the full propulsion load.

## Why it matters

At 55 MWh per vessel, this order sits at the large end of published marine battery packages for fast ferries, so the vessels' viability will hinge less on pack capacity than on the charging power and grid connection available at each terminal — the details AYK and Molslinjen have not yet published. Operators and port authorities planning similar crossings should treat shore-side electrical capacity as the binding constraint when sizing comparable ships.

## What to watch

Watch for Incat Tasmania's delivery of the first hull and Molslinjen's announced in-service date, plus any published figures for charging power at the terminals the ferries will use. No dates for either have been disclosed so far.
