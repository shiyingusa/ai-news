OpenAI said it is expanding its ChatGPT for Academic Researchers program so that 200,000 researchers worldwide can use its frontier models free of charge. The post presents the change as a broadening of an existing program rather than a new model release, and gives no indication that the models themselves are new.

It does not specify which models are covered, how researchers qualify or apply, when access begins, or whether 200,000 is a cap or a target.

</div>

<div class="fact-check" markdown="1">

## Verified and unconfirmed

- **Verified:** OpenAI's own announcement states the company is expanding its ChatGPT for Academic Researchers program to give 200,000 researchers worldwide free access to its frontier models (tool-2-1). OpenAI's earlier program page for the initiative, dated July 29, 2026, said participants receive free access across ChatGPT, ChatGPT Work and Codex — including the GPT-5.6 family at launch — plus expanded deep research, higher usage limits and larger context windows (tool-2-2).
- **Not yet confirmed:** The mechanics of the expansion rest mainly on a single outlet, Semafor, which reports 50,000 new grants awarded first-come, first-served, each recipient able to extend access to up to two collaborators (tool-2-3); it also describes the figure as "up to 200,000," whereas OpenAI's page states 200,000 flatly, and neither source makes clear whether that number is cumulative across the program or newly added. OpenAI's announcement gives no eligibility criteria, country list, application window, expiry date or which model versions the expanded cohort will receive.

</div>

## Background

ChatGPT for Academic Researchers is an existing OpenAI program that gives academics free use of its models rather than requiring them to pay for individual or institutional subscriptions. This announcement changes its scale, extending that free access to 200,000 researchers worldwide and explicitly covering OpenAI's frontier models, its most capable models rather than a smaller or older tier. OpenAI's notice is a single sentence: it does not say how researchers apply or prove eligibility, which specific models are included, or how long the access lasts.

## Why it matters

Researchers who qualify would get frontier-model access without paying, which matters most for labs, institutions, or regions with limited budgets. Because OpenAI has not published eligibility rules, application steps, or usage limits, individual researchers cannot yet tell whether they qualify or what restrictions would apply to their work.
