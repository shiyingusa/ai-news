<div class="story-lead" markdown="1">

NPR reported that internal Meta documents describe a 2024 initiative, internally called "Project Gryffindor," aimed at recruiting new teen Instagram users through schools — including school directories, banners and "school blast" notifications. The documents surfaced as part of a group of child-safety lawsuits against Meta, and include a November 2023 internal presentation stating that a school program would "give teens a new reason to join / find more value in IG" and "fuel organic and unattributed acquisition" of users. The reporting indicates Meta's publicly framed effort to make Instagram safer for schools grew out of work whose internal goals also covered "acquisition," "friending" and "invites." The material available does not specify which of these tactics actually launched, how many schools took part, or Meta's response to the report.

</div>

<div class="fact-check" markdown="1">

## Verified and unconfirmed

- **Verified:** NPR, in reporting summarized by The Verge, cites internal Meta documents — including a November 2023 presentation — that describe "Project Gryffindor," a school program intended to drive teen "acquisition" and give teens "a new reason to join" Instagram; the documents were disclosed as part of child-safety litigation against Meta and are posted on DocumentCloud.
- **Not yet confirmed:** Which elements of the plan (school directories, banners, "school blast" notifications) were actually deployed and at what scale, whether schools and parents were told about the acquisition goals, and Meta's own account of the program — the available reporting contains no company statement or response.

</div>

## Background

The internal documents surfaced in litigation: Meta is defending more than 1,400 lawsuits filed by U.S. school districts alleging that Instagram, Facebook and other platforms were deliberately engineered to addict children, disrupt classrooms and burden educators. Separately, in August 2026 Meta reached a tentative $17 billion settlement with nearly all U.S. states in a child-safety case that would also commit the company to sweeping restrictions on teens' use of its apps, as reported by Politico and NPR.

## Why it matters

Schools that join Instagram's School Partnership Program now have a documented reason to ask what commitments they signed up to: the internal documents frame the program around user growth rather than safety alone, so administrators and parents evaluating or already enrolled in such partnerships should ask whether their school directory data was shared and under what terms, and whether the growth objectives were disclosed when the school agreed to participate.

## What to watch

Watch for Meta's response to NPR's reporting and for further documents entered into the child-safety lawsuits, which would show whether the school-directory and "school blast" tactics were carried out as planned.
