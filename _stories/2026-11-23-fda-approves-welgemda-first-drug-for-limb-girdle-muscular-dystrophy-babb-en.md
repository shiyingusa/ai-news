<div class="story-lead" markdown="1">

The FDA on Monday approved BridgeBio's Welgemda (ribitol), the first drug cleared to treat limb-girdle muscular dystrophy — a broad collection of progressive muscle conditions that the report notes has long drawn little attention from physicians and drug developers. The approval is narrow: it covers only patients with the LGMD 2i/R9 subtype, which BridgeBio estimates affects 2,000 to 3,000 people in the U.S. and another 4,000 to 5,000 in Europe. BridgeBio said it will charge insurers $75,446.58 for a 28-day supply, putting the annual price just under $1 million. The report does not include the trial data behind the approval.

## Verified and unconfirmed

- **Verified:** STAT reported that the FDA approved BridgeBio's Welgemda (ribitol) on Monday as the first drug for limb-girdle muscular dystrophy, specifically the LGMD 2i/R9 subtype; BridgeBio said it will charge insurers $75,446.58 per 28-day supply, just under $1 million a year, and estimates 2,000–3,000 U.S. patients and 4,000–5,000 in Europe. BridgeBio's NDA for the same subtype was submitted in late March 2026 and accepted with priority review in late May 2026, per company announcements.
- **Not yet confirmed:** The STAT+ excerpt is truncated and carries no trial data, so the magnitude of benefit, the label's age range and any post-marketing requirements, the U.S. launch date, and actual insurer coverage remain unclear; the $75,446.58 figure is a list price before any rebates or discounts, and the patient counts are company estimates rather than registry counts.

</div>

## Background

Limb-girdle muscular dystrophy is a broad collection of progressive muscle conditions that had no FDA-approved treatment before this decision. The approval is narrowly scoped to LGMD 2i/R9, a subtype BridgeBio estimates affects 2,000 to 3,000 patients in the U.S. and 4,000 to 5,000 in Europe.

## Why it matters

For LGMD 2i/R9 patients there was previously no approved disease-directed treatment, so the practical question now shifts to access: whether insurers will cover a therapy priced at just under $1 million a year for a population of a few thousand people in the U.S., and what prior authorization or eligibility criteria they attach to it.

## What to watch

Watch for payer coverage policies and any BridgeBio disclosure of initial patient starts on the drug, which would show whether the approval translates into treatment for the estimated 2,000 to 3,000 U.S. patients with this subtype.
