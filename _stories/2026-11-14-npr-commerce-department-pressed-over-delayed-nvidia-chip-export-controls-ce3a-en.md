NPR reports that the US Commerce Department is facing pressure for delaying export controls on Nvidia chips, according to a headline carried in a chip-industry news feed. The supplied material contains only that headline: it names no officials applying the pressure, no date for the delay, no indication of which specific controls are affected, and no response from Commerce or Nvidia. It should therefore be treated as a reported, developing policy story rather than a confirmed change to export rules. No details on scope, timing, or the legal mechanism involved could be verified from the available content.

</div>

## Background

US curbs on advanced AI chip exports are run through the Commerce Department, whose Bureau of Industry and Security keeps the Entity List: once a firm is on it, US suppliers need a license before selling to it. The pressure on Commerce follows reporting that the department held back adding the cloud-computing firm Megaspeed to that list, with Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent publicly blaming Commerce for Nvidia chip sales to China. CNBC also reported that Jensen Huang, who resigned as Nvidia CEO amid the Megaspeed scandal, had pushed to ease restrictions on exporting advanced AI chips to China.

## Enforcement exposure

For companies that ship or buy high-end AI chips, the immediate effect is that the existing licensing regime stays in place while the new controls are held up — and the delay has become a cabinet-level dispute, with Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent publicly blaming Howard Lutnick's Commerce Department for failing to properly oversee exports of high-spec AI chips. The department's enforcement record is also under separate scrutiny, with the New York Times reporting that a Commerce Department inquiry into Megaspeed was halted after Nvidia intervened, a precedent that raises questions about how firmly any new rule would be applied once issued.
