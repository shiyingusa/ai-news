<div class="story-lead" markdown="1">

Toyota is expanding a recall of its electric C-HR SUV to about 19,000 vehicles globally, with roughly 10,000 of the affected cars in the United States, Electrek reported. The report describes the action as an expansion of an existing recall rather than a new one, but does not state the defect being addressed, the remedy, or when owners will be notified. No US regulator filing or Toyota statement detailing the campaign is cited in the report.

</div>

<div class="fact-check" markdown="1">

## Verified and unconfirmed

- **Verified:** Electrek reported that Toyota is recalling about 19,000 C-HR EVs worldwide, roughly 10,000 of them in the US, and framed it as an expansion of a prior recall.
- **Not yet confirmed:** the defect that triggered the recall, the fix, the notification schedule, and whether Toyota or NHTSA has formally confirmed the 19,000 and 10,000 figures — the report gives no cause, remedy, recall number, or regulator filing.

</div>

## Background

In September 2026 Toyota recalled about 10,000 C-HR EVs from the 2026 model year in North America over batteries that could be overcharged, Car and Driver reported. AI Daily Radar's November 17 digest reported the recall had reached roughly 19,000 C-HR+ vehicles worldwide, including 1,096 in Germany, attributing it to a battery management system fault that in the worst case could shut down the electric drivetrain while the car is moving; that report did not give model years, build dates or whether the fix is software or hardware.

## Why it matters

Owners of the roughly 10,000 US cars should watch for a Toyota recall notice and check their vehicle identification number against Toyota's or NHTSA's recall lookup, since the report does not say whether the remedy is a software update or a hardware replacement — a distinction that determines whether the car needs a dealer visit.

## What to watch

Toyota's US recall filing with NHTSA, which would specify the defect, the remedy and the affected build dates, is the next checkable document; neither it nor a Toyota statement appears in the report.
