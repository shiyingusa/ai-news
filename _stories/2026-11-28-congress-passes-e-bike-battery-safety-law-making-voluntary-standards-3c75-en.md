Congress has passed legislation that would turn what have largely been voluntary US e-bike battery safety standards into mandatory federal requirements, Electrek reports. The change would apply to the e-bike batteries and bikes sold in the US, shifting the burden of safety compliance from voluntary industry practice to federal law.

The report does not name the specific standards involved, the effective date, the penalties for non-compliance, or whether the bill has yet been signed into law; it describes the requirements only as "on the verge" of becoming mandatory.

## Background

US e-bike battery safety has rested largely on voluntary standards — chiefly Underwriters Laboratories' testing criteria — which manufacturers could adopt or skip. The Consumer Product Safety Commission published a proposed rule (16 CFR Part 1265) on June 24, 2026 that would make lithium-ion battery safety standards mandatory for e-bikes and other micromobility devices, and the Senate separately passed a bill directing the agency to issue a final rule adopting UL standards within 180 days of enactment. The legislation Congress has now passed follows that second, legislative track.

## Why it matters

If the requirements take effect as described, US e-bike sellers and importers could no longer rely on voluntary compliance, and would instead need to show their batteries meet a federal standard before selling them — a certification and testing burden that falls hardest on smaller brands and direct-to-consumer importers.
