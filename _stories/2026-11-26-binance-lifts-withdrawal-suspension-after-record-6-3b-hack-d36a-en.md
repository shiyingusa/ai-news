Binance said on Thursday that its recovery work is complete and that it has fully lifted the withdrawal suspension it imposed after a record $6.3 billion was stolen in a hack last month, Reuters reported. The step restores normal withdrawal access for customers of the exchange, which had blocked withdrawals while it worked through the aftermath of the theft. The report gives no figure for how much of the stolen funds was recovered, no account of whether customer balances were made whole, and no detail on what security changes were made.

## Verified and unconfirmed

- **Verified:** Binance said on Thursday that its withdrawal recovery work is complete and that it has lifted the withdrawal suspension, following a hack last month in which a record $6.3 billion was stolen, Reuters reported.
- **Not yet confirmed:** how much of the $6.3 billion has been recovered, whether customer funds were fully covered, what caused the breach and what security changes followed — the report does not address these, and the figures rest on Binance's own statement.

</div>

## Background

Suspending withdrawals is the standard first response to a large exchange breach: it stops stolen funds from being moved off the platform and gives the exchange time to reconcile customer balances and rebuild custody controls. The theft was reported last month as a record $6.3 billion loss, and Binance's statement covers only the restoration of withdrawal service, not how much of the money was recovered. The incident has already produced legal fallout: AI Daily Radar's November 23 digest reported that the US Department of Justice was seeking forfeiture of more than $1 billion in cryptocurrency that hackers linked to North Korea stole from Binance, according to a court filing Bloomberg reported, though that report did not establish whether those funds are part of the same theft.

## Why it matters

Customers who had funds stranded on Binance can now move them again, but the announcement does not say whether withdrawal limits, processing delays or chain-specific restrictions remain, so users should confirm the terms on their own accounts before depending on the exchange for liquidity.

## What to watch

Watch for Binance's next detailed statement or an independent audit breaking down how much of the $6.3 billion was recovered and whether customer balances were made whole.
