Verizon is in talks to acquire Fidium, a fiber-optic broadband operator owned by Searchlight Capital and Canada's BCI, for about $6 billion, the Financial Times reported, citing unnamed sources. The move is described as part of Verizon's stated goal of extending its network to 50 million US homes and businesses, with the acquisition aimed at strengthening its position in New England.

The report describes negotiations, not a signed agreement: neither the price nor the deal itself has been confirmed by Verizon, Fidium or its owners, and talks could still fall through.

<div class="fact-check" markdown="1">

## Verified and unconfirmed

- **Verified:** The Financial Times reported, based on unnamed sources, that Verizon is in talks to buy Fidium, a fiber broadband operator owned by Searchlight Capital and BCI, in a deal framed around Verizon's 50-million-premises expansion target.
- **Not yet confirmed:** The ~$6 billion figure, the stage of the negotiations and whether any agreement will be reached; none of Verizon, Fidium, Searchlight or BCI has publicly confirmed the talks, and no filing, timeline or deal terms have been disclosed.

</div>

## Background

Fidium Fiber is the consumer fiber brand of Consolidated Communications, which rebranded under the Fidium name in 2025 and advertises residential speeds of up to 8,000 Mbps — a tier CNET's ISP rankings note that few competitors match. The company is owned by private-equity firm Searchlight Capital and Canada's BCI, which would be the sellers in the reported talks.

## Why it matters

If completed, the deal would move Fidium's fiber customers in New England under Verizon, consolidating a regional fiber provider into one of the largest US carriers — a transaction of this size would also face antitrust and FCC review before any customer-facing change, so subscribers should expect the ownership question to be settled by regulators long before service terms change.

## What to watch

Watch for a definitive agreement announcement from Verizon or Searchlight/BCI, which would be followed by regulatory filings with the FCC or antitrust authorities revealing the actual purchase price and conditions.
