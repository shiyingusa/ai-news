Senators Josh Hawley and Dick Durbin have introduced legislation intended to make it easier for families to sue if their child has been harmed through Roblox, following a Senate hearing that examined child sexual abuse on the gaming platform, WIRED reports. The bill is a response to allegations, aired at the hearing, that predators use the platform to reach children. The source does not name the bill, describe its legal mechanism, say whether it targets only Roblox or online platforms more broadly, or indicate whether it has any co-sponsors or scheduled vote. It has been introduced, not passed, so no family's ability to sue has changed yet.

## Background

Senators Josh Hawley and Dick Durbin have pursued child-safety legislation before: both are sponsors of the proposed STOP CSAM Act (Strengthening Transparency and Obligations to Protect Children Suffering from Abuse and Mistreatment Act), aimed at child sexual abuse material. The Roblox measure follows a joint Hawley–Durbin investigation into the platform, which the senators said was being used to trade abuse material, extort children into producing explicit content, and lure them into in-person meetings; Hawley chaired the hearing at which survivors and experts testified.

## Why it matters

If enacted, the bill would give parents a clearer legal route against a platform over harm to a child — a form of liability exposure that gaming and social platforms have largely avoided — so its drafting, particularly whether it names Roblox alone or covers user-generated platforms generally, will determine how many companies and developers it affects.

## What to watch

Watch for the release of the bill's text and any committee markup or hearing, which will show what legal standard it sets and whether it applies to platforms beyond Roblox.
