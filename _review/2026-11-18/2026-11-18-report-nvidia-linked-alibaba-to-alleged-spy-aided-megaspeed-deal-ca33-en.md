<div class="story-lead" markdown="1">

The Information, in an item aggregated by Techmeme, reports that Nvidia's sales team made two separate introductions in 2022 that connected Alibaba with Alice Huang — described in the report as an alleged Chinese spy involved in smuggling Nvidia chips into China — and that Nvidia actively helped Megaspeed win Alibaba as a customer. The report presents these introductions as part of workarounds to U.S. export controls on advanced AI chips. The account rests on unnamed sources cited by The Information; the Techmeme item is a short summary of a paywalled article and contains no on-the-record confirmation from Nvidia, Alibaba, Megaspeed or Huang, and no response from any of them.

</div>

<div class="fact-check" markdown="1">

## Verified and unconfirmed

- **Verified:** The Information's report, as summarized by Techmeme, states that Nvidia's sales team made two separate introductions in 2022 connecting Alibaba with Alice Huang, and that Nvidia actively helped Megaspeed — the Singapore-based datacenter company where Huang is an executive — land Alibaba as a customer. Earlier reporting (The Guardian) describes Huang as an alleged Chinese spy who had previously been suspected of smuggling Nvidia chips into China, prompting US surveillance.
- **Not yet confirmed:** The account of Nvidia's role rests entirely on unnamed sources cited by The Information; the supplied material contains no on-record response from Nvidia, Alibaba or Megaspeed, no documents, and no detail on what the 2022 introductions covered or whether any chip sales followed. The espionage allegation against Huang is attributed to US officials and has not been substantiated in the supplied material; a critical commentary piece (Geopolitical Economy) argues the intelligence claims were made without public evidence.

</div>

## Background

The item sits on top of a widening U.S. export-control campaign. AI Daily Radar's Nov. 15 digest summarized a Wall Street Journal investigation that Nvidia chips were reaching Chinese firms through Southeast Asian data centers exploiting a gap in sanctions and providing cover for alleged smuggling; the Nov. 16 digest then covered a Commerce Department interim rule requiring licenses when AI hardware may supply compute to China or other restricted countries, extending controls to remote access. The Information's report adds specific alleged 2022 sales introductions—connecting Alibaba with Alice Huang and helping Megaspeed land Alibaba—to that enforcement backdrop.

## Why it matters

The report moves scrutiny from individual smuggling cases onto Nvidia's own sales channel: Nvidia's board has formed a committee to oversee national security and export controls, so how the company documents 2022-era introductions like these will now be measured against an internal oversight body, not just outside investigators. The downstream exposure is already visible in the numbers — Megaspeed imported over $4.6 billion in Nvidia GPUs while only a small fraction is visible at its Southeast Asian sites, per Cryptopolitan — meaning distributors and resellers that Nvidia introduces to buyers carry enforcement risk that can surface years later. Nvidia has not responded to the allegations in the supplied material.

## What to watch

Whether Nvidia, Alibaba, Megaspeed or Huang publicly respond to the report, and whether U.S. export-control enforcers at the Commerce Department open or confirm any inquiry into the described introductions.
