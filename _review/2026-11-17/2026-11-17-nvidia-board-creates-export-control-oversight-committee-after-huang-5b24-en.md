Nvidia's board has established a standing National Security and Export Control Oversight Committee to oversee the company's national security policies, export-control compliance and global trade risk, the company announced. Nvidia's National Security Team and Global Trade Compliance Team will report directly to the committee and to interim CEO Colette Kress, who took over after Jensen Huang resigned less than a week earlier, according to Tom's Hardware. Tom's Hardware reported that the resignation followed a scandal involving Huang's relationship with a woman alleged to be a Chinese spy and her reported role in the import of about $7.5 billion of banned Nvidia chips into China; those allegations were not independently confirmed in the supplied material. The announcement did not name committee members, set a meeting schedule or describe any specific change to Nvidia's export-compliance procedures.

## Verified and unconfirmed

- **Verified:** Nvidia’s board announced a standing National Security and Export Control Oversight Committee, with the company’s National Security Team and Global Trade Compliance Team reporting to it and interim CEO Colette Kress, according to Nvidia’s announcement as reported by Tom’s Hardware; PC Gamer, Yahoo Finance and CNBC corroborate that Jensen Huang resigned and Kress became interim CEO.
- **Not yet confirmed:** The claims that Alice Huang was a Chinese spy and helped move roughly $7.5 billion of banned Nvidia chips, mostly H100 and H200, into China are presented as reports and allegations, not proven facts; the supplied material provides no court or regulatory finding, and the committee’s actual authority is not detailed.

</div>

## Background

U.S. export controls on Nvidia's data-center chips date to 2022, when the A100 and H100 were restricted, pushing Nvidia to create the China-market A800 and H800; tighter limits in 2023 produced the further cut-down H20, and the Trump administration blocked H20 exports to China from April to June 2025. Scrutiny escalated in the week before the board's move: AI Daily Radar's November 15 digest reported a Wall Street Journal investigation into Nvidia chips reaching Chinese firms through Southeast Asian data centers, and its November 16 digest reported a Commerce Department interim rule requiring export licenses when AI chips may supply compute to China or other restricted countries, including through remote access rather than physical shipment.

## Why it matters

For Nvidia's customers, distributors and supply-chain partners, export-compliance decisions now run through a standing board committee and the interim CEO, so export-sensitive orders and end-use reviews may face more senior scrutiny. The committee's creation does not itself change any U.S. export license or rule; it changes who inside Nvidia oversees compliance with existing controls.

## What to watch

Watch whether Jensen Huang appears before the House China, Foreign Affairs and Intelligence committees for the requested closed-door briefing on Nvidia's security protocols, and whether the FBI inquiry mentioned by President Donald Trump leads to any public action.
