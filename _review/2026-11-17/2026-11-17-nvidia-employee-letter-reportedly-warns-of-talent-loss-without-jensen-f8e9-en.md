An internal letter reportedly sent to roughly 60 direct reports and Nvidia's 10-member board warns that failing to reinstate former CEO Jensen Huang could cost the company “irreplaceable talent,” according to the Wall Street Journal. The letter, attributed to director of developer technology Nader Khalil, says that talent “is now contemplating departing.” Nvidia's board is reportedly not considering Huang's return despite employee petitions, support from Silicon Valley executives and President Trump, and Huang's statement that he would be honored to return; he resigned last week amid reports of affairs and allegations that one relationship may have facilitated chip smuggling into China. Huang is scheduled to appear before a House Foreign Affairs panel on December 1.

</div>

## Verified and unconfirmed

- **Verified:** Jensen Huang resigned as Nvidia CEO last week amid reports of extramarital relationships, and has since said he would be honored to return; Nvidia's board is reported to be not considering reinstatement while a national-security inquiry proceeds. Huang is scheduled to appear before a House Foreign Affairs panel on December 1, with Nvidia general counsel Tim Teter testifying the following day, both in closed-door sessions with transcripts later released (CNN). A public reinstatement petition had more than 165,000 signatories as of November 16 (The Guardian).
- **Not yet confirmed:** The internal letter and its warning that "irreplaceable talent at Nvidia is now contemplating departing" rest on The Wall Street Journal's account of a document it says it saw but which has not been published; the Journal names developer-technology director Nader Khalil as author and board chairman Harvey Jones as recipient, with roughly 60 direct reports and all 10 directors copied (ZeroHedge summary of the Journal). No employee departures have been confirmed, and the article offers no figure for how many might leave. The underlying allegations — that Alice Huang was a spy or that the relationship eased smuggling of banned chips into China — remain unproven; the open letter's signatories say they have been shown no evidence, and Tom's Hardware asserts US intelligence lacks solid evidence without citing a source.

</div>

## Background

Jensen Huang resigned as Nvidia CEO last week after reports of an affair with Megaspeed executive Alice Huang and a second affair with an Nvidia vice president, plus allegations the first relationship may have facilitated chip smuggling into China. AI Daily Radar's November 15 digest reported that The Wall Street Journal had traced Nvidia AI chips to Chinese firms through large Southeast Asian data centers exploiting a gap in U.S. sanctions — an arrangement the investigation framed as a multibillion-dollar business built during Huang's tenure. Huang has said he would be honored to return and is scheduled to appear before a House Foreign Affairs panel on December 1; the board is reportedly not considering reinstatement despite the employee letter, an open petition at standwithjensen.com and public backing from President Trump.

## Why it matters

Nvidia's board now faces a retention risk in the engineering teams behind its GPU architectures and software: the internal letter says key staff are already contemplating departure, and the board has reportedly not offered the evidence-based explanation that employees are demanding.

## What to watch

Huang is scheduled to appear before a House Foreign Affairs panel on December 1. Also watch whether Nvidia's board responds formally to the letter and whether departures follow.
