A CBS News investigation, re-reported by Tom's Hardware, alleges that two California-based customers buying Nvidia AI chips are Chinese-owned companies operating as a workaround for U.S. export controls. RiCloud AI is owned by Shenzhen-based Yuxing InfoTech Investment Holdings, whose CEO and co-owner Cong Yu reportedly holds a degree from the PLA Command College; Airvres, listed as an "elite" partner on Nvidia's website, was previously called Inspur and changed its name after Inspur-related firms were added to a federal blocklist in March 2023.

Airvres reportedly exported at least $5.6 billion of chips to Southeast Asia between April 2024 and February 2026, including top-of-the-line Blackwell silicon, shipping to two Malaysian companies — one of them Megaspeed, which is under criminal investigation and was recently blacklisted. Nvidia is barred from selling its most advanced AI chips to China but may still sell gaming graphics cards and less capable AI chips there; the report describes chips being sold to U.S. entities and then leased or shipped onward to Southeast Asia.

## Verified and unconfirmed

- **Verified:** A CBS News investigation, cited by Tom's Hardware and the New York Post, names two California-registered Nvidia customers — Aivres and RiCloud AI — as Chinese-controlled; Aivres previously carried the Inspur name and was renamed after Inspur-linked firms were added to a federal blocklist in March 2023, while RiCloud is owned by Shenzhen-based Yuxing InfoTech, whose CEO Cong Yu holds a degree from the PLA Command College. According to the reporting, Aivres exported at least $5.6 billion of chips to Southeast Asia between April 2024 and February 2026, including Blackwell silicon, and is listed as an "elite" partner on Nvidia's website.
- **Not yet confirmed:** That any of this broke export law — no charges, indictments or regulatory findings against Aivres, RiCloud or Nvidia are reported, and Nvidia's response is not given in these accounts. The outlets also disagree on the company's name ("Aivres" in CBS and the New York Post, "Airvres" in Tom's Hardware), and details such as the $598 million in data-center commitments attributed to a whisky importer are single-report claims without independent verification.

</div>

## Background

The U.S. Entity List, the Commerce Department roster relevant to these blacklistings, requires suppliers to obtain a license before exporting controlled goods—including advanced AI chips—to listed entities. AI Daily Radar's November 24 digest, citing Bloomberg, reported that Megaspeed, a Singapore-linked distributor named in the CBS News report as a recipient of Airvres chip shipments, was added to that list along with Alibaba's Apex Enterprise Solutions. Earlier, AI Daily Radar's November 15 digest reported that the Wall Street Journal had described Nvidia chips reaching Chinese firms through Southeast Asian data centers that exploited a sanctions gap—the broader routing pattern the CBS report now ties to named California-registered buyers.

## Why it matters

Nvidia's own partner directory — which lists Airvres as an "elite" partner — is being used as a due-diligence signal by buyers and distributors, so a listing there does not by itself establish that a counterparty is outside restricted ownership; procurement and compliance teams checking U.S. customers of export-controlled accelerators should verify ultimate ownership rather than partner tier. The allegation also points at the onward-shipment route through Malaysia, which is where enforcement attention would fall next.

## What to watch

Whether the U.S. Commerce Department's Bureau of Industry and Security adds RiCloud AI, Airvres or their parent firms to the Entity List, and how the criminal investigation into Megaspeed — named as a recipient of Airvres shipments — resolves.
