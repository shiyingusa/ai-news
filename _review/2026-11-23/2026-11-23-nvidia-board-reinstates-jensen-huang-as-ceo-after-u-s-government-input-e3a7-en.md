Nvidia's board voted on Monday to reinstate Jensen Huang as president, CEO and board chairman, effective immediately, 11 days after he resigned amid multiple scandals. In its announcement, the company said the decision was based in part on "input from senior levels of the United States government" assuring the board that Huang's reinstatement "poses no national security risk." The White House confirmed it was involved: communications director Steven Cheung said President Trump "was totally involved" and that "we have to protect our geniuses," while the New York Times, citing two people familiar with the matter, reported that Trump directed an effort led by JD Vance to push for the reinstatement, and Semafor reported that Trump dialed into the board meeting to argue for Huang's return. The reversal came despite Huang's admitted affair with a woman U.S. intelligence sources suspect was working as a Chinese spy, and allegations that Nvidia subverted export controls meant to keep its chips out of China; the Financial Times reported that an inquiry into his conduct is ongoing and that the share price soared on the news.

## Verified and unconfirmed

- **
- **Verified:** ** Nvidia's own announcement states that its board reinstated Jensen Huang as President, CEO and board member, effective immediately, on Nov. 23, 2026, and that the board received input from "senior levels of the United States government" before concluding his return "poses no national security risk" (Nvidia press release, tool-2-1). The Guardian and the Los Angeles Times both report he had resigned earlier in November after reports of extramarital affairs, including one with a Singapore-based businesswoman U.S. intelligence suspects of being a Chinese spy (tool-2-2, tool-2-3). White House communications director Steven Cheung said on the record that Trump "was totally involved" and that "we have to protect our geniuses" (The Verge, citing Semafor).

**
- **Not yet confirmed:** ** The claim that Trump personally dialed into the board meeting rests on a single Semafor source, while a Vance-led White House push is attributed only to unnamed people familiar with the matter (NYT). The espionage suspicion, the alleged export-control subversion and the state of the multiple reported investigations are allegations or open questions, not established findings, and Trump's assertion that a "very thorough investigation" cleared Huang has not been documented. Outlets also disagree on the resignation date — Nov. 12 per the Los Angeles Times versus Nov. 13 per The Guardian — and no source in hand gives the size of the reported share-price jump.

## Background

The reinstatement reverses a resignation announced 11 days earlier, after Huang admitted an affair with a woman U.S. intelligence sources suspect was a Chinese spy, and as Nvidia faced allegations that it subverted export controls meant to keep its chips out of China. AI Daily Radar's November 15 digest covered the Wall Street Journal report at the center of that scrutiny, which said Nvidia chips were reaching Chinese firms through large Southeast Asian data centers that exploited a gap in U.S. sanctions. Multiple government investigations into the company's national-security, accounting and governance conduct were still developing when the board voted.

## Why it matters

The board's own statement makes a U.S. government assurance about national security the stated basis for a CEO appointment, a precedent that effectively subjects board authority over Nvidia's leadership to executive-branch approval. For investors and employees, the open question is not the vote but the unresolved investigations: the FT reports a probe into Huang's conduct is still ongoing, and the same conduct remains the subject of inquiries by multiple government departments, so the reinstatement does not close the legal or export-control exposure that triggered the resignation.

## What to watch

Watch the developing government investigations into Huang's conduct and the export-control allegations for a first public finding, charging decision or formal closure, since the board's action settled the leadership question but not the inquiries behind it.

## Community discussion

The most substantive thread argues that Nvidia, like OpenAI in 2023, shows a company's nominal governance structure is not its real one: the board was empowered to remove the CEO and did so, but other forces overruled it. Other commenters push back on the implication of wrongdoing, noting that intelligence briefings have reportedly not established that the woman was a spy and that no evidence of a crime has surfaced, and single out the board's line about government "input" as the operative part of the statement.
