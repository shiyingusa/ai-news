An Amazon delivery drone dropped off a shampoo order in Huntington Woods, Michigan, on November 20, then lost a propeller when it caught on a fiber-optic line and drifted sideways until it came to rest wedged between a neighbor's garage roof and some power lines, according to WXYZ 7 News Detroit. The customer had been told the package would arrive by drone within seven minutes. The US Federal Aviation Administration has opened an investigation into the crash. No injuries were reported, and the report gives no indication of the damage done to the drone, the roof or the cable.

</div>

<div class="fact-check" markdown="1">

## Verified and unconfirmed

- **Verified:** WXYZ 7 News Detroit reported the November 20 sequence — package dropped in a backyard, propeller lost to a fiber-optic line, drone ending up pinned between a garage roof and power lines — and Ars Technica reported that the FAA is investigating.
- **Not yet confirmed:** The report quotes no FAA statement, so the scope and focus of the investigation are unknown; Amazon has not commented publicly on the cause; the extent of damage and whether the drone's flight-log data has been recovered are not established.

</div>

## Background

Amazon has been expanding its Prime Air drone-delivery operations across the US, alongside other companies pursuing aerial package drops. Ars Technica describes the Michigan crash as the latest delivery-drone mishap caught on video, and the episode points to the overhead infrastructure—power lines and fiber-optic cables—that low-altitude delivery routes must avoid.

## Why it matters

Amazon and other operators are expanding drone delivery across the US, and this is at least the latest in a run of mishaps captured on video, so the FAA's findings will shape how much autonomy these aircraft are allowed over residential streets. For residents in active delivery zones, the practical concern is the return leg after a drop, where a damaged drone can drift into wiring rather than fall clear of people and property.

## What to watch

Watch for the FAA's findings and for any Amazon Prime Air statement on the cause or on changes to operations in the Detroit area; neither has been issued with a timeline.
