Amazon launched 36 more satellites for Amazon Leo, its low-Earth-orbit broadband constellation, on an Ariane 6 rocket Tuesday, raising the fleet to 432 after 40 satellites were lost earlier in November when United Launch Alliance's Vulcan rocket failed. An Amazon official told Ars Technica the company is accelerating several Falcon 9 launches it has purchased from SpaceX, which owns the rival Starlink network. The next Amazon Leo Falcon 9 flight is now planned for December, moved forward from next year, with more missions to follow in rapid succession.

## Verified and unconfirmed

- **Verified:** Ars Technica reported that the Ariane 6 mission placed the 36 satellites in orbit and that Amazon counts 432 satellites in the constellation; the December Falcon 9 schedule was attributed to an Amazon official.
- **Not yet confirmed:** the exact December launch date, how many additional Falcon 9 missions will fly and when, whether all 432 satellites are operational, and the cause or return-to-flight timing for Vulcan.

</div>

## Background

Amazon Leo is Amazon's low-Earth-orbit broadband constellation, built to compete with SpaceX's Starlink, and Amazon had booked 38 Vulcan launches from United Launch Alliance as its primary route to orbit. AI Daily Radar's November 16 digest reported that the first of those Vulcan flights failed about ten minutes after liftoff on November 15, destroying its 40 Amazon Leo satellites, with ULA saying it had no specific data yet on the cause. That loss left Amazon reliant on other rockets — Europe's Ariane 6 and, notably, SpaceX's Falcon 9 — to keep the constellation's build-out on schedule.

## Why it matters

With Vulcan grounded, Amazon's near-term constellation growth depends partly on launches from SpaceX, the owner of direct rival Starlink; the December Falcon 9 mission is the first concrete test of whether that purchased capacity can keep Leo's deployment on schedule.

## What to watch

Whether the first Amazon Leo Falcon 9 mission lifts off in December as planned, and when ULA announces a return-to-flight date for Vulcan.
