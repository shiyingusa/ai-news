<div class="story-lead" markdown="1">

France has attributed a cyberattack that manipulated supermarket refrigeration systems to Shahid Kaveh, an Iranian cyber unit, according to a Financial Times profile of the group. The attack, carried out one night in October, involved hackers raising the temperature inside refrigeration systems — an operation the FT describes as among the most unusual cyberattacks on record. The same unit reportedly compromised Israeli-made industrial devices in 2023 and 2024. The report does not name the retailers affected, describe how the attackers gained access, or quantify the damage, and no technical indicators or vendor advisory are cited.

</div>

<div class="fact-check" markdown="1">

## Verified and unconfirmed

- **Verified:** France's foreign ministry stated on 10 November 2026 that it attributes the destructive cyberattacks on French supermarket refrigeration systems to Shahid Kaveh, an offensive cyber unit affiliated with the Electronic Warfare and Cyber Defence Organization of the Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps, calling the actor a persistent threat to critical infrastructure; the Financial Times reports the same unit hacked Israeli-made industrial devices in 2023 and 2024.
- **Not yet confirmed:** The scale figures circulating — roughly 700 stores affected and at least 15 fires, attributed to ANSSI's chief — come from secondary reporting rather than the FT account or the attribution declaration; the 2023–2024 attacks on Israeli-made industrial equipment rest on FT reporting alone; and the timeline is inconsistent, since the FT describes "one night in October" while a Wikipedia entry dates a similar multi-country wave of refrigeration and freezer attacks to 2 November 2026.

</div>

## Background

Shahid Kaveh is an offensive cyber unit attached to the Electronic and Cyber Center of Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps, according to Check Point, which links its operations to the CyberAv3ngers persona that attacked US water utilities. France's attribution of the October refrigeration sabotage at roughly 700 retail sites follows earlier campaigns the unit reportedly ran against Israeli-made industrial devices in 2023 and 2024, and the FT reports it was also behind a wave of attacks on US industrial control devices in 2024.

## Why it matters

The reported shift from Israeli-made industrial devices in 2023–2024 to supermarket refrigeration in October suggests the unit is targeting internet-reachable operational technology rather than office IT, so operators of refrigeration, HVAC and other building-control systems should treat externally exposed controllers as a live threat surface and check them for remote access and default credentials. Because the FT account names no affected retailer, victims and their suppliers cannot yet confirm from public information whether their own equipment was involved.
