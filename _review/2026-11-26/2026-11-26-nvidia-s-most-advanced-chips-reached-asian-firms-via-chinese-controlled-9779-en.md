CBS News published an investigation by Josh Boswell reporting that Nvidia sold its most advanced chips to at least two U.S.-based companies controlled by Chinese-owned firms, and that some of those chips were then leased or sold to companies in Asia. U.S. export controls restrict advanced AI chip sales to China and depend on knowing the final user, not just the buyer's address, which makes that arrangement sensitive. The account summarized here does not identify the companies, chip models, or Asian buyers, and it does not say whether the transfers violated U.S. rules or include a response from Nvidia.

## Background

US export rules bar Nvidia from shipping its most advanced AI accelerators to China without a license, so the ownership and downstream use of the buyer — not just the shipping address — determines whether a sale is legal. AI Daily Radar's November 15 digest reported a Wall Street Journal investigation finding Nvidia chips reaching Chinese firms through large Southeast Asian data centers that exploited a gap in the controls, and the November 16 digest reported that the Commerce Department had issued an interim rule requiring licenses when AI chips may provide compute to China or other restricted countries, extending controls beyond physical shipments to remote access. Lawmakers have also been pressing Nvidia over its China business and what the company knew about possible diversion of its chips, CNBC reported.

## Why it matters

For companies that lease or resell Nvidia compute, the report makes ownership and downstream leasing the compliance facts that matter: a U.S. address does not by itself establish who the final user is.

## What to watch

Watch for Nvidia's response and for any statement from U.S. export-control regulators on whether they are examining the transfers; the CBS account summarized here reports neither.
