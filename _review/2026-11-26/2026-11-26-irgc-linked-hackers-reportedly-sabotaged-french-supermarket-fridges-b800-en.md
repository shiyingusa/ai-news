One night in October, hackers remotely raised the temperature settings in refrigeration systems at hundreds of supermarkets across France, destroying millions of euros of food. French intelligence agencies found traces of Farsi and other clues in the digital trail pointing to Shahid Kaveh, an elite hacking unit of the Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps (IRGC, Iran's ideologically loyal military force), according to the report. Shahid Kaveh, named after an Iranian commander who died in the 1980s Iran-Iraq war, was already known to Western intelligence agencies and is one of several units under the IRGC's Cyber Electronic Command. The attribution rests on reported intelligence traces rather than published primary documents, and the attackers have not been charged or formally named by the French government in the account.

</div>

## Verified and unconfirmed

- **Verified:** The French cybersecurity agency ANSSI attributed the October intrusion into supermarket refrigeration systems — which raised temperatures at hundreds of stores and destroyed millions of euros of food — to the Shahid Kaveh group, described as a cyber unit affiliated with Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps, according to reports citing the French attribution. Tehran denied the accusation, and Paris said it reserved the right to respond.
- **Not yet confirmed:** The underlying technical evidence — the Farsi-language traces and other digital clues said to have led investigators to Shahid Kaveh — has not been published, and the finding rests on the French agencies' assessment rather than independently released forensics; the exact number of stores affected, the value of the losses, and whether the group acted on IRGC orders have not been independently established. Reporting also indicates France did not attribute a later November wave of attacks elsewhere in Europe to Iran or any specific actor.

</div>

## Background

Shahid Kaveh is one of several hacking units overseen by the Revolutionary Guards' Cyber Electronic Command, and it was already known to Western intelligence agencies before this operation; the unit is named after Mahmoud Kaveh, an Iranian commander who died during the 1980s Iran-Iraq war. The October disruption was reported at the time as a refrigeration compromise that stemmed from poor security in the targeted systems.

## Why it matters

The episode shows that internet-connected refrigeration and building-management controllers are an operational target whose failure destroys inventory rather than data: operators of supermarket chains and cold-chain facilities should audit which of these devices are reachable from the public internet and whether they still use default or shared credentials, since the earlier October incident was reported to have stemmed from poor security.
