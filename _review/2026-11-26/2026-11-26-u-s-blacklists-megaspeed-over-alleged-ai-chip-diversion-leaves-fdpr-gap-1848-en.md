The U.S. Commerce Department added Megaspeed and six affiliated entities to the Entity List over concerns about diversion of advanced AI accelerators to China, subjecting their license applications to a presumption of denial. Unlike earlier listings of Chinese companies suspected of similar activity, the department did not apply a Foreign Direct Product Rule (FDPR) designation — specifically Footnote 4 — which would have extended U.S. export controls to foreign-made goods made with American technology. Nvidia and AMD's advanced accelerators remain covered by the separate Advanced Computing FDPR, whose worldwide scope the Bureau of Industry and Security retained in its Nov. 19 amendments, but the missing Footnote 4 may leave Megaspeed able to buy foreign-made lower-performance AI GPUs, networking chips, and other data-center hardware. Chris McGuire of the Council on Foreign Relations suggested the omission could be either a regulatory oversight or a deliberate decision to narrow the restrictions.

</div>

## Verified and unconfirmed

- **Verified:** The Federal Register notice cited by Tom's Hardware adds Megaspeed and six affiliates to the Entity List, with a presumption of denial and no Footnote 4 designation; BIS's Nov. 19 amendments kept the worldwide Advanced Computing FDPR.
- **Not yet confirmed:** Whether the omission was deliberate or an oversight, and whether Megaspeed can still obtain foreign-made lower-performance AI chips; the espionage allegation against former executive Alice Huang and the smuggling allegation linked to Megaspeed rest on prior reporting and unnamed intelligence claims, not on the Entity List notice.

</div>

## Background

The Entity List is the Commerce Department's roster of firms that must obtain a license — reviewed with a presumption of denial — before buying goods subject to U.S. export rules, while a "Footnote 4" designation adds the entity-specific foreign direct product rule that extends those rules to foreign-made goods produced with U.S. technology. AI Daily Radar's November 24 digest reported that Commerce added Megaspeed to the list alongside Alibaba's Singapore-based unit Apex Enterprise Solutions, and its November 15 digest covered a Wall Street Journal investigation into Nvidia chips reaching Chinese firms through Southeast Asian data centers. Advanced AI accelerators already fall under a separate worldwide foreign direct product rule (§734.9(h) of the Export Administration Regulations), which the Bureau of Industry and Security explicitly kept in its November 19 amendments.

## Why it matters

Megaspeed is effectively cut off from qualifying high-end Nvidia and AMD accelerators, but the missing Footnote 4 leaves a gap for foreign-made lower-performance AI GPUs and networking hardware; compliance teams should screen the six affiliates and check whether lower-performance foreign-made data-center hardware falls outside EAR scope.

## What to watch

Watch the next BIS Entity List update or any revision to this Federal Register notice for a Footnote 4 designation; that would show whether Commerce deliberately chose a narrower scope or is correcting an oversight.
