Sky News reported that the US Air Force's Cloud One platform — a multi-vendor environment combining Microsoft, Amazon, Oracle and Alphabet cloud services — has been hacked, with disruptions reported as its infrastructure is rebuilt. According to the report, the platform was launched in 2017 as a one-stop shop for the Air Force's IT needs and powers scores of applications, including systems that manage personnel data and pay, and logistics and maintenance.

The report does not describe the attack vector, which systems or data were affected, how many users were hit, or when the intrusion was detected, and it carries no on-record confirmation from the Air Force or the Department of Defense.

## Verified and unconfirmed

- **Verified:** Sky News, Defense News and The Guardian all reported on 27 November 2026 that hackers breached Cloud One, the Air Force platform launched in 2017 that pools Microsoft, Amazon, Oracle and Alphabet cloud services to run scores of applications, including personnel, pay, logistics and maintenance systems; Cybernews reported that the discovery paused some migrations, blocked updates and cost some users their administrator rights.
- **Not yet confirmed:** None of the reports cited a statement from the Air Force itself, and the article text was not available for this briefing, so the extent of the intrusion, which data or systems were reached, how attackers got in and when it was detected remain unconfirmed; the account of a month of disruptions and restricted access comes from a single commenter who says their work depends on Cloud One, not from an official disclosure.

</div>

## Background

Cloud One was launched in 2017 as a one-stop shop for the Air Force's IT needs, combining the cloud services of Microsoft, Amazon, Oracle and Alphabet to run scores of applications, including ones handling personnel data, pay, logistics and maintenance. The reported intrusion comes days after reports that a CIA database had been accessed, the Guardian noted. The account of the Cloud One breach rests on three people familiar with the matter speaking to Reuters, not on an on-the-record statement from the Air Force or the cloud providers.

## Why it matters

Because Cloud One hosts personnel, pay, logistics and maintenance applications, Air Force users and the contractors supporting them face restricted access and slower support while the environment is rebuilt; a commenter who says their work depends on the platform reported significant disruption over the past month and said they were taking holiday time early rather than work around the tight restrictions. Anyone with integrations into Cloud One should check whether their access has been curtailed before assuming an outage on their own side.

## What to watch

Watch for an official statement from the Air Force or the Department of Defense confirming the breach and setting out its scope, including how long the infrastructure rebuild and access limits are expected to last.
