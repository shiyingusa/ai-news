<div class="story-lead" markdown="1">

OpenAI has filed a countersuit against Apple, alleging that a staffer who left OpenAI and returned to Apple took trade secrets with them, and that Apple "coerced and threatened" suppliers that work with both companies, the Financial Times reported. The filing escalates an existing legal battle between the two companies beyond their own hiring into their shared supply chain.

The available report does not identify the former staffer, name the suppliers involved, or specify the court, filing date or relief sought, and no response from Apple was included.

</div>

<div class="fact-check" markdown="1">

## Verified and unconfirmed

- **Verified:** The Financial Times reported that OpenAI filed a countersuit accusing Apple of obtaining trade secrets through a former OpenAI staffer who returned to Apple, and of pressuring suppliers shared by the two companies; these are OpenAI's assertions in a court filing, not judicial findings.
- **Not yet confirmed:** The identity of the staffer, which suppliers were allegedly coerced, the court and filing date, the damages sought, and Apple's response are all absent from the available report, and none of the allegations has been tested in court.

</div>

## Background

Apple sued OpenAI and two former employees in July, alleging they misappropriated Apple trade secrets to advance the ChatGPT maker's push into consumer hardware; OpenAI denied the claims in August and characterised the suit as an attempt to stop it hiring Apple staff. OpenAI's counterclaim adds a new dimension to that dispute: it alleges Apple encouraged its own employees to store corporate files on personal devices and cloud accounts, and that Apple pressured suppliers that also work with OpenAI on its planned devices.

## Why it matters

The countersuit pulls companies that supply both Apple and OpenAI into the dispute rather than leaving it a fight over hiring, which means those suppliers may need to check their confidentiality and exclusivity commitments to each customer against claims that one side improperly pressured them.
