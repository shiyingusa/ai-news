# Evaluation goal

Evaluate the importance of timely news about spaceflight and space technology.

# Scoring rubric

- **9-10: Groundbreaking.** Historic launches or landings, first-of-their-kind missions, major mission failures, or policy decisions that reshape the space industry.
- **7-8: High value.** Notable test flights, satellite constellation milestones, significant contracts or funding, and important mission results.
- **5-6: Interesting.** Incremental updates, useful products, moderate interest, or developments worth knowing but not urgent.
- **3-4: Low priority.** Routine updates, rehashed coverage of older news, shallow takes, rumours without sourcing, or content dominated by promotion.
- **0-2: Off-topic or noise.** Items whose primary subject is not spaceflight or space technology, spam, or purely promotional content.

# Evaluation guidance

The primary subject must be spaceflight or space technology; other technology news scores 0-2 here. Routine launches of the same rocket score 5-6 unless something new happens. Consider novelty, likely real-world impact, source quality, and concrete supporting details. Prefer primary sources (official announcements, filings, papers, regulator documents, release notes) over re-reporting of the same event. Distinguish what has shipped or been measured from what is announced, rumoured or forecast. Treat substantive community debate as additional evidence of value, but do not equate popularity with importance. Do not reward hype, stock-price chatter or exaggerated headlines.

Use three to five specific topic tags.
